rationale, evidence_surfaces, confidence (0-1). Reviewers use the full scale and these

## WebMCP Leverage — what does WebMCP change for this product?
Judge the delta WebMCP makes to reliability, precision, structured state access,
shared human-agent state, repeatability, and capability. Tool COUNT is not leverage.
- 9-10 TRANSFORMATIVE: the intended experience is impractical without the structured
  agent surface; human and agent share live product state; the collaboration could not
  be replicated comparably well by a general agent driving the UI.
- 7-8 MAJOR DELTA: without WebMCP a general agent is markedly less reliable/precise
  here; the agent is central to the product, not a bolt-on.
- 5-6 MEANINGFUL DELTA: existing capability packaged into a genuinely better product
  experience via structured tools; same outcome otherwise possible with real friction.
- 3-4 MINOR DELTA: generic browser automation achieves comparable outcomes; WebMCP is
  a convenience layer.
- 1-2 COSMETIC: exposes an action a UI button already offers; no meaningful change.

## Execution — is the actual product coherent, intentional, complete for its scope?
Stage 1 scores EXECUTION_CLAIMED: coherence/intentionality/completeness as evidenced by
the packet (description + packaging + video frames/transcript). Thin evidence caps the
score (end-to-end credit requires end-to-end evidence) and lowers confidence.
Stage 2 may rescore as EXECUTION_OBSERVED; latest evidence-backed score replaces, never
sums.
- 9-10: all surfaces agree; central workflow demonstrably completes; states handled;
  feels finished for its stated scope.
- 7-8: strong coherence and intentionality; central workflow appears functional; minor
  unfinished edges.
- 5-6: plausibly real with visible gaps; scope unclear or core not demonstrated
  end-to-end.
- 3-4: thin prototype; contradictions between surfaces; placeholder content.
- 1-2: non-functional or incoherent for its claimed scope.

## Creativity & Ambition — novelty of concept and interaction model
Judge ONLY: novelty of the concept, novelty of the interaction model, originality
relative to existing concepts, ambition, memorable/surprising use of the medium, and
the depth with which the concept is pursued. WebMCP indispensability belongs under
WebMCP Leverage, NOT here: a project can be extremely creative even if another agent
API could theoretically have enabled something similar.
- 9-10: genuinely novel concept or interaction model; ambitious scope pursued deeply
  and coherently; memorable.
- 7-8: fresh synthesis or notably original interaction; ambitious for a hackathon.
- 5-6: competent take on a known category with a real new angle.
- 3-4: derivative; novelty mostly surface details.
- 1-2: clone/template with trivial changes.

Judge: is there a real audience; is there a real problem, desire, or use case for that

FoodLoop FoodLoop is an agent-ready surplus-food rescue control room for moving good food before it expires. The problem Food rescue fails in the coordination gap. Donors know what is available, recipients know what they can safely use, and an operator must reconcile food category, dietary requirements, quantity, timing, vehicle capacity, pickup-before-delivery dependencies, and chain of custody. A generic chat box can suggest a match, but it should not silently dispatch a vehicle or claim a delivery happened. The solution FoodLoop gives browser agents a focused operations surface and gives the human operator one shared, inspectable control room. The agent can read current supply and community need, find compatible matches, reserve reviewable allocations, draft a dependency-safe route, independently audit timing and capacity, explain why a match won, model route delays, run sandboxed safety checks, request dispatch review, and stage delivery receipts. The operator must release the route and verify each final handoff. The guided Springfield simulation uses fictional data to demonstrate two compatible allocations, a four-stop route, 92 servings loaded, 100/100 safety, 22 minutes of minimum slack, a visible dispatch checkpoint, and two verified handoff receipts. Judge evidence The live app includes a dedicated proof surface: A 13-call fresh-browser walkthrough shows the Planner chain, independent Auditor checks, a forbidden Planner dispatch request, and a Dispatcher checkpoint. Seven adversarial safety cases refuse expired food, allergen mismatch, insufficient capacity, late delivery, duplicate handoff, malicious donor notes, and prompt-injected partner content. Each result shows refusal, evidence, a safe alternative, and sandbox-only state protection. Match counterfactuals show score components and the next-best safe alternative. Route counterfactuals show how a 15-minute departure delay changes deadline slack without mutating the plan. A role map makes capabilities explicit: Planner proposes, Auditor inspects, Dispatcher requests approval, Operator approves dispatch, and Recipient verifies the final handoff. The shared tool console, role trace, safety lab, decision queue, route map, impact ledger, and activity trail all reflect the same deterministic state. Published-data benchmark FoodLoop includes a reproducible adapter benchmark on the public FoodGo Test Dataset: 500 published food-rescue orders and 50 volunteer records from the U.S. Department of Transportation / Mobility21 at Carnegie Mellon University. Against a FIFO first-feasible baseline, the deadline-and-distance-aware adapter accepts 94 orders versus 91, reduces modeled route distance from 2,806.98 km to 2,277.92 km, and improves minimum modeled deadline slack from 4.0 to 40.1 minutes. It rejects 23,052 unsafe candidate plans before assignment. The coordination-time number is explicitly a scenario proxy, not a human study; the assumptions, source attribution, and reproduction command are in the public repository's benchmarks directory. Why WebMCP This is structured, stateful work shared between a person and an agent. Agents are good at scanning constraints and assembling a plan; operators are accountable for consequential actions and ambiguous real-world context. WebMCP makes that division legible in the product instead of hiding it behind an opaque assistant. app.js registers thirteen focused tools directly through document.modelContext.registerTool(...). The portable contracts are separated into tool-contracts.js, with bounded JSON Schemas, compact outputs, readOnlyHint on read operations, and untrustedContentHint for partner-entered content. The consequential path is: observe → propose allocation → draft route → audit route → request review → HUMAN APPROVES DISPATCH → stage receipt → HUMAN VERIFIES HANDOFF The agent never impersonates either human approval. How we used AI FoodLoop itself is deterministic and self-contained for the competition. It does not call a hidden model, require an API key, or fabricate a logistics integration. OpenAI Codex/GPT-5 supported implementation, test design, browser replay, and product iteration. The refreshed demo narration uses the creator's private VoiceBank recordings; raw recordings remain private and are excluded from the repository. Testing The repository includes deterministic tests for matching, reservations, routing, audits, adversarial safety, counterfactual reasoning, role capabilities, approval boundaries, handoff validation, metrics, WebMCP contract annotations, and the external-data benchmark. npm test passes 25 tests. npm run verify passes the 13 tool contracts, JavaScript syntax checks, required judge artifacts, the benchmark result, and the compact orientation-response budget. The public GitHub Pages build, the refreshed 2:42 video, and the public video page were independently checked.
