Accomplishments that we're proud of WebMCP is the primary interaction mechanism, not a decorative contest feature. The complete capture, retrieve, propose, approve, direct human-triage, and decision-log loop works on the public deployment. The application preserves an explicit boundary between agent contribution and human authority. The public demo deliberately omits deletion from its UI and WebMCP: without authentication or record ownership, either would allow any visitor to erase shared data. A secret-protected operator command provides exact-ID and QA-prefix cleanup outside the public collaboration surface. The product has a clear real-world audience and workflow despite the deliberately playful squirrel metaphor. We resisted building authentication, embeddings, semantic search, notifications, teams, integrations, research analytics, or a rodent economy. What we learned Formal prompts are useful when the target is known. Conversation is often where the target is discovered. Squirrel Board grew out of noticing that collaborative conversations produce valuable side ideas through micro-feedback, correction, connection, and synthesis. Those ideas need somewhere to go that is durable but does not imply immediate commitment. We also learned that a good human-agent product can be defined as much by authority boundaries as by capabilities. The question is not merely “What can the agent do?” It is “What should remain a proposal until the human decides?” Hands-on testing also exposed an alternate-write-path defect: the human forms enforced text limits while the initial agent routes did not. Harmonizing server-side validation across both paths reinforced a practical lesson from the product itself: a constraint in one interface is not yet a system constraint. What's next for Squirrel Board Squirrel Board is a focused MVP for a broader class of lightweight tools designed around deliberate human-AI collaboration, from idea capture through planning and execution. Possible next directions include private workspaces, search and filtering, richer relationships between ideas, exports, and research instrumentation for studying how ideas emerge and evolve during sustained collaboration. Those are intentionally not part of the hackathon build. First, we capture the squirrels. Then we decide which ones deserve projects. Switch to Viewing as Alex and it is all there: the alert at the top of the feed, the task in her list. She asks "did Mom take her morning pills?" and gets an answer from the live plan. \n\n No forms. No phone tree. Mom just said how she felt. \n\n Why this is a strong fit for WebMCP \n\n Caregiving is coordination: a dozen small facts scattered across text threads, pill bottles and fridge notes. An agent can only help if it can act on the real plan, not a screenshot of it, and not a copy that drifts. Hearthkeeper exposes the plan as twelve structured tools registered with document.modelContext.registerTool : \n\n get_care_plan , log_symptom , check_interactions , add_medication , update_medication , mark_dose_taken , add_appointment , reschedule_appointment , cancel_appointment , assign_task , complete_task , notify_family \n\n Any WebMCP-capable agent, whether ChatGPT's in-app browser, Chrome with WebMCP enabled, or the built-in Gemini care agent, reads and updates exactly what the family sees. Tool descriptions carry the guardrails (the agent may move a dose time but never change a dose; dose changes come from the prescriber), and every write posts to the shared feed so nothing an agent does is invisible. \n\n How it creates a better experience \n\n \n For the parent: talk, don't navigate. Voice input, plain-language replies, and the agent takes the follow-up steps instead of handing back a to-do list. \n For the family: one plan instead of a group chat. Every agent action lands in the feed with who it was sent to. Tasks are concrete and assigned. Perspective switching shows the same plan from each person's side. \n For safety: the interaction check is a read-only tool with a small reference table, and the prompt escalates red-flag symptoms to emergency services rather than trying to be a doctor. \n \n\n What people and agents can do together that was hard before \n\n Before: Mom mentions dizziness on the phone, Alex googles it at work, Sam doesn't hear about it until Sunday, nobody calls the pharmacist. Now: Mom says it once, the agent does the coordination in seconds, and the family corrects or extends it on the same board. The human keeps judgment and relationships; the agent keeps the plan in sync. \n\n How WebMCP is implemented \n\n \n lib/webmcp.ts : tools are registered with document.modelContext.registerTool(..., { signal }) and unregistered via AbortController on unmount.
