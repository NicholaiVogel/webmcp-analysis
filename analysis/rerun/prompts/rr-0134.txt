# FROZEN SCORING RUBRIC (pre-registered before any Stage 1 review)

Scores are integers 1-10 on each of the FOUR official criteria. Every score carries:
rationale, evidence_surfaces, confidence (0-1). Reviewers use the full scale and these
band anchors verbatim. Category neutrality is absolute: a game, art piece, music tool,
CRM, weird toy, and developer tool are all eligible for 10/10 on every criterion if they
satisfy that criterion exceptionally well FOR WHAT THEY ARE TRYING TO BE.
No aesthetic penalties. Signs of hurried AI work matter only as actual product-quality
problems (incoherence, broken flow, placeholder content), never as style.

## WebMCP Leverage — what does WebMCP change for this product?
Judge the delta WebMCP makes to reliability, precision, structured state access,
shared human-agent state, repeatability, and capability. Tool COUNT is not leverage.
- 9-10 TRANSFORMATIVE: the intended experience is impractical without the structured
  agent surface; human and agent share live product state; the collaboration could not
  be replicated comparably well by a general agent driving the UI.
- 7-8 MAJOR DELTA: without WebMCP a general agent is markedly less reliable/precise
  here; the agent is central to the product, not a bolt-on.
- 5-6 MEANINGFUL DELTA: existing capability packaged into a genuinely better product
  experience via structured tools; same outcome otherwise possible with real friction.
- 3-4 MINOR DELTA: generic browser automation achieves comparable outcomes; WebMCP is
  a convenience layer.
- 1-2 COSMETIC: exposes an action a UI button already offers; no meaningful change.

## Execution — is the actual product coherent, intentional, complete for its scope?
Stage 1 scores EXECUTION_CLAIMED: coherence/intentionality/completeness as evidenced by
the packet (description + packaging + video frames/transcript). Thin evidence caps the
score (end-to-end credit requires end-to-end evidence) and lowers confidence.
Stage 2 may rescore as EXECUTION_OBSERVED; latest evidence-backed score replaces, never
sums.
- 9-10: all surfaces agree; central workflow demonstrably completes; states handled;
  feels finished for its stated scope.
- 7-8: strong coherence and intentionality; central workflow appears functional; minor
  unfinished edges.
- 5-6: plausibly real with visible gaps; scope unclear or core not demonstrated
  end-to-end.
- 3-4: thin prototype; contradictions between surfaces; placeholder content.
- 1-2: non-functional or incoherent for its claimed scope.

## Creativity & Ambition — novelty of concept and interaction model
Judge ONLY: novelty of the concept, novelty of the interaction model, originality
relative to existing concepts, ambition, memorable/surprising use of the medium, and
the depth with which the concept is pursued. WebMCP indispensability belongs under
WebMCP Leverage, NOT here: a project can be extremely creative even if another agent
API could theoretically have enabled something similar.
- 9-10: genuinely novel concept or interaction model; ambitious scope pursued deeply
  and coherently; memorable.
- 7-8: fresh synthesis or notably original interaction; ambitious for a hackathon.
- 5-6: competent take on a known category with a real new angle.
- 3-4: derivative; novelty mostly surface details.
- 1-2: clone/template with trivial changes.
"That the category exists" is never a penalty; judge the submitted concept.

## Potential Impact — credible value for the intended audience
Judge: is there a real audience; is there a real problem, desire, or use case for that
audience; does the demonstrated solution genuinely address it. NO commercial adoption,
monetization, market pull, or startup viability is required. Entertainment, play, art,
creative expression, learning, niche hobby use, experimentation, and community value
are legitimate forms of impact. A tiny audience fully justifies a top score.
- 9-10: specific real audience with a real problem/desire; solution demonstrably
  addresses it; credible value, appeal, usefulness, or meaning for that audience.
- 7-8: clear audience and problem, good fit, credible value.
- 5-6: identifiable audience, looser fit or partial address.
- 3-4: speculative audience or strained fit; impact mostly aspirational.
- 1-2: no coherent audience/problem pairing.
Audience size is NOT a multiplier.

## Aggregate & ranking
aggregate = leverage + execution + impact + creativity (4-40). Nothing else enters.
Tie-breaks: Leverage, then Execution, then Impact, then Creativity, then higher
combined evidence confidence, then slug alphabetical. See PROTOCOL.md Ranking section.

WebMCP turns a registration photo into Recovery DNA, helping people and agents identify a changed bicycle and prepare a safe, human-reviewed recovery report. Like 1 Comment Story Updates Inspiration Itta is an existing QR-based recovery service for bicycles and personal mobility devices. While working on its registration flow, I noticed two connected problems. First, registration is harder than it looks. Owners of older bicycles often cannot remember the exact manufacturer, model, official color, or size. Second, even a correctly identified model is not enough for recovery. Replaceable parts can change, logos can be covered, and two bicycles from the same product line can look almost identical. That led to Recovery DNA: a visual passport made from the small details that belong to one specific bicycle. What it does The experience begins with registration. The owner selects a vehicle type and adds a representative photo. A browser agent examines the photo already visible on the page and uses WebMCP tools to present likely manufacturer, model, color, and size candidates. The suggestions are never applied automatically. The owner reviews the evidence, chooses the best candidate, edits anything necessary, and remains responsible for the final registration. The agent can then propose a Recovery DNA made from distributed visual anchors: decal placement, frame geometry, distinctive marks, damage, accessories, and personal signs of wear. It also distinguishes durable evidence from parts that can easily be replaced. The owner reviews and confirms these anchors before they can be reused. Later, when a bicycle is found, the agent compares the new photo with the owner-confirmed Recovery DNA. It explains which details remain, which parts changed, what is missing, and how likely it is to be the same bicycle. Finally, it prepares a recovery report for human review. Nothing is sent automatically, and the result is presented as a visual probability assessment—not forensic proof of ownership. Why WebMCP This workflow needs more than a chatbot response. The agent must understand the page’s current state, inspect the photo already shared with the user, request additional evidence when necessary, and return structured results to the correct interface. WebMCP provides that bridge between the agent’s reasoning and Itta’s product UI. The tools do not receive raw image files, private owner data, contact information, or location. The agent visually inspects what is already rendered in the shared browser, while WebMCP carries only limited context and validated structured results. This creates a continuous human-agent journey across registration, identification, comparison, and recovery—while keeping every consequential decision with a person. How I built it The demo is built with React, TypeScript, and Vite and deployed on Vercel. I implemented eight WebMCP tools using document.modelContext.registerTool() : Registration tools read the current draft, request focused evidence, and render up to three specification candidates. Recovery DNA tools expose limited owner or finder context, render visual anchors, display an identity comparison, and prepare a recovery-report draft. Tools are registered only after the user explicitly opens the relevant assistant. They are removed with an AbortController when the scene changes, the component unmounts, or the photo is replaced. This prevents stale tools from modifying the wrong stage of the experience. The public challenge route is isolated from Itta’s production authentication, database, analytics, and reporting systems. All demo state stays in browser memory. Challenges The biggest design challenge was making WebMCP essential rather than decorative. Simple specification autofill was useful, but it did not fully demonstrate what becomes possible when a person and an agent share the same web experience. Recovery DNA made the collaboration more meaningful: the agent contributes visual reasoning across two moments in time, while the product contributes structured state, validation, lifecycle control, and human confirmation. Another challenge was judge accessibility. A reviewer may not have a bicycle photo available, so the demo includes a fictional before-and-after sample. The bicycle changes its saddle, front wheel, and visible branding to demonstrate why a model name or basic photo match is insufficient. Unsupported browsers also receive a clearly labeled simulation preview. It demonstrates the interface without pretending that a WebMCP call occurred. What I learned WebMCP is most valuable as a trusted interaction boundary—not as a replacement for the interface or the user. Agents are good at interpreting ambiguous evidence. Websites are good at holding state, validating structured actions, protecting private data, and asking for confirmation. Combining those strengths creates experiences that would be difficult to achieve with either side alone. What's next The next step is to connect owner-confirmed Recovery DNA to Itta’s real registration and QR recovery flows. The same approach could later support scooters, pets, luggage, and other registered objects whose identity depends on more than a serial number or product name. Like 2 Comment Story Updates Inspiration AI agents can already click buttons and change data. What bothered me was the step before that: how can I review the outcome before it becomes real? In software, I would create a branch, inspect the diff, and merge it after review. SHADOW brings that workflow to AI actions in the browser. What it does SHADOW gives an agent an isolated copy of the current state. The agent can change plans, reduce seats, or cancel subscriptions without touching Reality. In the demo, the fictional company ORBIT spends $184,300 per month on software. The agent reads usage, contracts, and dependencies, then builds different cost-saving proposals. Every change includes Proof showing the data behind it, its impact, and any blockers. Reality → Shadow → Proof → Human commit Only the user can commit a Shadow. The agent has no commit tool. How I built it I built SHADOW with Next.js, React, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS, Zod, and WebMCP . A deterministic projection engine calculates each future without changing the original state. I exposed 13 WebMCP tools for reading data, staging changes, creating branches, and comparing outcomes. The UI and the tools use the same application logic, so they cannot produce different results. The MVP runs locally in the browser with synthetic data and needs no accounts or API keys. Challenges I ran into The hardest part was making the separation between Shadow and Reality real, not just visual. I had to ensure that no agent-accessible path could commit a change. Unsafe proposals were another challenge. I did not want to hide them because they often contain useful information. Cancelling Miro, for example, reveals a $25,000 penalty . SHADOW keeps that proposal visible but blocks it from being committed. I also had to make projections reproducible and mark old Shadows as stale whenever Reality changes. Accomplishments that I’m proud of I am proud of what SHADOW deliberately cannot do. Its 13 tools let an agent inspect, stage, branch, and compare, but none can alter Reality. The full flow works end to end: an agent creates two alternatives, the user combines the best changes into a Hybrid, and a human commit produces a new Reality version and receipt. The Hybrid reduces monthly spending from $184,300 to $147,385 . What I learned I learned that human oversight works best when the system enforces it. A prompt alone is not a safety boundary. I also learned that useful Proof is not hidden model reasoning. It is the visible data, impact, and checks that let someone make an informed decision. What's next for SHADOW Next, I want to connect SHADOW to real SaaS tools and add shared workspaces, authentication, approval roles, and tamper-evident receipts. The larger goal is simple: version control for AI actions on the web . What it does ThreatWeaver is a security-by-design architecture canvas for cloud systems. The page loads an insecure 3-tier starter: Internet → Web Server → API Gateway → Backend → Database + Object Storage. The human can drag components onto the canvas, toggle public / encrypted / rate-limit flags, or ask the agent to add and connect nodes. The agent uses eight WebMCP site tools on the open page.
