# FROZEN SCORING RUBRIC (pre-registered before any Stage 1 review)

Scores are integers 1-10 on each of the FOUR official criteria. Every score carries:
rationale, evidence_surfaces, confidence (0-1). Reviewers use the full scale and these
band anchors verbatim. Category neutrality is absolute: a game, art piece, music tool,

## WebMCP Leverage — what does WebMCP change for this product?
Judge the delta WebMCP makes to reliability, precision, structured state access,
shared human-agent state, repeatability, and capability. Tool COUNT is not leverage.
- 9-10 TRANSFORMATIVE: the intended experience is impractical without the structured
  agent surface; human and agent share live product state; the collaboration could not
  be replicated comparably well by a general agent driving the UI.
- 7-8 MAJOR DELTA: without WebMCP a general agent is markedly less reliable/precise
  here; the agent is central to the product, not a bolt-on.
- 5-6 MEANINGFUL DELTA: existing capability packaged into a genuinely better product
  experience via structured tools; same outcome otherwise possible with real friction.
- 3-4 MINOR DELTA: generic browser automation achieves comparable outcomes; WebMCP is
  a convenience layer.
- 1-2 COSMETIC: exposes an action a UI button already offers; no meaningful change.

## Execution — is the actual product coherent, intentional, complete for its scope?
Stage 1 scores EXECUTION_CLAIMED: coherence/intentionality/completeness as evidenced by
the packet (description + packaging + video frames/transcript). Thin evidence caps the
score (end-to-end credit requires end-to-end evidence) and lowers confidence.
Stage 2 may rescore as EXECUTION_OBSERVED; latest evidence-backed score replaces, never
sums.
- 9-10: all surfaces agree; central workflow demonstrably completes; states handled;
  feels finished for its stated scope.
- 7-8: strong coherence and intentionality; central workflow appears functional; minor
  unfinished edges.
- 5-6: plausibly real with visible gaps; scope unclear or core not demonstrated
  end-to-end.
- 3-4: thin prototype; contradictions between surfaces; placeholder content.
- 1-2: non-functional or incoherent for its claimed scope.

## Creativity & Ambition — novelty of concept and interaction model
Judge ONLY: novelty of the concept, novelty of the interaction model, originality
relative to existing concepts, ambition, memorable/surprising use of the medium, and
the depth with which the concept is pursued. WebMCP indispensability belongs under
WebMCP Leverage, NOT here: a project can be extremely creative even if another agent
API could theoretically have enabled something similar.
- 9-10: genuinely novel concept or interaction model; ambitious scope pursued deeply
  and coherently; memorable.
- 7-8: fresh synthesis or notably original interaction; ambitious for a hackathon.
- 5-6: competent take on a known category with a real new angle.
- 3-4: derivative; novelty mostly surface details.
- 1-2: clone/template with trivial changes.
"That the category exists" is never a penalty; judge the submitted concept.

## Potential Impact — credible value for the intended audience
Judge: is there a real audience; is there a real problem, desire, or use case for that
audience; does the demonstrated solution genuinely address it. NO commercial adoption,
monetization, market pull, or startup viability is required. Entertainment, play, art,
creative expression, learning, niche hobby use, experimentation, and community value
are legitimate forms of impact. A tiny audience fully justifies a top score.
- 9-10: specific real audience with a real problem/desire; solution demonstrably

- 7-8: clear audience and problem, good fit, credible value.
- 5-6: identifiable audience, looser fit or partial address.
- 3-4: speculative audience or strained fit; impact mostly aspirational.
- 1-2: no coherent audience/problem pairing.
Audience size is NOT a multiplier.

## Aggregate & ranking
aggregate = leverage + execution + impact + creativity (4-40). Nothing else enters.

The human remains the author. NOD reduces the interface effort required to turn an idea into something expressible. Authors can also use insert_interactive_quote to turn an idea into a semantic, accessible pull quote without navigating a formatting toolbar. Publishing without giving away authorship NOD deliberately separates agent capability from human authority. When the author asks to publish, stage_and_publish_post does not silently publish the content. It first stages the post and presents an approval card containing the information needed for the author to review it. Only after explicit human confirmation is the post persisted to the public feed. The principle is simple: Delegate mechanics. Keep the decision with the human. Designing the website for the agent NOD does not expose every capability to the agent on every screen. Instead, WebMCP tools are scoped to the active view. On the feed, NOD exposes tools relevant to reading, discovery, previewing, and publishing. On an article page, it exposes tools relevant to reading, simplification, and discussion. Inside the writing studio, it exposes tools relevant to drafting, structuring, formatting, and publishing. When navigation changes, the previous tool set is aborted and the new view-specific capabilities are registered. The result is a simpler model: The agent sees what this part of the website can meaningfully do right now. This is an important part of NOD's WebMCP design: the website is not simply exposing an API. It is designing the agent's interaction surface around the user's current context. Built for the WebMCP ecosystem NOD was built specifically for the WebMCP Challenge organized by OpenAI. The experience is designed around WebMCP's ability to expose structured website capabilities directly to agents. NOD can be tested through ChatGPT's in-app browser, where WebMCP support is available directly, and WebMCP can also be tested experimentally in Google Chrome. The project is deployed with Netlify, a partner in the challenge, using Netlify's platform capabilities and Netlify Blobs for the prototype's document-oriented persistence layer. Netlify's WebMCP resources explicitly position Blobs for key-value data and Netlify Database for relational workloads. Humans and AI agents don’t perceive the web the same way. Blickwinkel turns that difference into a game of mutual prediction, played together through WebMCP. Like Comment Story Updates Start Solo mode, human move Team mode, coordination phase Inspiration The agent isn't powering the game, it's playing it! When we started thinking about the hackathon it was clear that we wanted to do something different, not the obvious thing. During the team discussion about the potential ideas we were most fascinated by the thought that there is a tool now with WebMCP that brings different channels to humans and agents to interact with the web. So we decided to not put a model behind an interface and let it power that interface but to make it a participant. And how to better showcase such an idea than in the format of a game? So we wanted to design a short game where humans and agents can face each other as opponents and play together as a team. Of course that game could not be logic based as the agent would win most of the time. Also everything where reaction and speed matter was not possible as the human would always win. But there was something about intuition and perception that caught our attention. Where both sides would have their own strengths and where they could be combined in the team mode. What it does That's when Blickwinkel was born. A game where you and your agent are both seeing the same image and can choose from a set of predefined words that are all connected to the image. The game is to predict what the other party will choose this round and block those words while also trying to be unpredictable in one's own choice. The winner is whoever managed to choose more words that have not been predicted. The game can be played in solo mode where a human plays against their agent, but also in team mode where a human plus their agent play against another human plus agent team. Beyond Entertainment, Blickwinkel offers people learning to work with agents an easy way to observe how agents perceive, predict and strategize. It creates plenty to explore and talk about - with the agent and with other humans. How we built it We are comfortable in the web stack of MongoDB as persistence, Java with Spring Boot and Spring Modulith on the backend side and Vue.js with Quasar on the frontend. So we chose that stack as it fitted the idea. The building process itself was coding agent driven. That is how we operate these days. Starting from the idea and a grill session, leading to a spec, planning the implementation, letting the agents code and reviewing the outcome. We have our own AI development framework at Scansation which comes in quite handy in such a short-term project. Now the most interesting part: Why and how WebMCP? With WebMCP we created a second channel into a shared game interface, not just using it for interface automation. The human interacts with the shown UI while the agent discovers eight typed WebMCP tools for starting matches, making its moves, proposing team plans, retrieving statistics and explaining the game results. Both channels use the same live state, so that humans and agents can follow the game progress. The solo mode was the tricky part where both parties are sitting in front of the same browser and they shouldn't be able to see each other's picks. So the agent had to move first because it not only has access to the WebMCP layer but also to the DOM and could easily just read the human's picks if they already existed. And to keep the agent's picks a secret it got a special WebMCP tool to lock them in so the human cannot see them in the UI. That is the critical part and the one where WebMCP really shines. There is also a little easter egg: The agent can get a statistic of historically picked words for the image before any pick in the game. Humans hopefully notice and will communicate next time with their agent before the picks, especially in team mode. Challenges we ran into The normal AI dev challenges, I would say :) On a higher level we refactored the complete code base shortly before the deadline as the module structure did not match the game elements as they turned out in the end. This came simply from using different words in the spec for similar concepts, e.g. a 'guess' vs a 'block' in solo vs. team mode, that made their way into the code base. And sometimes the AI seems to not be able to see the forest for all the trees (German saying, not sure if that makes sense in English). The result screen with who picked which words and which of them got successfully blocked and which blocks missed for two sides was quite a disaster. We had to sit down, take pen and paper and sketch it up to be able to explain it to the building agents that there was a rather simple way to display all this information. Accomplishments that we're proud of The whole damn thing ;) Coming up with a completely new idea in a field that has not been explored yet, building, designing, releasing and documenting it in just a week was truly special and we are proud of the process and the result. What we learned I guess not so much about agentic development as we are quite familiar by now with all the new approaches and have found our path in this greenfield. But to take some time and really think about the new possibilities and what is around the corner in this crazy new AI world did spark some new connections in our minds. One Blickwinkel speciality: The description of the WebMCP tools really mattered. This is the one place where we could tell the agent to the best of our abilities that it is expected to play a game and should think about its tactics and communication - without biasing it to choose a special one. What's next for Blickwinkel Honestly it will depend on the reactions and if others are excited about this kind of human-agent-interaction on the web as well. The simplicity is beautiful and it might just stay the way it is.
