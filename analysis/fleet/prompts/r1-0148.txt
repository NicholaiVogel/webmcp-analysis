# FROZEN SCORING RUBRIC (pre-registered before any Stage 1 review)

Scores are integers 1-10 on each of the FOUR official criteria. Every score carries:
rationale, evidence_surfaces, confidence (0-1). Reviewers use the full scale and these
band anchors verbatim. Category neutrality is absolute: a game, art piece, music tool,
CRM, weird toy, and developer tool are all eligible for 10/10 on every criterion if they
satisfy that criterion exceptionally well FOR WHAT THEY ARE TRYING TO BE.
No aesthetic penalties. Signs of hurried AI work matter only as actual product-quality
problems (incoherence, broken flow, placeholder content), never as style.

## WebMCP Leverage — what does WebMCP change for this product?
Judge the delta WebMCP makes to reliability, precision, structured state access,
shared human-agent state, repeatability, and capability. Tool COUNT is not leverage.
- 9-10 TRANSFORMATIVE: the intended experience is impractical without the structured
  agent surface; human and agent share live product state; the collaboration could not
  be replicated comparably well by a general agent driving the UI.
- 7-8 MAJOR DELTA: without WebMCP a general agent is markedly less reliable/precise
  here; the agent is central to the product, not a bolt-on.
- 5-6 MEANINGFUL DELTA: existing capability packaged into a genuinely better product
  experience via structured tools; same outcome otherwise possible with real friction.
- 3-4 MINOR DELTA: generic browser automation achieves comparable outcomes; WebMCP is
  a convenience layer.
- 1-2 COSMETIC: exposes an action a UI button already offers; no meaningful change.

## Execution — is the actual product coherent, intentional, complete for its scope?
Stage 1 scores EXECUTION_CLAIMED: coherence/intentionality/completeness as evidenced by
the packet (description + packaging + video frames/transcript). Thin evidence caps the
score (end-to-end credit requires end-to-end evidence) and lowers confidence.
Stage 2 may rescore as EXECUTION_OBSERVED; latest evidence-backed score replaces, never
sums.
- 9-10: all surfaces agree; central workflow demonstrably completes; states handled;
  feels finished for its stated scope.
- 7-8: strong coherence and intentionality; central workflow appears functional; minor
  unfinished edges.
- 5-6: plausibly real with visible gaps; scope unclear or core not demonstrated
  end-to-end.
- 3-4: thin prototype; contradictions between surfaces; placeholder content.
- 1-2: non-functional or incoherent for its claimed scope.

## Creativity & Ambition — novelty of concept and interaction model
Judge ONLY: novelty of the concept, novelty of the interaction model, originality
relative to existing concepts, ambition, memorable/surprising use of the medium, and
the depth with which the concept is pursued. WebMCP indispensability belongs under
WebMCP Leverage, NOT here: a project can be extremely creative even if another agent
API could theoretically have enabled something similar.
- 9-10: genuinely novel concept or interaction model; ambitious scope pursued deeply
  and coherently; memorable.
- 7-8: fresh synthesis or notably original interaction; ambitious for a hackathon.
- 5-6: competent take on a known category with a real new angle.
- 3-4: derivative; novelty mostly surface details.
- 1-2: clone/template with trivial changes.
"That the category exists" is never a penalty; judge the submitted concept.

## Potential Impact — credible value for the intended audience
Judge: is there a real audience; is there a real problem, desire, or use case for that
audience; does the demonstrated solution genuinely address it. NO commercial adoption,
monetization, market pull, or startup viability is required. Entertainment, play, art,
creative expression, learning, niche hobby use, experimentation, and community value
are legitimate forms of impact. A tiny audience fully justifies a top score.
- 9-10: specific real audience with a real problem/desire; solution demonstrably
  addresses it; credible value, appeal, usefulness, or meaning for that audience.
- 7-8: clear audience and problem, good fit, credible value.
- 5-6: identifiable audience, looser fit or partial address.
- 3-4: speculative audience or strained fit; impact mostly aspirational.
- 1-2: no coherent audience/problem pairing.
Audience size is NOT a multiplier.

## Aggregate & ranking
aggregate = leverage + execution + impact + creativity (4-40). Nothing else enters.
Tie-breaks: Leverage, then Execution, then Impact, then Creativity, then higher
combined evidence confidence, then slug alphabetical. See PROTOCOL.md Ranking section.

Tool schemas describe supported component kinds, strict inputs, revision requirements, and human-owned boundaries. Better human-agent experience The agent handles repetitive construction, validation, simulation, and diagnostic reasoning. The person supplies the evidence that a software agent should not invent and controls the consequential repair decision. ProbePilot enforces those distinctions in application code: WebMCP can request a measurement but cannot take one. The agent cannot create the bench or reveal its private fault. Hypotheses cite completed human measurements. A repair needs at least two measurements. The agent stages a repair; the person applies it. Community events are an important for social causes and supporting the local community. Benchkeeper uses WebMCP to help organizers plan these events and protect key bookings, and reveal trade-offs. Like Comment Story Updates Many communities have these local events to support different causes to raise funds or just do stuff in the community like repairing community parks to clean up from emergencies and more. These community events require different skills for example a repair event, a clean up event etc. These different community events require different skills from different people. However it's really hard to plan these events particularly if one person doesn't show up. Rebuilding the schedule means deciding which promises can still be kept. I built "Benchkeeper" to help these community organizers work through their decisions with a browser agent. WebMCP lets the agent work with explicit scheduling constraints and shares a proposal directly for the organizer. What Benchkeeper is a sort of planning desk for small community events. Organizers can add repair requests, manage volunteer availability, reserve break or walk-in time, and protect promised appointments. Through ten browser-native WebMCP tools, an agent can inspect the event, explore cancellations, propose a revised schedule, and explain why an item cannot fit. The organizer can compare the current and proposed plans, see who moves or loses a slot, and review the tradeoffs before applying anything. The tools cannot apply a plan or release a protected appointment. Those actions remain in the organizer’s interface. Benchkeeper also supports spreadsheet intake, undo and redo, JSON backups, and printable schedules. It sends no messages to visitors, and manual planning works without WebMCP. How I built this. We built the interface with React, TypeScript, and Vite, using Zod to validate tool inputs and imported data. A deterministic, bounded scheduling search considers repairer skills, availability, visitor time windows, parts readiness, and protected appointments. The WebMCP tools and human interface use the same application state. Revision checks reject stale edits, and approval is tied to the exact proposal being reviewed. Event data is stored in the browser tab’s sessionStorage, with downloadable backups. Cloudflare Workers serves the app; there is no event-data backend or embedded chatbot. We used Vitest, Playwright, and axe-core for automated checks, alongside real native WebMCP execution tests. Challenges Browser implementation differences made WebMCP integration more involved than registering a tool catalog. We had to test actual execution and handle differences in argument formats. Scheduling also exposed a subtle distinction: keeping someone’s appointment at exactly the same time is different from keeping that person booked at all. We refined the planning preferences and comparison reports to make that tradeoff explicit. Keeping human edits, agent proposals, and saved state consistent required careful handling of stale reviews and browser Back navigation. Accomplishments that we're proud of I built a complete cancellation-and-recovery workflow where an agent’s proposal appears in the same desk the organizer uses. Protected appointments stay fixed during replanning, excluded requests have concrete explanations, and organizers can preview the cost of making room for another item. I also tested the scheduler against thousands of small, exhaustively checked cases and kept discovered failures as regression tests. Native WebMCP execution was tested separately from the clearly labeled scripted rehearsal. What we learned Useful agent tools need clear consequences as well as clear inputs. Organizers need to know whose appointment changed, why a request was excluded, and whether a proposal still reflects the latest event. I also learned that a passing build does not establish a working product. Native browser execution, keyboard navigation, printed schedules, and saved-state recovery each exposed issues that needed their own checks. What's next My next priority is testing Benchkeeper is to run it on a small clean up operation for a school in my area. the current examples is synthetic so doing something good in the community and using this seems like a good next step. Then the feedback should guide if this was useful to them. For now, the tool works for pre-event planning tool, and its bounded search does not guarantee the best possible schedule. There is also a bigger opportunity with other industries to help with planning and coordination particularly with health-care, education, plumbing and more. Auditable local-first data workflows where people and AI agents collaborate through WebMCP. Like Comment Story Updates Inspiration Data preparation tools are visually dense, stateful, and risky for agents to operate through generic browser automation. An agent can target the wrong dataset, repeat a mutation, lose track of the current revision, or expose sensitive values while inferring state from tables and graph coordinates. TabulaFlow explores a better model: one visible data workflow shared by people and agents, with precise semantic operations and explicit human authority. What it does TabulaFlow is an auditable, local-first data workbench built around Source → Prepare → Compose . Source imports Excel, CSV, JSON, JSONL, and NDJSON while keeping local data in the browser. Prepare profiles data quality, manages ordered transformation recipes, creates deterministic Formula columns, applies protected filters, and supports human-reviewed qualitative coding. Compose validates and chains Append, Join, Difference, Aggregate, Filter, Distinct, Pivot, and Unpivot operations. WebMCP exposes stable, strict tools for agents to inspect, validate, mutate, export, and monitor the same application state shown to the user. People retain control of local file selection, agent-upload consent, destructive confirmation, semantic declassification, and qualitative interpretation. Why WebMCP is a strong fit Generic browser automation must guess from visual coordinates and transient UI state. TabulaFlow instead exposes a semantic agent API through WebMCP. Agents can discover action-specific contracts, target stable dataset and node IDs, dry-run changes, reject stale revisions, replay idempotently, poll asynchronous operations, and cancel before commit. This creates a better user experience because agent work is visible in the normal product, recorded in a shared privacy-safe activity ledger, and governed by the same validation and transaction boundaries as direct user actions. People and agents can now collaborate on multi-step data preparation and graph-based composition without hiding mutations inside an opaque autonomous workflow. How we built it Frontend: React 19 and Vite Analytical engine: DuckDB-Wasm in a dedicated browser worker Agent interface: document.modelContext.registerTool() with stable dispatcher tools and strict action-specific JSON Schemas Persistence: IndexedDB flow metadata, recipes, graph positions, and activity events Safety: optimistic workspace revisions, idempotency keys, protected-value bindings, bounded redacted previews, writer serialization, asynchronous lifecycle, cooperative cancellation, and visible user confirmations Hosting: ChatGPT Sites-compatible production bundle The current contract exposes 17 stable WebMCP tools and 60 bounded Source, Prepare, Compose, and qualitative-coding action routes. What people and agents can do together Agents can inspect schemas and quality, validate candidate changes, maintain recipes, create Compose operations, promote results, export datasets, and report evidence-backed qualitative coding suggestions. Users can see and review the same state, undo transformations, reject unsafe actions, and retain exclusive authority over sensitive boundaries. How we used AI Codex and ChatGPT supported implementation, debugging, regression testing, live WebMCP QC, documentation, and demo preparation. The human owner set product direction, reviewed trade-offs, controlled releases, and accepted or rejected changes. Testing instructions Open the live app in ChatGPT's in-app browser or Chrome with WebMCP enabled. Download the synthetic fixture from the public repository at examples/webmcp-demo.csv . Import it manually in Source. No login is required. Call the capabilities, workspace-state, and workflow-guide tools. Inspect the dataset and data profile through Prepare. Add a Formula column named weight_gap_kg with [chargeable_weight_kg] - [actual_weight_kg] . In Compose, validate and create an Aggregate grouped by service , with count and average actual-weight metrics. Inspect the operation schema, preview, and quality. Demonstrate stale-revision rejection, idempotent replay, cancellation, or the shared activity ledger. Do not confirm deletion or reset. Full walkthrough: https://github.com/jacobcalvyn/TabulaFlow/blob/main/docs/DEMO.md Challenges and accomplishments The hardest work was making a complex data mutation surface agent-safe without duplicating application logic. We hardened strict schema discovery, protected recipe round-trips, privacy-aware previews, atomic tool publication, asynchronous cancellation, response pagination, and local-file recovery boundaries. The result passed live WebMCP regression coverage across Prepare and all eight Compose operation types, alongside repository tests and production build checks. Built during the challenge TabulaFlow's WebMCP integration, safety contracts, Formula workflow, Compose parity, agent upload consent, qualitative-coding boundary, and demo materials were implemented and hardened during the challenge period. Dated evidence is available in docs/HACKATHON.md and the Git history. Known limitations Browser imports are capped at 50 MB, 100,000 rows, and 1,000 stored columns. Local file permissions may require explicit re-linking. Formula columns intentionally exclude window, rank, LOD, regex, and nondeterministic current-time functions. WebMCP requires a compatible browser implementation.
