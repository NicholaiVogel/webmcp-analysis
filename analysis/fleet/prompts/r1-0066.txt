# FROZEN SCORING RUBRIC (pre-registered before any Stage 1 review)

Scores are integers 1-10 on each of the FOUR official criteria. Every score carries:
rationale, evidence_surfaces, confidence (0-1). Reviewers use the full scale and these
band anchors verbatim. Category neutrality is absolute: a game, art piece, music tool,
CRM, weird toy, and developer tool are all eligible for 10/10 on every criterion if they
satisfy that criterion exceptionally well FOR WHAT THEY ARE TRYING TO BE.
No aesthetic penalties. Signs of hurried AI work matter only as actual product-quality
problems (incoherence, broken flow, placeholder content), never as style.

## WebMCP Leverage — what does WebMCP change for this product?
Judge the delta WebMCP makes to reliability, precision, structured state access,
shared human-agent state, repeatability, and capability. Tool COUNT is not leverage.
- 9-10 TRANSFORMATIVE: the intended experience is impractical without the structured
  agent surface; human and agent share live product state; the collaboration could not
  be replicated comparably well by a general agent driving the UI.
- 7-8 MAJOR DELTA: without WebMCP a general agent is markedly less reliable/precise
  here; the agent is central to the product, not a bolt-on.
- 5-6 MEANINGFUL DELTA: existing capability packaged into a genuinely better product
  experience via structured tools; same outcome otherwise possible with real friction.
- 3-4 MINOR DELTA: generic browser automation achieves comparable outcomes; WebMCP is
  a convenience layer.
- 1-2 COSMETIC: exposes an action a UI button already offers; no meaningful change.

## Execution — is the actual product coherent, intentional, complete for its scope?
Stage 1 scores EXECUTION_CLAIMED: coherence/intentionality/completeness as evidenced by
the packet (description + packaging + video frames/transcript). Thin evidence caps the
score (end-to-end credit requires end-to-end evidence) and lowers confidence.
Stage 2 may rescore as EXECUTION_OBSERVED; latest evidence-backed score replaces, never
sums.
- 9-10: all surfaces agree; central workflow demonstrably completes; states handled;
  feels finished for its stated scope.
- 7-8: strong coherence and intentionality; central workflow appears functional; minor
  unfinished edges.
- 5-6: plausibly real with visible gaps; scope unclear or core not demonstrated
  end-to-end.
- 3-4: thin prototype; contradictions between surfaces; placeholder content.
- 1-2: non-functional or incoherent for its claimed scope.

## Creativity & Ambition — novelty of concept and interaction model
Judge ONLY: novelty of the concept, novelty of the interaction model, originality
relative to existing concepts, ambition, memorable/surprising use of the medium, and
the depth with which the concept is pursued. WebMCP indispensability belongs under
WebMCP Leverage, NOT here: a project can be extremely creative even if another agent
API could theoretically have enabled something similar.
- 9-10: genuinely novel concept or interaction model; ambitious scope pursued deeply
  and coherently; memorable.
- 7-8: fresh synthesis or notably original interaction; ambitious for a hackathon.
- 5-6: competent take on a known category with a real new angle.
- 3-4: derivative; novelty mostly surface details.
- 1-2: clone/template with trivial changes.
"That the category exists" is never a penalty; judge the submitted concept.

## Potential Impact — credible value for the intended audience
Judge: is there a real audience; is there a real problem, desire, or use case for that
audience; does the demonstrated solution genuinely address it. NO commercial adoption,
monetization, market pull, or startup viability is required. Entertainment, play, art,
creative expression, learning, niche hobby use, experimentation, and community value
are legitimate forms of impact. A tiny audience fully justifies a top score.
- 9-10: specific real audience with a real problem/desire; solution demonstrably
  addresses it; credible value, appeal, usefulness, or meaning for that audience.
- 7-8: clear audience and problem, good fit, credible value.
- 5-6: identifiable audience, looser fit or partial address.
- 3-4: speculative audience or strained fit; impact mostly aspirational.
- 1-2: no coherent audience/problem pairing.
Audience size is NOT a multiplier.

## Aggregate & ranking
aggregate = leverage + execution + impact + creativity (4-40). Nothing else enters.
Tie-breaks: Leverage, then Execution, then Impact, then Creativity, then higher
combined evidence confidence, then slug alphabetical. See PROTOCOL.md Ranking section.

Evidence qualifies. Authority promotes. WebMCP makes it possible for websites to expose structured tools to AI agents. But exposing a function does not answer a harder question: Which actions have actually earned the right to become agent-callable tools? WebMCP Foundry is a capability compiler and verification workbench for the agent-native web. It takes actions that already exist in a human-facing web application and turns them into minimized, tested, effect-aware, evidence-backed, authority-governed WebMCP capabilities . Instead of treating registerTool() as the beginning of the process, Foundry treats native WebMCP registration as the output of an engineering lifecycle : human action → candidate → contract → minimized inputs → effects + authority → verification → evidence → promotion → LIVE WebMCP tool If the underlying implementation changes, its old evidence no longer silently remains valid. Foundry marks the affected capability STALE and withdraws it from the browser's native WebMCP registry until fresh evidence qualifies it again. What it does The demo uses Ledgerly , an ordinary human-facing financial web application. Foundry discovers actions such as transaction search, notes, balance adjustments, fund transfers, and account deletion. It then determines what part of each action should actually be agent-controlled. For example, an initially over-broad action with four controllable fields is reduced to only two agent-visible inputs, while trusted session and identity state remain application-controlled. Foundry then verifies proposed capabilities using explicit contracts, observed effects, negative tests, and deliberately incorrect must-kill mutants . A check only contributes evidence if it can reject its assigned known-bad implementation. The demo shows the complete lifecycle: Discover actions from the human application. Minimize an over-broad interface from 4 inputs to 2 agent-controlled inputs. Find a concrete counterexample in an incorrect implementation. Block promotion because the evidence is FAIL. Repair the implementation and obtain fresh evidence. Refuse an agent's attempt to self-promote a consequential financial capability. Allow an appropriately authorized human to promote it. Register it through native document.modelContext . Execute the capability through the browser's native WebMCP tool registry. Change the underlying source, causing LIVE → STALE and automatic native tool withdrawal. The browser registry and Foundry state are continuously reconciled: LIVE ⇔ present in document.modelContext.getTools() Everything else — CANDIDATE, VERIFIED-but-unpromoted, BLOCKED, STALE or WITHDRAWN — must remain absent. Why WebMCP Without WebMCP, Foundry would only be a verifier describing which actions could be safe agent tools. WebMCP makes the final step real. A capability that passes Foundry's gates becomes an actual browser-native tool. When its evidence becomes stale, Foundry aborts its registration and the tool actually disappears from the agent's available interface. That closes the loop between software assurance and the browser's agent surface. How we built it Almost the entire authoritative system is implemented in dependency-free Julia using only the standard library . Julia owns: capability and effect models authority policy contract minimization verification mutation testing deterministic gates stale-dependency analysis the hash-chained evidence ledger HTTP serving JSON handling the demo application the Foundry UI native-host acceptance grading JavaScript is intentionally limited to the browser boundary where WebMCP exists. A small bridge projects Foundry's LIVE manifest onto document.modelContext , maintains one AbortController per tool, reconciles getTools() , and reports native browser observations back to the authoritative Julia system. The development polyfill is explicitly separated from native WebMCP and cannot satisfy Foundry's native acceptance gate . Challenges One of the biggest challenges was avoiding a verifier that simply agreed with itself. We addressed this with external observations, explicit non-PASS verdicts, counterexamples, and must-kill mutants. UNKNOWN, UNGRADABLE and BLOCKED cannot silently become PASS. Another challenge was the experimental nature of WebMCP itself. During native testing we found that our Edge build exposed the expected document.modelContext API but differed from the current WebIDL expectation for one executeTool() input form. Foundry records that disagreement rather than hiding it. Deployment was another practical challenge. The public entry point now monitors the live Foundry and Ledgerly services and redirects judges to their current HTTPS origins. Accomplishments we're proud of A working native WebMCP lifecycle rather than a simulated tool registry. LIVE → STALE → native withdrawal driven by real dependency changes. Agent self-ratification of consequential capabilities is refused. Trusted/session-bound fields cannot be forged through the agent contract. Verification checks must demonstrate that they can reject known-bad controls. A replayable, hash-chained evidence and promotion ledger. A dependency-free Julia authority kernel with only a minimal JavaScript WebMCP boundary. Native WebMCP acceptance tested against the deployed application. What we learned WebMCP makes exposing tools easy. The difficult engineering problem begins one level earlier: What should be exposed, under what contract, backed by what evidence, and for how long does that evidence remain valid? We found it useful to treat an application's agent interface as a derived software artifact rather than a static list of callable functions. What's next The next step is to generalize Foundry beyond the Ledgerly demonstration: richer application adapters, CI integration, additional capability-effect models, and standards-backed normative provenance. The longer-term idea is for WebMCP to be one compilation target of a protocol-independent capability IR. WebMCP lets websites expose tools to agents. Instead of forcing a customer to search for an order number, navigate support menus, or explain the same problem several times, the customer can simply describe what went wrong in natural language. The agent can then discover the website's structured WebMCP capabilities, find the correct purchase, read authoritative Shopify state, and prepare the next step. But there is one deliberate boundary: The agent can help. It cannot silently become the customer or the merchant. The customer keeps customer authority. The merchant keeps merchant authority. From that natural-language request, the agent discovers the site's WebMCP tools, finds the correct authenticated purchase, reads its return eligibility, and prepares a resolution. Then: Agent prepares → Customer commits → Merchant decides → Shopify records the truth. 💡 Inspiration AI shopping experiences are becoming increasingly capable before checkout. Agents can search. They can compare. They can recommend. They can help people buy. But commerce does not end at checkout. Products arrive damaged. The wrong variant is delivered. Shipments arrive late. Customers need exchanges, returns, replacements, or refunds. And that part of the experience is still dominated by: order numbers support menus forms ticket queues policy pages repeated explanations disconnected customer and merchant workflows That led us to a simple question: If the web is becoming agentic, what should happen when the purchase itself goes wrong? We did not want to build another chatbot floating on top of an ecommerce site. We wanted the website itself to expose meaningful, structured capabilities that an agent could understand and use safely. That is where WebMCP became the core of the product rather than an add-on. ✨ What it does Post-Purchase Resolution lets a customer describe a purchase problem naturally. For example: “The action camera I bought recently arrived damaged and won't turn on. Can you find the purchase and tell me what I can do? That action occurs under the customer's own Shopify Customer Account authority. 🏪 Merchant The merchant receives the pending request in a separate decision desk.
