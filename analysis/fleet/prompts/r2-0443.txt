=== RUBRIC (frozen) ===
# FROZEN SCORING RUBRIC (pre-registered before any Stage 1 review)

Scores are integers 1-10 on each of the FOUR official criteria. Every score carries:
rationale, evidence_surfaces, confidence (0-1). Reviewers use the full scale and these
band anchors verbatim. Category neutrality is absolute: a game, art piece, music tool,
CRM, weird toy, and developer tool are all eligible for 10/10 on every criterion if they
satisfy that criterion exceptionally well FOR WHAT THEY ARE TRYING TO BE.
No aesthetic penalties. Signs of hurried AI work matter only as actual product-quality
problems (incoherence, broken flow, placeholder content), never as style.

## WebMCP Leverage — what does WebMCP change for this product?
Judge the delta WebMCP makes to reliability, precision, structured state access,
shared human-agent state, repeatability, and capability. Tool COUNT is not leverage.
- 9-10 TRANSFORMATIVE: the intended experience is impractical without the structured
  agent surface; human and agent share live product state; the collaboration could not
  be replicated comparably well by a general agent driving the UI.
- 7-8 MAJOR DELTA: without WebMCP a general agent is markedly less reliable/precise
  here; the agent is central to the product, not a bolt-on.
- 5-6 MEANINGFUL DELTA: existing capability packaged into a genuinely better product
  experience via structured tools; same outcome otherwise possible with real friction.
- 3-4 MINOR DELTA: generic browser automation achieves comparable outcomes; WebMCP is
  a convenience layer.
- 1-2 COSMETIC: exposes an action a UI button already offers; no meaningful change.

## Execution — is the actual product coherent, intentional, complete for its scope?
Stage 1 scores EXECUTION_CLAIMED: coherence/intentionality/completeness as evidenced by
the packet (description + packaging + video frames/transcript). Thin evidence caps the
score (end-to-end credit requires end-to-end evidence) and lowers confidence.
Stage 2 may rescore as EXECUTION_OBSERVED; latest evidence-backed score replaces, never
sums.
- 9-10: all surfaces agree; central workflow demonstrably completes; states handled;
  feels finished for its stated scope.
- 7-8: strong coherence and intentionality; central workflow appears functional; minor
  unfinished edges.
- 5-6: plausibly real with visible gaps; scope unclear or core not demonstrated
  end-to-end.
- 3-4: thin prototype; contradictions between surfaces; placeholder content.
- 1-2: non-functional or incoherent for its claimed scope.

## Creativity & Ambition — novelty of concept and interaction model
Judge ONLY: novelty of the concept, novelty of the interaction model, originality
relative to existing concepts, ambition, memorable/surprising use of the medium, and
the depth with which the concept is pursued. WebMCP indispensability belongs under
WebMCP Leverage, NOT here: a project can be extremely creative even if another agent
API could theoretically have enabled something similar.
- 9-10: genuinely novel concept or interaction model; ambitious scope pursued deeply
  and coherently; memorable.
- 7-8: fresh synthesis or notably original interaction; ambitious for a hackathon.
- 5-6: competent take on a known category with a real new angle.
- 3-4: derivative; novelty mostly surface details.
- 1-2: clone/template with trivial changes.
"That the category exists" is never a penalty; judge the submitted concept.

## Potential Impact — credible value for the intended audience
Judge: is there a real audience; is there a real problem, desire, or use case for that
audience; does the demonstrated solution genuinely address it. NO commercial adoption,
monetization, market pull, or startup viability is required. Entertainment, play, art,
creative expression, learning, niche hobby use, experimentation, and community value
are legitimate forms of impact. A tiny audience fully justifies a top score.
- 9-10: specific real audience with a real problem/desire; solution demonstrably
  addresses it; credible value, appeal, usefulness, or meaning for that audience.
- 7-8: clear audience and problem, good fit, credible value.
- 5-6: identifiable audience, looser fit or partial address.
- 3-4: speculative audience or strained fit; impact mostly aspirational.
- 1-2: no coherent audience/problem pairing.
Audience size is NOT a multiplier.

## Aggregate & ranking
aggregate = leverage + execution + impact + creativity (4-40). Nothing else enters.
Tie-breaks: Leverage, then Execution, then Impact, then Creativity, then higher
combined evidence confidence, then slug alphabetical. See PROTOCOL.md Ranking section.

I wanted to build something that catches that before the worksheet goes out, not another tool that generates content, but one that keeps checking whether the content actually works, the whole time you're building it. I know that system through and through, so I also know this: applying from start to finish takes hours of concentration and focus. That is true for individuals, assisters, and human agents alike. That is why it immediately clicked for me: this is a massive opportunity to upgrade US healthcare benefits sites, so faster eligibility submissions can lead to faster benefits for every citizen. I am going to showcase this demo I made for the hackathon to my project leadership as well. What it does CivicFlow is a demo public-benefits portal. A person can fill out six sections: About You, Household, Income, Current Coverage, Documents, and Review & Sign. A browser agent can work on that same form through WebMCP, instead of guessing from the page layout. The agent can check what is left, jump to a section, add a household member or income source, set current coverage, look at demo document status, and review what is blocking submit. There is also an optional Gemini Live companion for voice or chat. If the companion wants to change something, it has to wait for the person to click Save change, Need correction, or Cancel. Talking is not enough. This is a fictional local demo. Submit Demo is human-only, stays on the device, and does not call a real government system. There is no real eligibility, enrollment, document upload, or agent submit. How we built it I built CivicFlow with React, TypeScript, and Vite. The form, the WebMCP tools, and the Gemini companion all go through the same app state. That way the agent cannot take a side path the person cannot see. There are ten typed tools. Seven are always there. The other three only show up when they make sense, like when a household member is selected or the person is on Review & Sign. The page registers those tools with document.modelContext.registerTool , and it updates the list when the current section or selection changes. Gemini only sees the tools that are available right now. It still has to pass schema checks, and any write waits for a button click. The Gemini API key stays on the server. If WebMCP or voice is not available, the normal form still works. I used Vitest and Playwright to cover the main flow and the safety rules. Challenges we ran into The hard part was not adding more tools. It was making sure help never turned into silent control. The form, the browser agent, and the voice companion had to share one state, with no hidden shortcut around validation or human review. Contextual tools were also tricky. They have to show up and disappear as the person moves around the form. And a successful tool call means nothing if the person cannot see what changed, so I added the floating companion, a speaking cue, the current tool list, operation status, and an activity history. Keeping the Gemini key on the server, while still letting the demo work without voice, was another one. Accomplishments that we're proud of What I am most proud of is getting a realtime voice agent to talk to the site tools. You can ask for help out loud, and it can guide you through the form without typing. That is the part that feels closest to how I wish benefits sites worked. I also think this can help people with vision or other disabilities, because they would not have to hunt through a long form just to make progress. The other piece I really liked building is the draft popup during realtime voice. The companion can propose a change, but it does not silently write it. A confirmation card shows up first, and you still choose Save change, Need correction, or Cancel. That small pause is what kept voice helpful instead of scary. What we learned WebMCP is most useful when it is part of the product, not a shortcut around the UI. Clear tool names, schemas, and contextual tools tell the agent what it can do. Visible confirmation and activity history help the person trust it, and step in if something looks wrong. I also learned that the page still has to be a good form on its own. If WebMCP or voice is down, a person should still be able to finish the demo by hand. What's next for CivicFlow: A WebMCP Public Benefit Portal Next, I want to put this in front of people who actually fill out long benefits forms, and keep improving accessibility and the “did that change what I think it changed?” feedback. I also want to take the demo to Optum leadership and talk about whether this pattern could help real healthcare benefits sites. Connecting to real program rules, identity, uploads, eligibility, or live submit is a much bigger security and policy project. That is not part of this hackathon demo.
