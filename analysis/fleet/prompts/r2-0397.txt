# FROZEN SCORING RUBRIC (pre-registered before any Stage 1 review)

Scores are integers 1-10 on each of the FOUR official criteria. Every score carries:
rationale, evidence_surfaces, confidence (0-1). Reviewers use the full scale and these
band anchors verbatim. Category neutrality is absolute: a game, art piece, music tool,
CRM, weird toy, and developer tool are all eligible for 10/10 on every criterion if they
satisfy that criterion exceptionally well FOR WHAT THEY ARE TRYING TO BE.
No aesthetic penalties. Signs of hurried AI work matter only as actual product-quality
problems (incoherence, broken flow, placeholder content), never as style.

## WebMCP Leverage — what does WebMCP change for this product?
Judge the delta WebMCP makes to reliability, precision, structured state access,
shared human-agent state, repeatability, and capability. Tool COUNT is not leverage.
- 9-10 TRANSFORMATIVE: the intended experience is impractical without the structured
  agent surface; human and agent share live product state; the collaboration could not
  be replicated comparably well by a general agent driving the UI.
- 7-8 MAJOR DELTA: without WebMCP a general agent is markedly less reliable/precise
  here; the agent is central to the product, not a bolt-on.
- 5-6 MEANINGFUL DELTA: existing capability packaged into a genuinely better product
  experience via structured tools; same outcome otherwise possible with real friction.
- 3-4 MINOR DELTA: generic browser automation achieves comparable outcomes; WebMCP is
  a convenience layer.
- 1-2 COSMETIC: exposes an action a UI button already offers; no meaningful change.

## Execution — is the actual product coherent, intentional, complete for its scope?
Stage 1 scores EXECUTION_CLAIMED: coherence/intentionality/completeness as evidenced by
the packet (description + packaging + video frames/transcript). Thin evidence caps the
score (end-to-end credit requires end-to-end evidence) and lowers confidence.
Stage 2 may rescore as EXECUTION_OBSERVED; latest evidence-backed score replaces, never
sums.
- 9-10: all surfaces agree; central workflow demonstrably completes; states handled;
  feels finished for its stated scope.
- 7-8: strong coherence and intentionality; central workflow appears functional; minor
  unfinished edges.
- 5-6: plausibly real with visible gaps; scope unclear or core not demonstrated
  end-to-end.
- 3-4: thin prototype; contradictions between surfaces; placeholder content.
- 1-2: non-functional or incoherent for its claimed scope.

## Creativity & Ambition — novelty of concept and interaction model
Judge ONLY: novelty of the concept, novelty of the interaction model, originality
relative to existing concepts, ambition, memorable/surprising use of the medium, and
the depth with which the concept is pursued. WebMCP indispensability belongs under
WebMCP Leverage, NOT here: a project can be extremely creative even if another agent
API could theoretically have enabled something similar.
- 9-10: genuinely novel concept or interaction model; ambitious scope pursued deeply
  and coherently; memorable.
- 7-8: fresh synthesis or notably original interaction; ambitious for a hackathon.
- 5-6: competent take on a known category with a real new angle.
- 3-4: derivative; novelty mostly surface details.
- 1-2: clone/template with trivial changes.
"That the category exists" is never a penalty; judge the submitted concept.

## Potential Impact — credible value for the intended audience
Judge: is there a real audience; is there a real problem, desire, or use case for that
audience; does the demonstrated solution genuinely address it. NO commercial adoption,
monetization, market pull, or startup viability is required. Entertainment, play, art,
creative expression, learning, niche hobby use, experimentation, and community value
are legitimate forms of impact. A tiny audience fully justifies a top score.
- 9-10: specific real audience with a real problem/desire; solution demonstrably
  addresses it; credible value, appeal, usefulness, or meaning for that audience.
- 7-8: clear audience and problem, good fit, credible value.
- 5-6: identifiable audience, looser fit or partial address.
- 3-4: speculative audience or strained fit; impact mostly aspirational.
- 1-2: no coherent audience/problem pairing.
Audience size is NOT a multiplier.

## Aggregate & ranking
aggregate = leverage + execution + impact + creativity (4-40). Nothing else enters.
Tie-breaks: Leverage, then Execution, then Impact, then Creativity, then higher
combined evidence confidence, then slug alphabetical. See PROTOCOL.md Ranking section.

Camera Zoom Interpolation At any playback timestamp t , the camera transform state is represented by a 2D affine vector: T(t) = [ s(t), x_f(t), y_f(t) ]ᵀ where s(t) is the zoom scale factor and (x_f, y_f) ∈ [0, 1]² is the normalized focal target. Like Comment Story Updates 1 Inspiration I built blnq.studio because front-end work had become a pile of tabs: an editor, a dev server, a browser, DevTools, an accessibility checker, a design spec, and now an AI chat window that can't see any of them. Every one of those handoffs is where quality leaks out — especially accessibility, which is the first thing dropped when you're moving fast and the last thing an AI agent notices, because it can't see what it built. I wanted a single tab where the code, the live preview, the audit and the AI all look at the same thing. WebMCP was the missing piece: it lets the editor tab itself become the tool surface an agent uses, so the loop closes without any extra setup. What it does blnq.studio is a browser-based editor purpose-built for front-end: code, preview, ship — all in one tab. Live multi-file editing — Monaco under the hood, real HTML/CSS/JS files with folders and ES module imports, no build step. The preview updates as you type. Accessibility first — every preview refresh runs the pour engine against WCAG 2.0–2.2 A/AA plus best-practice rules. The A11y panel lists each violation with its impact, a plain-English fix and the exact element. Colour-contrast checks are built into the editor, not bolted on afterwards. Multi-device preview — switch viewports, check every breakpoint, see the FPS overlay, all without leaving the tab. Design systems your AI respects — drop a DESIGN.md at the project root and the editor treats it as the source of truth: colour and font literals that drift from your tokens get flagged, so AI-written CSS stays inside the system instead of inventing new values. Baseline compatibility — a built-in tool tab tells you which features you're using aren't yet widely available across browsers. Collaborate and fork — real-time multi-cursor collaboration over Yjs, forks and merge requests, a community showcase. Ship from the editor — push to GitHub, deploy from the menu, or download the whole project as one self-contained HTML file. AI & MCP, on your terms — AI is optional. When you want it, connect Claude Code (or any MCP client) over the built-in MCP server, use the in-editor chat with your own model, or — new for this challenge — turn on WebMCP and let a browser-native agent drive the tab directly. WebMCP: the editor tab is the tool server Click Start WebMCP and blnq registers 30+ tools with the browser via document.modelContext . No MCP ID, no config file, no separate process. An agent running in the browser can then: Read and edit files by line range or find-and-replace, with undo snapshots and live edits landing in the editor and preview See what it built — blnq_screenshot returns a real PNG, plus element inspection, console errors and viewport switching Audit with blnq_check_preview , which returns JS errors, every WCAG violation with its element HTML and fix hint, and any design-token drift Interact — click, type, press keys, wait for elements — so it can tab through a form or open a modal and re-audit the new state Fix and re-audit until the report is clean The key design decision was that WebMCP isn't a separate integration. blnq has one shared tool registry; the MCP server and the WebMCP endpoint are two thin adapters over it. SituationRoom turns messy vendor proposals into a live, auditable decision model that humans and AI agents build together by mapping evidence, testing scenarios, and approving decisions using WebMCP. Like Comment Story Updates Complex decisions rarely fail because there is too little information. They fail because the information is fragmented. A procurement team comparing vendors might have pricing tables, contracts, technical proposals, requirements, risk notes, implementation timelines, and assumptions spread across dozens of pages. An AI assistant can summarize those documents, but a summary is not a decision system. It is difficult to inspect exactly why a recommendation was made, correct individual assumptions, test alternatives, or safely turn a recommendation into an action. I built SituationRoom around a different question: What should a website become when an AI agent is no longer just visiting it, but collaborating with a human inside it? Instead of making an agent navigate a traditional application through buttons and forms, SituationRoom exposes the semantics of the decision itself through WebMCP. For that purpose, SituationRoom is an agent-native workspace for complex, high-stakes decisions. My demonstration focuses on vendor procurement as follows: Given several competing vendor proposals and a set of business constraints, an AI agent can use SituationRoom's WebMCP tools to construct a structured decision model. It can: define evaluation criteria and their relative importance create mandatory requirements normalize costs across proposals associate claims with their supporting evidence identify missing information and contradictions record risk assumptions construct total-cost-of-ownership models run alternative decision scenarios compare the resulting rankings prepare a proposed decision for human review SituationRoom visualizes that state as an evidence matrix, cost model, risk analysis, scenario comparison, and audit timeline. The human can then directly change an assumption, lock a requirement, correct an evidence mapping, or alter a decision criterion. The agent sees the changed state through WebMCP and can immediately reason again from the corrected model. Before a consequential decision is finalized, SituationRoom separates preview from commit so that the human can inspect exactly what will happen before approving it.
