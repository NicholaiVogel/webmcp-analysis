# FROZEN SCORING RUBRIC (pre-registered before any Stage 1 review)

Scores are integers 1-10 on each of the FOUR official criteria. Every score carries:
rationale, evidence_surfaces, confidence (0-1). Reviewers use the full scale and these
band anchors verbatim. Category neutrality is absolute: a game, art piece, music tool,
CRM, weird toy, and developer tool are all eligible for 10/10 on every criterion if they
satisfy that criterion exceptionally well FOR WHAT THEY ARE TRYING TO BE.
No aesthetic penalties. Signs of hurried AI work matter only as actual product-quality
problems (incoherence, broken flow, placeholder content), never as style.

## WebMCP Leverage — what does WebMCP change for this product?
Judge the delta WebMCP makes to reliability, precision, structured state access,
shared human-agent state, repeatability, and capability. Tool COUNT is not leverage.
- 9-10 TRANSFORMATIVE: the intended experience is impractical without the structured
  agent surface; human and agent share live product state; the collaboration could not
  be replicated comparably well by a general agent driving the UI.
- 7-8 MAJOR DELTA: without WebMCP a general agent is markedly less reliable/precise
  here; the agent is central to the product, not a bolt-on.
- 5-6 MEANINGFUL DELTA: existing capability packaged into a genuinely better product
  experience via structured tools; same outcome otherwise possible with real friction.
- 3-4 MINOR DELTA: generic browser automation achieves comparable outcomes; WebMCP is
  a convenience layer.
- 1-2 COSMETIC: exposes an action a UI button already offers; no meaningful change.

## Execution — is the actual product coherent, intentional, complete for its scope?
Stage 1 scores EXECUTION_CLAIMED: coherence/intentionality/completeness as evidenced by
the packet (description + packaging + video frames/transcript). Thin evidence caps the
score (end-to-end credit requires end-to-end evidence) and lowers confidence.
Stage 2 may rescore as EXECUTION_OBSERVED; latest evidence-backed score replaces, never
sums.
- 9-10: all surfaces agree; central workflow demonstrably completes; states handled;
  feels finished for its stated scope.
- 7-8: strong coherence and intentionality; central workflow appears functional; minor
  unfinished edges.
- 5-6: plausibly real with visible gaps; scope unclear or core not demonstrated
  end-to-end.
- 3-4: thin prototype; contradictions between surfaces; placeholder content.
- 1-2: non-functional or incoherent for its claimed scope.

## Creativity & Ambition — novelty of concept and interaction model
Judge ONLY: novelty of the concept, novelty of the interaction model, originality
relative to existing concepts, ambition, memorable/surprising use of the medium, and
the depth with which the concept is pursued. WebMCP indispensability belongs under
WebMCP Leverage, NOT here: a project can be extremely creative even if another agent
API could theoretically have enabled something similar.
- 9-10: genuinely novel concept or interaction model; ambitious scope pursued deeply
  and coherently; memorable.
- 7-8: fresh synthesis or notably original interaction; ambitious for a hackathon.
- 5-6: competent take on a known category with a real new angle.
- 3-4: derivative; novelty mostly surface details.
- 1-2: clone/template with trivial changes.
"That the category exists" is never a penalty; judge the submitted concept.

## Potential Impact — credible value for the intended audience
Judge: is there a real audience; is there a real problem, desire, or use case for that
audience; does the demonstrated solution genuinely address it. NO commercial adoption,
monetization, market pull, or startup viability is required. Entertainment, play, art,
creative expression, learning, niche hobby use, experimentation, and community value
are legitimate forms of impact. A tiny audience fully justifies a top score.
- 9-10: specific real audience with a real problem/desire; solution demonstrably
  addresses it; credible value, appeal, usefulness, or meaning for that audience.
- 7-8: clear audience and problem, good fit, credible value.
- 5-6: identifiable audience, looser fit or partial address.
- 3-4: speculative audience or strained fit; impact mostly aspirational.
- 1-2: no coherent audience/problem pairing.
Audience size is NOT a multiplier.

## Aggregate & ranking
aggregate = leverage + execution + impact + creativity (4-40). Nothing else enters.
Tie-breaks: Leverage, then Execution, then Impact, then Creativity, then higher
combined evidence confidence, then slug alphabetical. See PROTOCOL.md Ranking section.

How I built it WattKeep is a React, TypeScript and Vite single-page application. A deterministic simulator and an observable local store power both the human interface and the WebMCP adapter. The adapter registers its tools through document.modelContext , with constrained JSON schemas and structured success or recoverable-error results. Every staged proposal records a proposal ID, base revision, scenario and simulation fingerprint. Commit-time validation checks those values against the current workspace before a person can apply the change. Human commits, undo, forecast refresh, discard and session reset are recorded in an append-only journal. The application remains fully usable when WebMCP is unavailable. Inspection and simulation are safe to automate. Applying a policy is not, so I separated proposal preparation from human confirmation and removed commit and undo from the tool surface. Revision and fingerprint checks turn that race into a visible, recoverable error instead of silently applying outdated advice. I also kept the demonstration honest. WattKeep is a planning simulator. The video shows tool calls changing the same state the person can see. Human-only commit and undo controls preserve the final decision. WattKeep showed me that reliable human-agent collaboration also needs transaction-style safety, especially when an agent prepares a decision that a person must approve later. Most WebMCP demos answer one question: what can an agent help me make? I wanted the other one. What should an agent not be allowed to do, and who decides? The answer I wanted to build was: the page decides. Not the prompt. Not the model's judgement. The page. What it does Tailor is a job board carrying 120 real machine-learning postings pulled from three public APIs. Like 1 Comment Story Updates Agent Stage: two showcase scripts rendered by svgent, a terminal and an app Inspiration svgent is a client-only studio for fictional coding-agent sessions. A script describes a chat window or a terminal, with typing, thinking, tool runs, approvals and choices, and the studio renders it to SVG, PNG, GIF and MP4. People use it for READMEs, docs, talks and posts, where a recording of a real session would leak secrets and could not be re-shot or translated. Before this, the studio's AI assist was copy and paste: copy a prompt into a chat, paste the JSON back. WebMCP replaces that with tool calls on the page. The page can also answer with facts a language model cannot see: how long a page runs, when each message lands, what the frame looks like at eleven seconds. The agent works on the stage itself, not on a guess about it. What it does Agent Stage is the studio with WebMCP site tools. The agent in ChatGPT's built-in browser writes the script, sets the surface, look, camera and pages, measures the result, looks at a frame, proposes rewrites, and asks to export. The person keeps the decisions: rewrites and exports wait on a card, every string the script puts on the stage is listed, and each change can be undone. load_script validates a script and previews it at once. direct_camera turns on a deterministic follow camera. inspect_timeline returns per-message timing and a review. snapshot_frame returns a PNG of any moment. fit_duration lands a page on an exact length and reports what it changed. propose_patch puts rewrites on a card. approve_proposal exists only while a proposal is pending and waits for the person's click. export asks first. The rail shows every call. The "Every word on the stage" list holds every string the script puts on the stage, including what a collapse or a page turn hides. The tool descriptions tell the agent to treat what it knows from the conversation as private and to use stand-ins for real names, unless the person relaxes that on the page or in the chat. Credentials never pass. A loaded script keeps only the fields that render. Looks, fonts, images and camera go through their own tools. Live: https://agent.svgent.zakideee.dev/ Code: https://github.com/zakideee/svgent (the page is apps/webmcp/ ) How we built it 13 tools on document.modelContext , with navigator.modelContext as the fallback, registered with an AbortSignal for teardown. approve_proposal is registered only while a proposal is pending. Results are plain objects plus an MCP-style content array (text, and an image part for frames). The studio package gained a small imperative handle: read, replace and patch the project, seek, play, export, render a frame, spotlight a message. An agent's edit goes through the same state the person's controls use, so undo, autosave, validation and preview all see it. Text layout and rasterization run in WASM (boundsvg) in the browser. The animated SVG is CSS keyframes with no JavaScript. There is no server; nothing leaves the tab except the files the person downloads. The submission is apps/webmcp/ : the WebMCP tools, the agent rail, the privacy default, the showcase scripts and the share card, plus the studio handle ( StudioHandle , chrome , renderFrame , spotlight ) and one preview aspect-ratio fix. The studio, scene and render packages predate the challenge. Challenges we ran into ChatGPT's browser exposes document.modelContext , Chromium exposes navigator.modelContext , and the API can appear after the page has loaded. Registration prefers the document, polls for a late API, and reports the outcome in the rail. A dynamic tool ( approve_proposal ) that unregisters itself while a call is in flight fails that call. It now stays registered for a short grace period after the proposal resolves. A script an agent writes from a conversation can carry real names, paths, hosts and keys from that conversation. The privacy default, the script allowlist and the "every word" list exist for that. Accomplishments that we're proud of The whole loop runs in the ChatGPT desktop app (built-in browser, GPT-5.6 Sol) and in Chromium with WebMCP enabled, and the demo video is a recording of it. The agent edits through the same state as the person's controls, so undo, autosave, validation and preview treat both alike. Four of the showcase scripts are problems agent-app users have: a clip for the team channel without the secrets; a reproduction of a decline that was missed, when recording is not allowed; three slides that walk a colleague through choices and approvals; a README hero for your own MCP server.
