# FROZEN SCORING RUBRIC (pre-registered before any Stage 1 review)

Scores are integers 1-10 on each of the FOUR official criteria. Every score carries:
rationale, evidence_surfaces, confidence (0-1). Reviewers use the full scale and these
band anchors verbatim. Category neutrality is absolute: a game, art piece, music tool,
CRM, weird toy, and developer tool are all eligible for 10/10 on every criterion if they
satisfy that criterion exceptionally well FOR WHAT THEY ARE TRYING TO BE.
No aesthetic penalties. Signs of hurried AI work matter only as actual product-quality
problems (incoherence, broken flow, placeholder content), never as style.

## WebMCP Leverage — what does WebMCP change for this product?
Judge the delta WebMCP makes to reliability, precision, structured state access,
shared human-agent state, repeatability, and capability. Tool COUNT is not leverage.
- 9-10 TRANSFORMATIVE: the intended experience is impractical without the structured
  agent surface; human and agent share live product state; the collaboration could not
  be replicated comparably well by a general agent driving the UI.
- 7-8 MAJOR DELTA: without WebMCP a general agent is markedly less reliable/precise
  here; the agent is central to the product, not a bolt-on.
- 5-6 MEANINGFUL DELTA: existing capability packaged into a genuinely better product
  experience via structured tools; same outcome otherwise possible with real friction.
- 3-4 MINOR DELTA: generic browser automation achieves comparable outcomes; WebMCP is
  a convenience layer.
- 1-2 COSMETIC: exposes an action a UI button already offers; no meaningful change.

## Execution — is the actual product coherent, intentional, complete for its scope?
Stage 1 scores EXECUTION_CLAIMED: coherence/intentionality/completeness as evidenced by
the packet (description + packaging + video frames/transcript). Thin evidence caps the
score (end-to-end credit requires end-to-end evidence) and lowers confidence.
Stage 2 may rescore as EXECUTION_OBSERVED; latest evidence-backed score replaces, never
sums.
- 9-10: all surfaces agree; central workflow demonstrably completes; states handled;
  feels finished for its stated scope.
- 7-8: strong coherence and intentionality; central workflow appears functional; minor
  unfinished edges.
- 5-6: plausibly real with visible gaps; scope unclear or core not demonstrated
  end-to-end.
- 3-4: thin prototype; contradictions between surfaces; placeholder content.
- 1-2: non-functional or incoherent for its claimed scope.

## Creativity & Ambition — novelty of concept and interaction model
Judge ONLY: novelty of the concept, novelty of the interaction model, originality
relative to existing concepts, ambition, memorable/surprising use of the medium, and
the depth with which the concept is pursued. WebMCP indispensability belongs under
WebMCP Leverage, NOT here: a project can be extremely creative even if another agent
API could theoretically have enabled something similar.
- 9-10: genuinely novel concept or interaction model; ambitious scope pursued deeply
  and coherently; memorable.
- 7-8: fresh synthesis or notably original interaction; ambitious for a hackathon.
- 5-6: competent take on a known category with a real new angle.
- 3-4: derivative; novelty mostly surface details.
- 1-2: clone/template with trivial changes.
"That the category exists" is never a penalty; judge the submitted concept.

## Potential Impact — credible value for the intended audience
Judge: is there a real audience; is there a real problem, desire, or use case for that
audience; does the demonstrated solution genuinely address it. NO commercial adoption,
monetization, market pull, or startup viability is required. Entertainment, play, art,
creative expression, learning, niche hobby use, experimentation, and community value
are legitimate forms of impact. A tiny audience fully justifies a top score.
- 9-10: specific real audience with a real problem/desire; solution demonstrably
  addresses it; credible value, appeal, usefulness, or meaning for that audience.
- 7-8: clear audience and problem, good fit, credible value.
- 5-6: identifiable audience, looser fit or partial address.
- 3-4: speculative audience or strained fit; impact mostly aspirational.
- 1-2: no coherent audience/problem pairing.
Audience size is NOT a multiplier.

## Aggregate & ranking
aggregate = leverage + execution + impact + creativity (4-40). Nothing else enters.
Tie-breaks: Leverage, then Execution, then Impact, then Creativity, then higher
combined evidence confidence, then slug alphabetical. See PROTOCOL.md Ranking section.

Browser agents were supposed to help, but they still look at the screen and guess where to click — fragile, slow, and easily misled by page content. WebMCP flips that: the page names its own actions. And messaging is the perfect fit, because messaging is made of verbs — send, react, reply, summarize. Name the verbs, and nobody needs sight to use them. Nouns need eyes. Verbs need a voice. What it does Verb is a complete real-time messenger — 1:1 and group chats, reactions, stickers, image and file attachments, editing, unsend, drafts, search, typing and seen states — where every meaningful action is also a typed WebMCP tool: 22 in a signed-in session . Destructive actions (unsend, leave a group) require a second call with confirm: true . How we implemented WebMCP Verb talks to the browser's document.modelContext API directly — no wrapper library. The lifecycle is session-aware. A connection provider owns registration end to end. Weople is relationship intelligence. It connects context, commitments, and opportunities to surface the right person at the right moment. Human judgment stays in control. Like Comment Story Updates Weople started from a simple problem: relationships rarely weaken because people stop caring. They weaken because people, commitments, context, and opportunities fall out of working attention. I wanted something different from a CRM. A CRM manages contacts and records activity. Weople is built for relationship intelligence: helping someone notice the right person at the right moment. The broader idea predates the WebMCP Challenge, but the challenge gave me a reason to turn it into a focused, working vertical slice. The question I wanted to answer: What changes when an agent can reason over the same relationship context a person is using, instead of merely looking at a webpage? That became the challenge version of Weople. What Weople does Weople maintains structured relationship context: people, commitments, upcoming moments, evidence, hypotheses, and relationship policies. In the demo, the user asks ChatGPT: What am I missing today? If something is genuinely worth my attention, add it to my Weople Today view. Through WebMCP, ChatGPT reads the structured context and notices a connection Weople had not precomputed: Maya has an upcoming working session. I still have an unresolved promise to introduce her to someone with relevant experience. Maya's company announced an expansion. That expansion may create scaling pressure, but that's an inference, not a confirmed fact. Carlos recently worked through a similar scaling problem. ChatGPT connects those pieces and creates a timely intervention directly inside the live Weople workspace. The point isn't remembering information. It's surfacing a connection that would otherwise go unnoticed. Why WebMCP WebMCP is a strong fit for Weople because the important information isn't always what's visible on the screen. Relationship intelligence depends on structured concepts such as: people commitments evidence upcoming interactions hypotheses timing authority and relationship policies Without WebMCP, an agent would have to infer meaning from the rendered interface or navigate through multiple pages to reconstruct that state. Instead, Weople exposes semantic tools that let ChatGPT work directly with the application's underlying relationship context. The challenge version exposes six WebMCP tools: Read get_today_context get_person_context get_relationship_context Write create_intervention snooze_intervention update_relationship_policy The human interface and the agent operate on the same Weople state. The agent doesn't need a separate, agent-only version of the application. That creates a collaboration neither side has alone: Weople maintains relationship continuity, ChatGPT notices connections across that context, and the person supplies judgment and authority. The agent can surface a possibility without taking over the relationship. Human judgment stays in the loop I didn't want Weople to become an autonomous agent that contacts people on someone's behalf. Noticing an opportunity and being authorized to act on it are different things. Weople keeps human judgment explicit. The agent might notice that Maya and Carlos could benefit from an introduction. The user still decides whether that introduction is appropriate. The same principle applies to timing. An intervention can be useful but mistimed, so the user can snooze it without rejecting the reasoning behind it. The challenge build keeps observed facts separate from inference. Northstar announcing a new facility is stored as an observed fact. The idea that the expansion may produce operational scaling pressure is stored as a hypothesis, with its own evidence and confidence. That lets the agent reason with uncertainty without turning it into fact. How I built it I kept the challenge version small and focused, rather than trying to build the entire future Weople product. I built the first working version with ChatGPT Sites . I used it to quickly build the interaction model, synthetic relationship state, Today view, People view, person details, policies, and the WebMCP tool surface. Once the application worked, I synced the project to GitHub for a standard source repository and version history. I then pulled the project down to my desktop and continued refining it locally. I used the Impeccable design skill in that pass to improve the visual design, hierarchy, spacing, typography, and overall presentation, while preserving the interaction model and WebMCP behavior. The final challenge application is deliberately lightweight. It uses deterministic synthetic data and local state rather than external email, calendar, or contact integrations. That let me focus the submission on the core question the challenge is testing: what becomes possible when a person and an agent share the same structured web application context? Challenges The most difficult part was not the relationship reasoning itself. It was understanding the boundary between the webpage and the browser environment executing the WebMCP tools. During testing, the Weople page registered its WebMCP tools correctly, and ChatGPT read context successfully. But write operations failed at first because ChatGPT sometimes opened a separate browser window. That new document did not contain the document.modelContext from the already-open Weople page. At one point the agent reasoned correctly about the Maya/Carlos opportunity and described the action it intended to take, but the actual Weople intervention was never written. That was a useful failure because it forced me to distinguish between: the model saying an action occurred and the WebMCP tool actually returning a successful result and changing application state. Once the browser was operating on the document that actually registered the Weople tools, the full read → reason → write loop worked, and the intervention appeared immediately in the live interface. What I learned The biggest lesson: WebMCP gets interesting when it exposes an application's semantics instead of recreating its buttons as tools. create_intervention is useful because it represents a meaningful Weople operation. The agent does not need to know which button to press, where a card is rendered, or how the DOM is structured. That changed how I think about the relationship between people, agents, and software. The web application does not have to become invisible when an agent appears. The person can continue using Weople visually while ChatGPT works through the same state semantically. The two interfaces become different projections of the same system. That's the experience I wanted this prototype to demonstrate. What's next The challenge version is a narrow vertical slice. The larger direction for Weople is to connect relationship context from sources like calendars, email, contacts, and personal knowledge. Relationship-specific reasoning, evidence, attention, and authority stay inside Weople. The goal is not to automate relationships. It is to augment human attention around them. Weople remembers. The agent notices. The human keeps judgment. General-purpose agents can try to interpret screenshots, but pixels are an unreliable source for exact figures, units, and provenance. A screen reader can traverse a table row by row, but a chart often collapses to “image.” The underlying problem is broader, though: conventional dashboards present answers chosen by their designers instead of letting each person interrogate the underlying evidence directly. Auricle opens with this gap in plain sight: 2,026 rows. Zero answers. Four real climate datasets are available as semantic tables, but no dashboard or on-page chatbot is present. Solution Auricle turns the webpage itself into an interviewable climate instrument. The page exposes WebMCP tools backed by its own data. A user asks through their browser agent; the agent calls the page's tools; Auricle computes an exact, source-attributed answer and mirrors the result into the shared page. Ask “Show me warming over time” and the page creates a NASA temperature chart. That action also registers the chart's five-tool family at runtime. Ask “When was the hottest year?” and Auricle answers +1.28 °C in 2024 while highlighting the point in gold. Ask “Play the century as sound” and it sonifies 146 years as a rising tone with a peak cue. Ask for warming stripes and the same data appears in a second, queryable form. The result is not a chatbot bolted onto a dashboard. The browser agent supplies the conversation; the site supplies authoritative tools, data, visual state, speech-ready answers, and sonification. Every chart on the screen exists because somebody asked for it. Why This Matters Auricle demonstrates a new interaction model for the agentic web: people and agents compose and interrogate a live dashboard together without exporting data or guessing at pixels. The page remains the source of truth, the human directs the inquiry, and everyone in the session shares one evolving surface. For blind and low-vision users, page-owned tools can replace pixel interpretation with exact, source-backed spoken answers and sonification. For everyone else, the same system makes complex evidence faster to explore and easier to verify. Accessibility is therefore a demanding proof case for a broadly useful product—not the boundary of Auricle's audience. The reusable contribution is an agent-accessibility grammar: Orient the agent before asking it to act. Make the tool surface follow the user's focus. Return narratable answers with figures, units, and provenance. Mirror every agent action into the visible and accessible page state. Treat non-visual encodings such as sound as first-class capabilities. This pattern can extend beyond climate data to finance, health, public policy, and any data-rich site where fixed dashboards or visual-only answers limit exploration. Key Features Dashboard by conversation: the initial publication has no charts; create_view commissions each requested view. Dynamic WebMCP surface: five global tools exist at boot, while dataset-specific families register, change with focus, and unregister when their final view is removed—21 tools across the complete experience. Exact page-owned answers: queries run against baked NASA, Our World in Data, and NOAA data, with figure, unit, and source in every narrated return. Shared visual evidence: tool calls create views, highlight points and ranges, and leave a provenance footnote on the page. Accessible HTML foundation: semantic landmarks, real data tables, chart labels, keyboard-operable controls, reduced-motion support, and a visually hidden aria-live answer log. Sonification: time series become a roughly three-second pitch sweep with a distinct peak cue. Nine visualization forms: line, area, warming stripes, vertical and horizontal bars, share bars, scatter, live, and stat views. Self-describing experience: describe_screen tells a newly arrived agent what is present, focused, available, and possible next. Architecture Auricle is a static Vite + React + TypeScript application deployed on Vercel with no backend. Source datasets are fetched by a keyless script and baked into JSON, so the production experience does not depend on runtime data APIs. A framework-agnostic registry wraps each internal tool definition into the WebMCP document.modelContext.registerTool shape. Five orientation and workspace tools register at page load. When create_view commissions a dataset, an AbortController-scoped family registers for that dataset; focus changes replace the active family, and removing the last view aborts it. The simulated live CO₂ view re-registers its family on each bounded tick so its tool description reflects current session state. Agent calls share one execution path: validate the input, run the page-owned handler, return speech-ready text, emit a mirror event to every relevant view, and append the answer to both the visible provenance trail and hidden aria-live region. Charts are hand-rolled SVG. Known Limitations Accessibility is an important beneficiary and proof case, not a claim that Auricle has completed user validation. The experience was built by sighted developers and has not yet been tested with blind or low-vision users. ChatGPT's built-in browser with Site Tools was the AI agent tested end to end: it discovered Auricle's five global tools, invoked create_view to draw the warming curve, observed five temperature tools register dynamically, and invoked temp-anomaly_describe_trend, whose exact answer Auricle mirrored visibly into the page. Chrome 152 with WebMCP enabled and the Model Context Tool Inspector was used only as developer tooling for protocol-level inspection, not as another AI agent. The Mauna Loa “live” movement is a clearly labeled bounded simulation seeded from the latest baked NOAA weekly mean; it is not presented as a real-time NOAA stream. Sonification and warming stripes have established prior art. Auricle's contribution is combining them with exact, queryable WebMCP operations under a reusable agent-interaction and accessibility grammar. An agent can investigate quickly, but giving it broad action authority makes the human approval step vague or performative. We wanted the browser itself to expose a precise operational contract: enough authority for an agent to gather evidence and stage an exact response, but no authority to approve its own consequential action. Runbook Zero is my answer. It treats WebMCP as a state-aware incident control plane and keeps the product interface as the shared source of truth for both the operator and the agent. What it does Runbook Zero is an installable incident product for the website an operator is actually using. The Codex plugin begins read only, inspects bounded evidence from the selected site, inventories its WebMCP capabilities, and derives an issue specific component, dependency, user flow, telemetry, change, and evidence graph. The generated Incident Pack opens in the Runbook Zero workbench, where real document.modelContext tools let an agent inspect services, trace paths, query signals, record a hypothesis, compare mitigations, and stage one exact action. Every tool call updates and focuses the same topology, telemetry, evidence, mitigation, provenance, and capability interface the human sees. The visible Capability Firewall shows active agent capabilities, unavailable consequential capabilities, application phase, and authority state. apply_approved_mitigation is not registered before approval. The agent has no approval tool. A human must review the exact target, diff, risk, reversibility, assumptions, incident, and seed, then click the visible approval control. Only after that click does apply appear, narrowed to the exact approved mitigation. It disappears immediately after use. For a live target, Runbook Zero releases an origin-, tool-, and input bound receipt that Codex carries back to the target site. If no matching action exists, it produces an honest operator handoff instead of pretending automation happened. The product also includes three polished deterministic packs—checkout pool regression ( INC-042 ), payment queue backlog ( INC-117 ), and catalog cache stampede ( INC-203 )—plus safe local JSON import. They all run through the same domain and WebMCP contracts. The canonical pack makes the judging flow reproducible the installed plugin proves Runbook Zero is not limited to a premade walkthrough. How we built it The workbench uses React 19, TypeScript, Vite, Zustand, a guarded state machine, and a validated Incident Pack v1 schema. Thirteen page defined WebMCP contracts are registered with document.modelContext.registerTool . The active subset is derived from incident phase, the current pack, approval state, and execution mode; stale registrations are cancelled with AbortController whenever that state changes. The repository hosted Codex plugin defines a conservative Site Capture v2 contract. It treats page text, tool descriptions, and tool results as untrusted evidence, rejects browser secrets and invalid cross references, and deterministically builds a graph and provisional diagnosis from the evidence actually observed. Live action receipts bind the incident, seed, origin, target tool, and exact JSON input. Post action evidence must still satisfy the pack's recovery thresholds before an incident can resolve. We validate the product with 17 Vitest files / 69 tests covering domain commands, state transitions, pack validation, dynamic registration, stale tools, the approval invariant, imported-pack failures, deterministic reset, and UI behavior. Four Playwright regression journeys cover reset-to-resolved, multi-pack behavior, keyboard and viewport usability, and live-site receipts. Real local, deployed in-app-browser, installed-plugin, and Chrome WebMCP evidence is recorded separately from the test-only browser harness. The public AGPL-3.0 app is deployed on ChatGPT Sites. Challenges we ran into The hardest challenge was making human authority a runtime property not a disabled button. Rejecting an early apply call was not enough, the consequential tool had to be completely absent from discovery until the exact staged object had visible human approval, and old handles had to become stale as soon as state changed. The second challenge was cross origin honesty. Runbook Zero cannot claim that its page silently changed an unrelated website. We separated approval bound release from target site execution and verification, with an operator handoff fallback when the site exposes no applicable action. The third challenge was escaping a scenario specific demo without sacrificing a deterministic judging path. Generalizing the schema, UI, tool inputs, graph layout, thresholds, and recovery engine. Accomplishments that we're proud of WebMCP is the product's control plane. The same thirteen tool contracts operate against bundled, imported, and evidence derived live site incidents. The agent and human share one synchronized operational interface with explicit Agent, Human, System, and Change provenance. Pre-approval application is impossible through the registered WebMCP surface. Approval itself is human only. The Capability Firewall makes the transition from locked apply to exact approved apply visually obvious. New issues produce new evidence graphs instead of replaying a fixed demo. Live actions use exact origin/tool/input receipts and an honest handoff fallback. The canonical workflow resets deterministically and resolves through fixed recovery frames. The public app, source, AGPL-3.0 license, installable Codex plugin, tests, and judging evidence are reproducible from the repository. What we learned WebMCP capability discovery can also be policy. The safest consequential tool is not one that merely promises to reject unsafe input. It is a tool that does not exist until the surrounding human-visible state makes its use valid. We also learned that agent legibility matters as much as agent capability. When tool calls update the interface already in front of the operator, the human can see what the agent inspected, why it formed a hypothesis, what exact change it staged, and which authority boundary remains. Finally evidence topology is a far stronger bridge from demo to product than a library of hardcoded incident scripts. What's next for Runbook Zero Next we will validate more real sites, add capture adapters for richer browser and observability evidence, and expand the portable policy and receipt model. Production integrations will follow only where they can preserve the same boundary: read only observation, evidence backed diagnosis, exact staging, visible human approval, origin bound execution, independent verification, and auditable closure.
