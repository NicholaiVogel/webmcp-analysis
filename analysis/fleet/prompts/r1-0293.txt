Judge the delta WebMCP makes to reliability, precision, structured state access,
shared human-agent state, repeatability, and capability. Tool COUNT is not leverage.
- 9-10 TRANSFORMATIVE: the intended experience is impractical without the structured
  agent surface; human and agent share live product state; the collaboration could not
  be replicated comparably well by a general agent driving the UI.

  here; the agent is central to the product, not a bolt-on.
- 5-6 MEANINGFUL DELTA: existing capability packaged into a genuinely better product
  experience via structured tools; same outcome otherwise possible with real friction.
- 3-4 MINOR DELTA: generic browser automation achieves comparable outcomes; WebMCP is
  a convenience layer.
- 1-2 COSMETIC: exposes an action a UI button already offers; no meaningful change.

## Execution — is the actual product coherent, intentional, complete for its scope?
Stage 1 scores EXECUTION_CLAIMED: coherence/intentionality/completeness as evidenced by
the packet (description + packaging + video frames/transcript). Thin evidence caps the

sums.
- 9-10: all surfaces agree; central workflow demonstrably completes; states handled;
  feels finished for its stated scope.
- 7-8: strong coherence and intentionality; central workflow appears functional; minor
  unfinished edges.
- 5-6: plausibly real with visible gaps; scope unclear or core not demonstrated
  end-to-end.
- 3-4: thin prototype; contradictions between surfaces; placeholder content.
- 1-2: non-functional or incoherent for its claimed scope.

## Creativity & Ambition — novelty of concept and interaction model
Judge ONLY: novelty of the concept, novelty of the interaction model, originality
relative to existing concepts, ambition, memorable/surprising use of the medium, and
the depth with which the concept is pursued. WebMCP indispensability belongs under
WebMCP Leverage, NOT here: a project can be extremely creative even if another agent
API could theoretically have enabled something similar.
- 9-10: genuinely novel concept or interaction model; ambitious scope pursued deeply
  and coherently; memorable.
- 7-8: fresh synthesis or notably original interaction; ambitious for a hackathon.
- 5-6: competent take on a known category with a real new angle.
- 3-4: derivative; novelty mostly surface details.
- 1-2: clone/template with trivial changes.
"That the category exists" is never a penalty; judge the submitted concept.

## Potential Impact — credible value for the intended audience
Judge: is there a real audience; is there a real problem, desire, or use case for that

They lose it when a genuine enquiry gets buried, a follow-up is missed, or nobody realizes that a high-intent customer is still waiting. Most sales software focuses on acquiring, organizing, or generating leads. We wanted to explore a different question: What revenue has the business already paid to acquire — but quietly failed to recover? EnquiryOS is our answer: Revenue Incident Response for the Agentic Web. A Revenue Incident is a point where observed customer intent and the business response diverge in a way that may have caused recoverable loss. The product is deliberately not a generic CRM, chatbot, or autonomous sales agent. It is an evidence-backed recovery layer that lets a browser agent investigate what was missed while keeping consequential action under explicit human control. What it does EnquiryOS exposes five semantic WebMCP tools directly from the web application: audit_missed_enquiries inspect_enquiry_evidence rank_recovery_queue draft_recovery_message prepare_recovery Together they form a complete recovery workflow: Audit → Inspect evidence → Rank → Draft → Stage → Human commits An agent can ask EnquiryOS what the business may have missed, inspect the evidence behind a Revenue Incident, understand why one incident ranks above another, produce an evidence-grounded recovery draft, and stage it for approval. There is deliberately no agent-callable send tool . The authority boundary is: Observe → Investigate → Reason → Propose → Stage → Human commits This means the browser agent can do the investigative and preparatory work, but the final consequential action remains with the person operating the business. Why WebMCP matters Without WebMCP, an agent interacting with a web application generally has to infer intent from visual structure and operate through brittle UI actions. EnquiryOS instead exposes explicit semantic capabilities through document.modelContext . The agent does not need to guess that a particular button means "find missed enquiries" or scrape the page to understand the recovery queue. It can invoke a purpose-built tool such as audit_missed_enquiries , receive structured evidence, and immediately see the corresponding state reflected in the application's visible interface. The tool calls and the human interface share the same application state. Every WebMCP action also appears in a visible Live Trace, so the human can see what the agent has done. That creates a much stronger human-agent interaction model: the agent investigates, EnquiryOS provides the evidence, and the human retains authority. The demo The demo follows one Revenue Incident involving Ananya Sharma. Her incoming message asks about Saturday availability and package options. EnquiryOS identifies that there was no subsequent business reply and that the wait exceeded the service target. The incident is marked confirmed , with 97% evidence confidence , and receives a deterministic recovery score of 96/100 . Importantly, the score is not presented as an unexplained AI judgment. EnquiryOS shows the evidence signals that produced the priority: high-value enquiry; strong evidence confidence; an explicit availability question; no outbound reply; service-target breach. The agent can then generate a recovery draft grounded in that inspected evidence. Finally, prepare_recovery moves the incident into Awaiting approval . At that point the agent stops. The human sees the checkpoint and retains the only action capable of committing the recovery. How we built it EnquiryOS is a focused Next.js and React application written in TypeScript. The WebMCP integration uses native document.modelContext.registerTool registrations with structured input schemas, tool descriptions, and explicit behavioral boundaries. The five tools operate on a deterministic synthetic enquiry dataset so the judging experience is reproducible and the evidence chain can be inspected consistently. The interface contains three synchronized views: a Revenue Incident recovery queue; an evidence and recovery workspace; a Live Trace of agent tool activity. The recovery score is deterministic rather than generated by an opaque model. Drafts are also constrained by the evidence associated with the selected incident. PowerForge.Web generates and verifies the complete WebMCP contract. Codex invokes search_site and leaves matching results visible. One opt-in engine capability serves seven independent documentation sites. Inspiration Documentation search is usually designed for people clicking through a page. An agent must either imitate that interface, scrape the rendered HTML, or bypass the site's intended search experience entirely. Those paths are harder to validate and leave the person with little visibility into what the agent actually did. WebMCP provides a cleaner contract: the page can expose a small, typed capability that works inside the current document. We wanted that capability to be reusable across generated static sites rather than implemented as custom JavaScript for one showcase page. What it does On a participating site's search route, PowerForge.Web registers a read-only search_site Website Tool. The tool accepts a bounded query and result limit, searches the site's generated JSON index, and returns structured results containing titles, descriptions, collections, tags, languages, and URLs. The same invocation also updates the visible search page. The agent receives structured data while the person sees the corresponding query and result cards, can inspect the choices, refine the search, and open a result normally. Browsers without WebMCP continue to use the ordinary search page. The live judging target is OfficeIMO: Live application: https://officeimo.com/search/ Demo video: https://youtu.be/xIPtNwYBO58 Public source: https://github.com/EvotecIT/PSPublishModule Why WebMCP is a strong fit Search is a good first agent journey because it benefits from structure without requiring broad authority. WebMCP removes UI guessing, gives the agent an explicit schema, and keeps the action bound to the page the person is already viewing. The tool is read-only, the query is bounded, and the result remains visible. The improvement is not only faster retrieval. The person and agent now share one observable state: the agent can perform the structured search, and the person can immediately review the same results in the website's normal interface. How we built it PowerForge.Web is a .NET static-site engine. A site opts in through the engine's typed agent-readiness configuration and declares a route-scoped WebMCP tool. During generation, the engine: Builds the site's normal search route and JSON search index. Emits a same-origin WebMCP runtime. Registers search_site through the imperative document.modelContext.registerTool(...) API. Connects the tool to the visible search UI through a reusable adapter. Verifies the rendered route, executable script ordering, same-origin index and runtime, asset integrity, schema, and declared tool metadata. Advertises WebMCP readiness only when the complete generated artifact passes those checks. The runtime supports cancellation, bounded input, cached index loading, explicit cache invalidation, and theme-owned ranking through an adapter. The current shared implementation intentionally supports a read-only site-search journey; future write or product-specific actions will require separate authority and product contracts. What people and agents can do together Before this addition, a person could search the documentation, but an agent had no explicit contract for using that experience. It had to infer controls or search elsewhere. Now the person can open the OfficeIMO search page and ask Codex to find Word-to-PDF resources using the page's Website Tool. Codex invokes search_site , receives three structured matches, and leaves the OfficeIMO results visible. The person can verify the query, inspect the result cards, and continue browsing from the same page. Challenges we ran into Generated static sites create a different reliability problem from server-backed tools: configuration may look correct while the deployed page is missing its route, index, script, or fingerprinted asset. We therefore treated rendered output as the contract and added both local and remote readiness checks. We also needed to keep structured agent results synchronized with the theme's human-facing search behavior. PowerForge.Web provides a fallback search surface and an adapter that lets richer themes own ranking and rendering without replacing the shared WebMCP runtime.
