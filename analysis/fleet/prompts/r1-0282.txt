# FROZEN SCORING RUBRIC (pre-registered before any Stage 1 review)

Scores are integers 1-10 on each of the FOUR official criteria. Every score carries:
rationale, evidence_surfaces, confidence (0-1). Reviewers use the full scale and these
band anchors verbatim. Category neutrality is absolute: a game, art piece, music tool,
CRM, weird toy, and developer tool are all eligible for 10/10 on every criterion if they
satisfy that criterion exceptionally well FOR WHAT THEY ARE TRYING TO BE.
No aesthetic penalties. Signs of hurried AI work matter only as actual product-quality
problems (incoherence, broken flow, placeholder content), never as style.

## WebMCP Leverage — what does WebMCP change for this product?
Judge the delta WebMCP makes to reliability, precision, structured state access,
shared human-agent state, repeatability, and capability. Tool COUNT is not leverage.
- 9-10 TRANSFORMATIVE: the intended experience is impractical without the structured
  agent surface; human and agent share live product state; the collaboration could not
  be replicated comparably well by a general agent driving the UI.
- 7-8 MAJOR DELTA: without WebMCP a general agent is markedly less reliable/precise
  here; the agent is central to the product, not a bolt-on.
- 5-6 MEANINGFUL DELTA: existing capability packaged into a genuinely better product
  experience via structured tools; same outcome otherwise possible with real friction.
- 3-4 MINOR DELTA: generic browser automation achieves comparable outcomes; WebMCP is
  a convenience layer.
- 1-2 COSMETIC: exposes an action a UI button already offers; no meaningful change.

## Execution — is the actual product coherent, intentional, complete for its scope?
Stage 1 scores EXECUTION_CLAIMED: coherence/intentionality/completeness as evidenced by
the packet (description + packaging + video frames/transcript). Thin evidence caps the
score (end-to-end credit requires end-to-end evidence) and lowers confidence.
Stage 2 may rescore as EXECUTION_OBSERVED; latest evidence-backed score replaces, never
sums.
- 9-10: all surfaces agree; central workflow demonstrably completes; states handled;
  feels finished for its stated scope.
- 7-8: strong coherence and intentionality; central workflow appears functional; minor
  unfinished edges.
- 5-6: plausibly real with visible gaps; scope unclear or core not demonstrated
  end-to-end.
- 3-4: thin prototype; contradictions between surfaces; placeholder content.
- 1-2: non-functional or incoherent for its claimed scope.

## Creativity & Ambition — novelty of concept and interaction model
Judge ONLY: novelty of the concept, novelty of the interaction model, originality
relative to existing concepts, ambition, memorable/surprising use of the medium, and
the depth with which the concept is pursued. WebMCP indispensability belongs under
WebMCP Leverage, NOT here: a project can be extremely creative even if another agent
API could theoretically have enabled something similar.
- 9-10: genuinely novel concept or interaction model; ambitious scope pursued deeply
  and coherently; memorable.
- 7-8: fresh synthesis or notably original interaction; ambitious for a hackathon.
- 5-6: competent take on a known category with a real new angle.
- 3-4: derivative; novelty mostly surface details.
- 1-2: clone/template with trivial changes.
"That the category exists" is never a penalty; judge the submitted concept.

## Potential Impact — credible value for the intended audience
Judge: is there a real audience; is there a real problem, desire, or use case for that
audience; does the demonstrated solution genuinely address it. NO commercial adoption,
monetization, market pull, or startup viability is required. Entertainment, play, art,
creative expression, learning, niche hobby use, experimentation, and community value
are legitimate forms of impact. A tiny audience fully justifies a top score.
- 9-10: specific real audience with a real problem/desire; solution demonstrably
  addresses it; credible value, appeal, usefulness, or meaning for that audience.
- 7-8: clear audience and problem, good fit, credible value.
- 5-6: identifiable audience, looser fit or partial address.
- 3-4: speculative audience or strained fit; impact mostly aspirational.
- 1-2: no coherent audience/problem pairing.
Audience size is NOT a multiplier.

## Aggregate & ranking
aggregate = leverage + execution + impact + creativity (4-40). Nothing else enters.
Tie-breaks: Leverage, then Execution, then Impact, then Creativity, then higher
combined evidence confidence, then slug alphabetical. See PROTOCOL.md Ranking section.

Inspiration Travel recovery is a good stress test for WebMCP because it is multi-step, stateful, and involves consequential actions. The scenario behind Replan is simple: imagine a nonstop flight to Miami being canceled the day before a cruise departure from PortMiami. The traveler needs to recover quickly while balancing constraints such as timing, hotel availability, ground transportation, and budget. I wanted to explore what happens when the website exposes its capabilities directly to an AI agent instead of requiring a custom conversational layer inside the application. What it does Replan is an agentic travel recovery app where a traveler sets goals and constraints in natural language while an AI agent discovers and operates the site's capabilities directly. The traveler can ask the agent to search for replacement flights, update constraints, hold a reservation, confirm a booking, rebook a hotel, arrange ground transportation, notify a contact, track the running trip cost, and generate an itinerary summary. The app exposes 12 WebMCP tools: ping search_flights search_flights_semantic update_constraints hold_reservation confirm_booking search_hotels update_hotel_reservation book_ground_transport notify_contact calculate_total_cost generate_itinerary_summary The human remains in control of consequential actions through explicit confirmation gates, while the agent handles the multi-step coordination. How we built it Replan is built with React, Vite, and TypeScript, deployed on Vercel. Convex powers the one live backend piece—the semantic-search vector index, reached from the browser tool through an HTTP endpoint, so no Convex client code ships in the runtime bundle. The key integration is WebMCP. The page registers its capabilities through the WebMCP API using document.modelContext.registerTool() . ChatGPT, in its in-app browser, discovers those tools and calls them from natural-language requests. There is no custom LLM, NLU, voice layer, or custom agent in the Replan codebase. The application exposes structured capabilities and lets the agent decide how to compose them. The 12 tools are registered statically at page load. Each tool validates its inputs and returns structured JSON, including structured errors that allow the agent to understand failed operations and recover. One of the tools, search_flights_semantic , uses natural-language semantic ranking backed by a live vector index with embeddings rather than simple keyword matching. I verified the complete interaction flow manually: 12/12 tools registered, 36/36 demo turns passed, 0 failures . Challenges we ran into One of the main challenges was designing tool validation and error responses so that an AI agent could recover from invalid or conflicting requests. Structured errors turned out to be especially important because the agent needs actionable information rather than only human-readable error messages. Another challenge was designing confirmation boundaries. Search and other reversible operations can be performed speculatively, but consequential actions such as confirming a booking need an explicit human confirmation step. The final challenge was compressing a 36-turn recovery workflow into a clear demonstration under three minutes without losing the important WebMCP interactions. Accomplishments that we're proud of I successfully exposed the complete travel recovery workflow through WebMCP without building a custom agent or natural-language processing layer. The final implementation has 12 WebMCP tools , and the complete human verification covered 36/36 turns with 0 failures . Most importantly, the entire recovery chain can be driven from natural language: the agent discovers the capabilities exposed by the webpage, composes multiple tools, handles state and constraints, and stops for human confirmation when an action becomes consequential. What we learned WebMCP changes the design boundary between a website and an AI agent. The website does not need to understand how the agent reasons. It needs to expose clear, reliable capabilities that an agent can discover and compose. I also learned that confirmation gates are not just a UI concern. They shape the tool design itself by separating safe, reversible operations from consequential actions. Finally, structured JSON responses and meaningful errors are critical when tools are being operated by an agent. They provide the information needed for the agent to adapt instead of simply failing. Potential extensions include partial availability, multi-leg itineraries, more complex constraint conflicts, and broader use of live vector search across additional travel capabilities. The goal is to continue exploring how far a website can go when it exposes reliable, composable capabilities directly to an AI agent through WebMCP. Before Inspiration Websites are built for the average user, but everyone has different preferences, accessibility needs, and workflows. We wanted users to ask their existing AI agent to adapt any webpage for them, even when they do not own the website. What it does Perso-XXL is a browser extension that lets AI agents safely personalize webpages. Agents can change styles, hide or move elements, add safe interface components, create shortcuts, and remember the user's preferences. Changes are local, reversible, and saved for future visits. With WebMCP, capable agents use Perso-XXL directly. OpenRouter is only a fallback for users without a compatible agent. How we built it Perso-XXL exposes WebMCP tools such as inspect_page and apply_page_plan . The agent first receives short-lived, opaque target IDs. It then submits a structured modification plan. Perso-XXL validates the plan, checks that targets are still fresh, and applies it atomically. The extension uses a secure bridge between the page and extension environments. It also supports confirmation, cancellation, rollback, persistent modifications, and dynamically registered shortcuts. Challenges we ran into The main challenge was giving agents useful control without allowing arbitrary JavaScript, HTML, CSS, or selectors. Modern pages also change constantly, so inspected elements can quickly become stale. We addressed this with one-use snapshots, fingerprints, navigation checks, and target revalidation. We also had to support both normal Chrome extensions and built-in agent browsers, which expose WebMCP differently. Accomplishments that we're proud of We are proud that Perso-XXL: Works on websites the user does not own. Lets existing agents personalize pages without another AI call. Rejects unsafe and stale modifications. Supports reversible and persistent changes. Turns saved workflows into callable WebMCP tools. Gives agents access to the user's style preferences. Includes automated browser, security, rollback, and adversarial tests. What we learned WebMCP is most useful when tools expose meaningful capabilities instead of raw DOM access. We also learned that data and presentation should be separate. A website can securely provide information, while the user and agent decide how to display it. Finally, reversibility is essential. Agents are more useful when users can safely experiment, inspect the result, and undo it. What's next for Perso-XXL Next, we want to: Move the OpenRouter fallback to a secure backend. Add encrypted preference synchronization. Improve accessibility-focused personalization. Expand the safe modification capabilities. Improve previews and explanations before applying changes. Support more browsers as WebMCP becomes widely available. Our long-term goal is to make web personalization belong to the user.
