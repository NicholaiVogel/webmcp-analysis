# FROZEN SCORING RUBRIC (pre-registered before any Stage 1 review)

Scores are integers 1-10 on each of the FOUR official criteria. Every score carries:
rationale, evidence_surfaces, confidence (0-1). Reviewers use the full scale and these
band anchors verbatim. Category neutrality is absolute: a game, art piece, music tool,
CRM, weird toy, and developer tool are all eligible for 10/10 on every criterion if they
satisfy that criterion exceptionally well FOR WHAT THEY ARE TRYING TO BE.
No aesthetic penalties. Signs of hurried AI work matter only as actual product-quality
problems (incoherence, broken flow, placeholder content), never as style.

## WebMCP Leverage — what does WebMCP change for this product?
Judge the delta WebMCP makes to reliability, precision, structured state access,
shared human-agent state, repeatability, and capability. Tool COUNT is not leverage.
- 9-10 TRANSFORMATIVE: the intended experience is impractical without the structured
  agent surface; human and agent share live product state; the collaboration could not
  be replicated comparably well by a general agent driving the UI.
- 7-8 MAJOR DELTA: without WebMCP a general agent is markedly less reliable/precise
  here; the agent is central to the product, not a bolt-on.
- 5-6 MEANINGFUL DELTA: existing capability packaged into a genuinely better product
  experience via structured tools; same outcome otherwise possible with real friction.
- 3-4 MINOR DELTA: generic browser automation achieves comparable outcomes; WebMCP is
  a convenience layer.
- 1-2 COSMETIC: exposes an action a UI button already offers; no meaningful change.

## Execution — is the actual product coherent, intentional, complete for its scope?
Stage 1 scores EXECUTION_CLAIMED: coherence/intentionality/completeness as evidenced by
the packet (description + packaging + video frames/transcript). Thin evidence caps the
score (end-to-end credit requires end-to-end evidence) and lowers confidence.
Stage 2 may rescore as EXECUTION_OBSERVED; latest evidence-backed score replaces, never
sums.
- 9-10: all surfaces agree; central workflow demonstrably completes; states handled;
  feels finished for its stated scope.
- 7-8: strong coherence and intentionality; central workflow appears functional; minor
  unfinished edges.
- 5-6: plausibly real with visible gaps; scope unclear or core not demonstrated
  end-to-end.
- 3-4: thin prototype; contradictions between surfaces; placeholder content.
- 1-2: non-functional or incoherent for its claimed scope.

## Creativity & Ambition — novelty of concept and interaction model
Judge ONLY: novelty of the concept, novelty of the interaction model, originality
relative to existing concepts, ambition, memorable/surprising use of the medium, and
the depth with which the concept is pursued. WebMCP indispensability belongs under
WebMCP Leverage, NOT here: a project can be extremely creative even if another agent
API could theoretically have enabled something similar.
- 9-10: genuinely novel concept or interaction model; ambitious scope pursued deeply
  and coherently; memorable.
- 7-8: fresh synthesis or notably original interaction; ambitious for a hackathon.
- 5-6: competent take on a known category with a real new angle.
- 3-4: derivative; novelty mostly surface details.
- 1-2: clone/template with trivial changes.
"That the category exists" is never a penalty; judge the submitted concept.

## Potential Impact — credible value for the intended audience
Judge: is there a real audience; is there a real problem, desire, or use case for that
audience; does the demonstrated solution genuinely address it. NO commercial adoption,
monetization, market pull, or startup viability is required. Entertainment, play, art,
creative expression, learning, niche hobby use, experimentation, and community value
are legitimate forms of impact. A tiny audience fully justifies a top score.
- 9-10: specific real audience with a real problem/desire; solution demonstrably
  addresses it; credible value, appeal, usefulness, or meaning for that audience.
- 7-8: clear audience and problem, good fit, credible value.
- 5-6: identifiable audience, looser fit or partial address.
- 3-4: speculative audience or strained fit; impact mostly aspirational.
- 1-2: no coherent audience/problem pairing.
Audience size is NOT a multiplier.

## Aggregate & ranking
aggregate = leverage + execution + impact + creativity (4-40). Nothing else enters.
Tie-breaks: Leverage, then Execution, then Impact, then Creativity, then higher
combined evidence confidence, then slug alphabetical. See PROTOCOL.md Ranking section.

Your agent researches. You decide what becomes canonical. Like Comment Story Updates A contested claim becomes an inspectable graph; three agent proposals wait for human review. Nine dated source records preserve provenance before synthesis. Two proposed groups and one atomic verdict stay staged—not canonical—until reviewed. What Threadline is Threadline is a provenance-preserving argument debugger for contested online claims. A browser agent reads the same investigation a person is reviewing and uses eight in-page WebMCP tools to stage sources, atomic claims, typed relations, non-destructive groups, and a bounded verdict. Agent-authored proposals are visibly noncanonical until a person accepts them. If a person rejects a proposal with a reason, that rationale returns through get_workspace so a later agent call can revise its approach. The result is not another feed summary. It is an inspectable collaboration in which the agent does the expensive work of researching and structuring an argument, while the person retains authority over what becomes durable truth. The problem Fast-moving technical discussion on X and the wider web flattens primary evidence, repetition, interpretation, prediction, and contradiction into one stream. A claim can look independently corroborated simply because many posts repeat the same underlying source. An autonomous summary hides those dependencies and can quietly become the next person's “fact.” Threadline makes the evidence lineage visible. It separates what a source directly establishes from what people infer, predict, amplify, or dispute. What people and agents do together The agent can: inspect bounded canonical workspace state and deterministic structural analysis; inspect full source evidence inside explicit untrusted-data boundaries; research and stage public sources it actually accessed; propose atomic claims and typed supports , contradicts , qualifies , or amplifies relations; propose non-destructive groups for repeated claims; propose one bounded verdict and its evidence relations as an atomic patch; and focus the visible graph on unresolved or primary-evidence work. The person can: inspect every source trail and staged graph entity; accept a defensible proposal; reject a weak proposal with a recorded rationale; decide which verdict becomes canonical; reopen saved investigations after reload; and export portable JSON or a Markdown evidence brief with the decision receipt. Why WebMCP is essential Threadline registers eight imperative tools with document.modelContext.registerTool : Tool Purpose Authority get_workspace Compact canonical state, structural analysis, recent human decisions, and rejection rationale Read only inspect_sources Full evidence records inside explicit untrusted-data delimiters Read only stage_sources Propose public evidence the agent actually accessed Review queue only stage_claims Propose small evidence-grounded claims Review queue only stage_relations Propose typed claim-to-claim relations Review queue only stage_merge Propose non-destructive repeated-claim groups Review queue only stage_verdict Propose one verdict and its relations atomically Review queue only set_focus Change reversible presentation focus Presentation only This is more than “chat with my site”: The registered tool surface enforces proposal-only authority. There is no registered accept, reject, reset, import, delete, rename, or case-switch tool. Acceptance is available only through the person-facing UI. This is a capability boundary, not authenticated proof of personhood. The agent and person share one live object. Stable source, claim, relation, group, proposal, and case IDs refer to the exact graph on screen. The agent does not reconstruct graph meaning from pixels. Optimistic concurrency prevents silent cross-talk. Every non-discovery call requires expectedCaseId ; every staging call also requires expectedRevision . A stale staging call or cross-case call returns structured recovery output before any proposal or canonical mutation. Rejection becomes protocol feedback. get_workspace exposes up to eight recent human decisions, including rationale, for later agent calls. Schemas and trust boundaries are explicit. Inputs are closed and bounded. Read tools carry read-only and untrusted-content hints, and full third-party evidence is returned only inside named untrusted-data delimiters. Staging is idempotent. Equivalent proposals reuse a stable ID, and pending entity IDs are reserved so competing proposals cannot present different content under one identity. Because the tools live in the page, Threadline needs no backend, database, account, API key, or bundled AI runtime. A complete product, not a proof of concept The primary interface is a responsive, keyboard-accessible provenance graph and review queue—not a chatbot. Pending graph entities are visibly marked STAGED — NOT CANONICAL and open their owning proposal. Amplification is first-class: repeated claims can be grouped without deleting original wording, relations, or sources. A verdict is an atomic patch. Its conclusion and evidence relations enter canonical state together, while only direct claim-to-verdict edges become its basis receipt. Up to eight investigations can be created, opened, renamed, reset, removed, imported, and exported. When exclusive Web Locks are available, cases labeled Saved survive reload. Otherwise they remain clearly labeled Memory only and exportable before reload. Screenshot transcriptions are explicitly labeled human-supplied evidence; Threadline never fabricates a public URL or treats an attributed statement as proof of its underlying claim. The deterministic preview is visibly labeled as a checked-in fixture replay, never as a live model response. Threadline uses dependency-free browser ES modules, semantic HTML, CSS, functional SVG, and Node's built-in test runner. It is hosted on ChatGPT Sites and released under the MIT License. The bundled challenge case The main investigation asks: Will WebMCP replace browser automation? Nine dated public records include Chrome documentation, the WebMCP explainer, a pinned WindTunnel benchmark result, and WebKit and Mozilla standards-position records. The graph separates a large demonstrated efficiency advantage on instrumented sites from task-level parity, explicit fallback guidance, and unresolved cross-browser implementation. The evidence supports a narrower verdict than the feed does: WebMCP is a semantic fast lane on instrumented sites, not a replacement for browser automation. The walkthrough deliberately stages one defensible group, one weak group, and one atomic verdict. The person accepts the first, rejects “Adoption and fallback” with the rationale “Same topic, different claim,” inspects the verdict evidence, and accepts the verdict. The unresolved standards claim remains visible after resolution.
