# FROZEN SCORING RUBRIC (pre-registered before any Stage 1 review)

Scores are integers 1-10 on each of the FOUR official criteria. Every score carries:
rationale, evidence_surfaces, confidence (0-1). Reviewers use the full scale and these
band anchors verbatim. Category neutrality is absolute: a game, art piece, music tool,
CRM, weird toy, and developer tool are all eligible for 10/10 on every criterion if they
satisfy that criterion exceptionally well FOR WHAT THEY ARE TRYING TO BE.
No aesthetic penalties. Signs of hurried AI work matter only as actual product-quality
problems (incoherence, broken flow, placeholder content), never as style.

## WebMCP Leverage — what does WebMCP change for this product?
Judge the delta WebMCP makes to reliability, precision, structured state access,
shared human-agent state, repeatability, and capability. Tool COUNT is not leverage.
- 9-10 TRANSFORMATIVE: the intended experience is impractical without the structured
  agent surface; human and agent share live product state; the collaboration could not
  be replicated comparably well by a general agent driving the UI.
- 7-8 MAJOR DELTA: without WebMCP a general agent is markedly less reliable/precise
  here; the agent is central to the product, not a bolt-on.
- 5-6 MEANINGFUL DELTA: existing capability packaged into a genuinely better product
  experience via structured tools; same outcome otherwise possible with real friction.
- 3-4 MINOR DELTA: generic browser automation achieves comparable outcomes; WebMCP is
  a convenience layer.
- 1-2 COSMETIC: exposes an action a UI button already offers; no meaningful change.

## Execution — is the actual product coherent, intentional, complete for its scope?
Stage 1 scores EXECUTION_CLAIMED: coherence/intentionality/completeness as evidenced by
the packet (description + packaging + video frames/transcript). Thin evidence caps the
score (end-to-end credit requires end-to-end evidence) and lowers confidence.
Stage 2 may rescore as EXECUTION_OBSERVED; latest evidence-backed score replaces, never
sums.
- 9-10: all surfaces agree; central workflow demonstrably completes; states handled;
  feels finished for its stated scope.
- 7-8: strong coherence and intentionality; central workflow appears functional; minor
  unfinished edges.
- 5-6: plausibly real with visible gaps; scope unclear or core not demonstrated
  end-to-end.
- 3-4: thin prototype; contradictions between surfaces; placeholder content.
- 1-2: non-functional or incoherent for its claimed scope.

## Creativity & Ambition — novelty of concept and interaction model
Judge ONLY: novelty of the concept, novelty of the interaction model, originality
relative to existing concepts, ambition, memorable/surprising use of the medium, and
the depth with which the concept is pursued. WebMCP indispensability belongs under
WebMCP Leverage, NOT here: a project can be extremely creative even if another agent
API could theoretically have enabled something similar.
- 9-10: genuinely novel concept or interaction model; ambitious scope pursued deeply
  and coherently; memorable.
- 7-8: fresh synthesis or notably original interaction; ambitious for a hackathon.
- 5-6: competent take on a known category with a real new angle.
- 3-4: derivative; novelty mostly surface details.
- 1-2: clone/template with trivial changes.
"That the category exists" is never a penalty; judge the submitted concept.

## Potential Impact — credible value for the intended audience
Judge: is there a real audience; is there a real problem, desire, or use case for that
audience; does the demonstrated solution genuinely address it. NO commercial adoption,
monetization, market pull, or startup viability is required. Entertainment, play, art,
creative expression, learning, niche hobby use, experimentation, and community value
are legitimate forms of impact. A tiny audience fully justifies a top score.
- 9-10: specific real audience with a real problem/desire; solution demonstrably
  addresses it; credible value, appeal, usefulness, or meaning for that audience.
- 7-8: clear audience and problem, good fit, credible value.
- 5-6: identifiable audience, looser fit or partial address.
- 3-4: speculative audience or strained fit; impact mostly aspirational.
- 1-2: no coherent audience/problem pairing.
Audience size is NOT a multiplier.

## Aggregate & ranking
aggregate = leverage + execution + impact + creativity (4-40). Nothing else enters.
Tie-breaks: Leverage, then Execution, then Impact, then Creativity, then higher
combined evidence confidence, then slug alphabetical. See PROTOCOL.md Ranking section.

A bilingual family memory canvas where people and AI reconstruct history from stories, photos, and documents—keeping sources, uncertainty, and contradictions visible Like Comment Story Updates ## Inspiration Family history is often built from incomplete memories: one person remembers a year, another remembers a place, and an old photograph suggests something different. Most software tries to resolve these differences into one clean answer. Memoir Canvas takes the opposite approach. It makes uncertainty visible and gives the final decision back to the family. Every memory appears as a firefly in a moonlit meadow. Green means the story has been told, orange means sources conflict, and white means there is an empty place waiting for someone to contribute. Opening a memory reveals a book-like page where recollections and evidence remain visible together. The project was inspired by a simple principle: An AI must not turn a guess into a fact. ## What I learned The most important lesson was that trustworthy AI behavior cannot depend only on instructions or prompts. If the system truly must not invent certainty, that rule needs to exist below the model. Memoir Canvas uses database constraints, PostgreSQL roles, deferred constraint triggers, and a shared command layer to enforce this boundary. An agent can add memories, cite sources, detect conflicts, and propose follow-up questions. It cannot resolve a contradiction by itself. I also learned that uncertainty can be part of the user experience rather than something hidden in metadata. The open conflict becomes a physical tear in the page, and the book refuses to close until a person resolves it. The interface makes the epistemic state understandable without requiring the user to inspect a database. ## How I built it Memoir Canvas is a local-first Vite and React application. A real PostgreSQL-compatible database runs inside the browser through PGlite and is persisted in IndexedDB. No application server, account, or runtime API is required. The architecture has one write door: Human controls and agent tools both call src/domain/commands.ts . Only the command layer writes to the database. Every write and audit event happens in the same transaction. The view renders a read-only projection from the store. WebMCP tools are generated as a pure function of the current UI state. The app exposes eight WebMCP tools: read_memory_graph , add_person , add_memory_claim , link_claim_to_source , flag_conflict , propose_followup_question , resolve_claim , and generate_story_card . The tool list changes depending on what the person is viewing. In particular, resolve_claim only becomes available when a human has opened a specific conflict. Even then, an agent calling it is refused by the database because only a human can provide the confirmation. The central demonstration follows one family story: An oral account says Grandma moved to Đà Nẵng around 1972. A photograph is dated 1974. The sources are linked as contradictory. The page tears and cannot turn. The agent proposes a follow-up question. A family member opens the conflict and confirms the correct year. The tear heals and the story card carries the weakest certainty of the claims it contains. Story cards are bilingual, with Vietnamese and English content, so the archive remains useful to the family while still being understandable to English-speaking judges. ## Challenges The hardest challenge was preserving the same safety guarantees across both human and agent interaction. It would have been easy to give the agent a separate shortcut, but that would make the human-facing safeguards meaningless. Both paths now use the same command functions and database constraints. Another challenge was representing conflict visually without making the interface feel like a dashboard. The project moved from a conventional panel layout to a book and meadow metaphor: pages turn by gesture, conflicts split the book, and memories appear as lights in the landscape. Browser portability was also important. The app must work in embedded webviews where WebGL or WebMCP may be unavailable. The forest has a CSS fallback, the photographs are processed locally through EXIF metadata, and the core interactions remain accessible through keyboard navigation. We wanted the agent to be genuinely useful without allowing it to silently turn its own recommendation into an irreversible action. \n\n We also had to make the experience work across WebMCP-capable environments while honestly handling browsers where WebMCP is unavailable. \n\n Accomplishments that we're proud of \n\n We're proud that RelayPay demonstrates a complete agent → tools → reasoning → human approval → action → verification loop rather than just a chatbot calling an API. \n\n The agent can investigate a real-looking refund exception through multiple WebMCP tools, build a recovery proposal, and reach a clear human decision point. \n\n We're especially proud of the separation between preparation and execution. The agent can do the investigative work, but the application—not the agent—enforces the final approval boundary. \n\n We also built an execution trace so the interaction is observable rather than hidden behind a black box. \n\n What we learned \n\n We learned that building an agent-native application requires designing around capabilities and boundaries, not just adding AI to an existing interface. \n\n WebMCP made us think of the browser itself as an agent-accessible application surface. Tool descriptions, schemas, permissions, state transitions, and failure cases all become part of the product design. \n\n Most importantly, we learned that human-agent collaboration works best when the agent handles the tedious investigation and preparation while the human retains authority over consequential decisions. \n\n What's next for RelayPay \n\n The next step is connecting RelayPay to real commerce and payment infrastructure so the same workflow can operate on live refund data. \n\n We want to add richer evidence collection, policy-aware recovery recommendations, merchant-side integrations, audit trails, and stronger verification. \n\n Longer term, RelayPay could evolve from a refund exception tool into an agent-native operations layer for commerce, where humans set the boundaries and agents handle the repetitive work inside them. \n\n \n\n \n Built With \n\n css fiber next.js react tailwind three three.js typescript vercel webmcp \n \n\n \n Try it out \n\n \n \n \n GitHub Repo \n \n \n \n relaypay.vercel.app \n \n \n \n\n \n\n \n\n \n \n \n Created by\n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n Bangaru Murali Manoj kumar \n \n \n\n \n\n \n \n \n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n\n \n\n\n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n Like\n \n \n\n\n \n \n \n\n \n \n\n \n\n\n \n \n \n \n Share this project:\n \n\n\n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n\n \n \n \n \n\n \n \n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n\n\n\n \n \n \n Updates \n\n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n \n Bangaru Murali Manoj kumar \n started this project\n \n —\n \n a day ago \n \n \n\n
