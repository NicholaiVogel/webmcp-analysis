# FROZEN SCORING RUBRIC (pre-registered before any Stage 1 review)

Scores are integers 1-10 on each of the FOUR official criteria. Every score carries:
rationale, evidence_surfaces, confidence (0-1). Reviewers use the full scale and these
band anchors verbatim. Category neutrality is absolute: a game, art piece, music tool,
CRM, weird toy, and developer tool are all eligible for 10/10 on every criterion if they
satisfy that criterion exceptionally well FOR WHAT THEY ARE TRYING TO BE.
No aesthetic penalties. Signs of hurried AI work matter only as actual product-quality
problems (incoherence, broken flow, placeholder content), never as style.

## WebMCP Leverage — what does WebMCP change for this product?
Judge the delta WebMCP makes to reliability, precision, structured state access,
shared human-agent state, repeatability, and capability. Tool COUNT is not leverage.
- 9-10 TRANSFORMATIVE: the intended experience is impractical without the structured
  agent surface; human and agent share live product state; the collaboration could not
  be replicated comparably well by a general agent driving the UI.
- 7-8 MAJOR DELTA: without WebMCP a general agent is markedly less reliable/precise
  here; the agent is central to the product, not a bolt-on.
- 5-6 MEANINGFUL DELTA: existing capability packaged into a genuinely better product
  experience via structured tools; same outcome otherwise possible with real friction.
- 3-4 MINOR DELTA: generic browser automation achieves comparable outcomes; WebMCP is
  a convenience layer.
- 1-2 COSMETIC: exposes an action a UI button already offers; no meaningful change.

## Execution — is the actual product coherent, intentional, complete for its scope?
Stage 1 scores EXECUTION_CLAIMED: coherence/intentionality/completeness as evidenced by
the packet (description + packaging + video frames/transcript). Thin evidence caps the
score (end-to-end credit requires end-to-end evidence) and lowers confidence.
Stage 2 may rescore as EXECUTION_OBSERVED; latest evidence-backed score replaces, never
sums.
- 9-10: all surfaces agree; central workflow demonstrably completes; states handled;
  feels finished for its stated scope.
- 7-8: strong coherence and intentionality; central workflow appears functional; minor
  unfinished edges.
- 5-6: plausibly real with visible gaps; scope unclear or core not demonstrated
  end-to-end.
- 3-4: thin prototype; contradictions between surfaces; placeholder content.
- 1-2: non-functional or incoherent for its claimed scope.

## Creativity & Ambition — novelty of concept and interaction model
Judge ONLY: novelty of the concept, novelty of the interaction model, originality
relative to existing concepts, ambition, memorable/surprising use of the medium, and
the depth with which the concept is pursued. WebMCP indispensability belongs under
WebMCP Leverage, NOT here: a project can be extremely creative even if another agent
API could theoretically have enabled something similar.
- 9-10: genuinely novel concept or interaction model; ambitious scope pursued deeply
  and coherently; memorable.
- 7-8: fresh synthesis or notably original interaction; ambitious for a hackathon.
- 5-6: competent take on a known category with a real new angle.
- 3-4: derivative; novelty mostly surface details.
- 1-2: clone/template with trivial changes.
"That the category exists" is never a penalty; judge the submitted concept.

## Potential Impact — credible value for the intended audience
Judge: is there a real audience; is there a real problem, desire, or use case for that
audience; does the demonstrated solution genuinely address it. NO commercial adoption,
monetization, market pull, or startup viability is required. Entertainment, play, art,
creative expression, learning, niche hobby use, experimentation, and community value
are legitimate forms of impact. A tiny audience fully justifies a top score.
- 9-10: specific real audience with a real problem/desire; solution demonstrably
  addresses it; credible value, appeal, usefulness, or meaning for that audience.
- 7-8: clear audience and problem, good fit, credible value.
- 5-6: identifiable audience, looser fit or partial address.
- 3-4: speculative audience or strained fit; impact mostly aspirational.
- 1-2: no coherent audience/problem pairing.
Audience size is NOT a multiplier.

## Aggregate & ranking
aggregate = leverage + execution + impact + creativity (4-40). Nothing else enters.
Tie-breaks: Leverage, then Execution, then Impact, then Creativity, then higher
combined evidence confidence, then slug alphabetical. See PROTOCOL.md Ranking section.

The interesting part for me is that the user owns the AI agent . A business website does not have to create its own assistant, replace its existing workflow or force visitors to learn a new interface. Instead, it can expose a small number of safe, useful capabilities that a person's chosen agent can understand and use. Clip Magic is a real small-business website, so I deliberately chose a practical task for this challenge: preparing a website-review enquiry. I chose a pragmatic approach rather than a dramatic one. The problem is familiar: people often know what they need, but do not know which service label to choose or how much detail to type into a set of fields. For some users, especially those who find dense, field-by-field interfaces difficult, a conversational route can be useful. It is not a replacement for accessible form design, and it does not remove human choice. The ordinary form remains available; the agent simply helps a user reach the same workflow and leaves the final decision with them. Most people I know would rather explain what they need than fill in boxes on screen or on paper. The opportunity extends well beyond one website. Small and medium-sized enterprises represent around 90% of businesses worldwide , yet reliable data about the proportion of websites they operate is not generally available. What is clear is that there is already a large installed base of small-business websites: in 2025, 76.67% of EU small enterprises with 10–49 employees reported having a website ( Eurostat e-business data ), while 17% reported using AI, compared with 55% of large enterprises ( Eurostat AI statistics ). During testing, Chrome's WebMCP client could discover the declarative tool, but ChatGPT's in-app browser did not. The bridge derives its name, description, parameters and required fields from the rendered declarative form, so it provides compatibility without becoming a second independent schema. WebMCP tools are discovered at the document level, so the capability is available as soon as the page loads. The Contact form remains the existing destination for the agent's prepared data, and the existing FormBuilder workflow remains unchanged. The capability is not tied to the Contact dialog's open or closed state. The dialog can be opened and closed normally without changing WebMCP availability; when the agent uses the capability, it opens the existing Contact workflow and populates the visible form. The live implementation was tested in both ChatGPT's in-app browser and Chrome with WebMCP enabled. Challenges The biggest challenge was not creating the tool. It was making WebMCP coexist safely with a real website that already had years of working behaviour and production constraints. Working with an existing form The challenge was to add an agent-facing capability without rebuilding the Contact system or duplicating its business rules. The solution was to keep the WebMCP layer close to the existing visible form while leaving the shared dialog system and FormBuilder workflow intact. Different WebMCP client behaviour Declarative WebMCP worked in Chrome testing but was not discovered by ChatGPT's in-app browser during development. Rather than abandon the declarative implementation, I kept it as the primary definition and added the small compatibility bridge. This let both clients exercise the same capability without duplicating the schema manually. The useful boundary for this project is that the agent can do the repetitive preparation work, but the person sees the actual values in the real form and remains responsible for submission. The workflow was tested for cancellation, required-field validation, stale tool reuse, rapid double-submission, unexpected parameters, honeypot handling, email-header injection, unsafe URL schemes, prompt-injection text, session/token behaviour and controlled network failure. What I learned The most useful lesson was that WebMCP does not have to mean rebuilding a website for agents. For an existing business site, the better approach may be to identify a useful customer outcome, find the authoritative workflow that already handles it, and expose the smallest capability needed to help the user's agent reach that workflow reliably. I also learned that progressive enhancement matters just as much for agents as it does for browsers . The human path should not become dependent on WebMCP. The WebMCP path should not gain privileges the human path does not have. Both should meet at the same validation, security and business rules. Finally, testing natural-language requests reinforced an important distinction: the website does not need to encode every sales or qualification decision as another tool.

Evidence Desk lets agents investigate disputed evidence through four bounded WebMCP tools while humans keep the verdict—and preserves the investigation and human decision in a tamper-evident receipt. Like 1 Comment Story Updates Inspiration AI can read a packet in seconds. The hard part is showing a person exactly what it used—without quietly turning the agent into the decision-maker. Evidence-heavy procurement, compliance, audit, and dispute decisions need agent speed, but they also need human accountability and a reviewable chain of evidence. What it does Evidence Desk gives an agent four bounded WebMCP tools over a signed synthetic packet: search_evidence , inspect_exhibit , evaluate_claim , and update_caseboard . The agent searches thirteen exhibits, inspects hash-linked source spans, evaluates a contested claim, and proposes evidence to a shared caseboard. The demo makes one contradiction visible: the packet records acceptance on March 14, while the signed inspection report says inspection occurred March 19. The evaluator returns CONTRADICTED with two reasons tied to exact spans. Then the agent stops. It cannot pin or remove evidence, reject a proposal, approve the verdict, or seal the receipt. Those decisions remain human-only. How we built it The TypeScript and Vite app registers four tools with awaited document.modelContext.registerTool(...) calls. Inputs are validated at runtime, outputs are bounded JSON strings, and corpus text is treated as untrusted. Real tool executions emit visible started and completed lifecycle events in the provenance rail. An Ed25519-signed manifest and per-span SHA-256 checks quarantine altered evidence before evaluation. After human adjudication, Evidence Desk creates a readable, locally signed receipt binding the tool trail, evidence, final human decision, and signed build manifest. The app runs entirely client-side on synthetic data, and its runtime application logic makes no network requests. Challenges we ran into The central engineering challenge was separating capability from authority. Registration is not execution, simulation is not live provenance, a proposal is not approval, and a structurally valid receipt is not meaningful if it seals zero accepted evidence. We hardened those boundaries: simulation-only sessions stay labeled SIM ; untrusted receipt entries fail verification instead of throwing; the evaluator accepts common date-and-event word orders while rejecting unrelated or impossible dates; long reason lists disclose their full meaning; and sealing refuses an empty evidence anchor. Accomplishments that we're proud of All four production tools executed live on the deployed page. Every call showed its started and completed lifecycle events. The evaluator produced a span-tied CONTRADICTED verdict, while the agent remained unable to perform the human-only actions. The Forgery Bench makes the trust boundary testable: change one signed date byte and the manifest check quarantines the exhibit, withdraws the verdict, and refuses sealing. The implementation closes with 189 passing tests, typecheck, and a production build. What we learned WebMCP is most valuable here not as UI automation, but as a semantic contract between a person, an agent, and shared evidence. The agent becomes faster and more precise without inheriting the person's authority. Visible provenance and negative controls matter as much as the happy path. What's next for Evidence Desk The next step is moving from the synthetic packet to user-supplied signed evidence bundles, durable organizational signing keys, trusted timestamps, and configurable reviewer roles—while preserving the same human-only adjudication boundary. The current local receipt is tamper-evident, not tamper-proof. It does not prove identity or trusted time and is not a legal signature. Agent proposes. Human decides. Evidence preserves what happened. An agent-native election evidence layer where WebMCP exposes published sources, visible gaps, and human-in-the-loop verification directly in the browser. Just in time for the Swedish election 2026. Like Comment Story Updates Utlovat.se WebMCP landing page explaining that agents can sort Swedish election evidence but cannot recommend a political party. tlovat.se research brief for healthcare, showing a recorded unclear position for Moderates, 12 evidence records for Social Democrats, Evidence cards in an Utlovat.se research brief, each with a Swedish quote, publication date, four-year cost range, source link, archive copy Inspiration During democratic elections, voters are inundated with conflicting claims, manifesto promises, and shifting party positions. When citizens in general, and first time voters in particular, turn to AI assistants for guidance, your average, I have and answer for everything, general-purpose LLMs present a dangerous hazard: they risk hallucinating plausible quotes, confusing past election cycles with current platforms, and inventing policy stances where parties are actually silent . In high-stakes civic decision-making, an AI summary is not enough. Citizens need evidence, not a verdict. And to be encouraged to question the feasibility of the "five minutes to Election Day" promises. utlovat.se (meaning "As Promised" in Swedish) is aims to be an independent, non-partisan election platform tracking over 2,000 promises and 700+ reviewed parliamentary actions for the 2026 general election. We (actually there is only me and my assisting agents) were inspired to build on OpenAI's WebMCP because it offers the missing piece of the agentic web: a standardized, browser-native protocol that allows a website to expose its immutable, verified evidence directly to AI agents . A perfect fit with how youths of today consume information. What It Does With WebMCP utlovat.se transforms from a static website into an active, agent-native truth layer. Through WebMCP, an AI assistant using a WebMCP-enabled browser gains access to 7 specialized tools in total (5 global + 2 contextual): Grounded Deterministic Search (search_verified_evidence): Queries exact manifesto commitments, returning verified quotes, publication dates, transparent cost approximations (≈), and linked Wayback Machine archive copies when available (we have set a 100% archive target and are very close to achieving it). Interactive Evidence Boards (build_research_brief): Generates side-by-side comparative cards on /granska and /webmcp without recommending any party. (Absolute neutrality in all aspects are paramount) Human-in-the-Loop Protocol Verification (get_evidence_board_status): Ensures that evidence remains strictly unverified until a human visually inspects the primary citations and marks the board as confirmed. Parliamentary Trail Tracing (trace_promise): Connects a 2026 election promise to Handlingsvågen's (our Swedish feature name) database of reviewed Swedish Parliament (Riksdagen) voting records and motion histories. Multi-Party Fiscal Matrix (show_party_comparison): Opens the interactive comparison matrix on /jamfor, accounting for joint coalition proposals without double-counting costs. Contextual On-Page Tools (trace_current_promise, build_current_question_brief): Dynamically activates contextual tools when browsing individual promise pages or topic questions. Honest Silence & Gap Transparency: Explicitly reports when a party has no recorded stance on a query, rather than treating a bounded empty result as proof of a position. How We Built It We built the WebMCP integration natively into utlovat.se using TypeScript, Astro, and Tailwind CSS: WebMCP Client-Side Tool Registry (site/src/scripts/webmcp.ts): We registered our 7 tools directly onto document.modelContext.registerTool, defining JSON schemas with strict parameter typing and non-partisan output structures. Cryptographic Data Hashing: Every tool response includes an authoritative data_hash (SHA-256), anchoring conclusions to a fixed, verifiable dataset snapshot. Web Archive Links: When available, a political quote includes a linked Wayback Machine archive copy, making the source trail inspectable and more resilient to link rot. (And also makes it possible to highlight a change in stance in any question). Parliamentary Open Data Bridge: We engineered client-side JSON API endpoints (/api/v1/promise-traces.json) linking election promises to Swedish Parliament document IDs (e.g. data.riksdagen.se/dokument/HB023145) and chamber voting roll calls. Dedicated English Surface (/webmcp): Built a dedicated English hub with the 5 global WebMCP tools, clear testing prompts and cost-range badges. The 2 contextual tools become available after navigation to an individual promise or topic page. Challenges We Faced Enforcing Strict Epistemic Neutrality: The primary challenge in election technology is preventing algorithmic bias. We designed tool descriptions and read-only output structures that explicitly instruct agents not to declare "kept/broken" verdicts, rank parties, or make subjective recommendations. Honest Silence Reporting: In standard RAG pipelines, an empty search return is ambiguous. We had to architect bounded search semantics so that if a party has no promise on a topic, the agent explicitly returns a structured gap report rather than extrapolating. Sometimes silence speaks a thousand words. Zero-Self-Certification for Human-in-the-Loop: We had to ensure agents could not fake human verification. The get_evidence_board_status tool queries the live DOM state, which only transitions to confirmed ✓ when the physical user clicks the inspection button. Anti-Double-Counting in Coalition Manifestos: Calculating joint party proposals required shared commitment keying so coalition proposals are not counted twice in fiscal matrix calculations.
