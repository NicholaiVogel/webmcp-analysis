# FROZEN SCORING RUBRIC (pre-registered before any Stage 1 review)

Scores are integers 1-10 on each of the FOUR official criteria. Every score carries:
rationale, evidence_surfaces, confidence (0-1). Reviewers use the full scale and these
band anchors verbatim. Category neutrality is absolute: a game, art piece, music tool,
CRM, weird toy, and developer tool are all eligible for 10/10 on every criterion if they
satisfy that criterion exceptionally well FOR WHAT THEY ARE TRYING TO BE.
No aesthetic penalties. Signs of hurried AI work matter only as actual product-quality
problems (incoherence, broken flow, placeholder content), never as style.

## WebMCP Leverage — what does WebMCP change for this product?
Judge the delta WebMCP makes to reliability, precision, structured state access,
shared human-agent state, repeatability, and capability. Tool COUNT is not leverage.
- 9-10 TRANSFORMATIVE: the intended experience is impractical without the structured
  agent surface; human and agent share live product state; the collaboration could not
  be replicated comparably well by a general agent driving the UI.
- 7-8 MAJOR DELTA: without WebMCP a general agent is markedly less reliable/precise
  here; the agent is central to the product, not a bolt-on.
- 5-6 MEANINGFUL DELTA: existing capability packaged into a genuinely better product
  experience via structured tools; same outcome otherwise possible with real friction.
- 3-4 MINOR DELTA: generic browser automation achieves comparable outcomes; WebMCP is
  a convenience layer.
- 1-2 COSMETIC: exposes an action a UI button already offers; no meaningful change.

## Execution — is the actual product coherent, intentional, complete for its scope?
Stage 1 scores EXECUTION_CLAIMED: coherence/intentionality/completeness as evidenced by
the packet (description + packaging + video frames/transcript). Thin evidence caps the
score (end-to-end credit requires end-to-end evidence) and lowers confidence.
Stage 2 may rescore as EXECUTION_OBSERVED; latest evidence-backed score replaces, never
sums.
- 9-10: all surfaces agree; central workflow demonstrably completes; states handled;
  feels finished for its stated scope.
- 7-8: strong coherence and intentionality; central workflow appears functional; minor
  unfinished edges.
- 5-6: plausibly real with visible gaps; scope unclear or core not demonstrated
  end-to-end.
- 3-4: thin prototype; contradictions between surfaces; placeholder content.
- 1-2: non-functional or incoherent for its claimed scope.

## Creativity & Ambition — novelty of concept and interaction model
Judge ONLY: novelty of the concept, novelty of the interaction model, originality
relative to existing concepts, ambition, memorable/surprising use of the medium, and
the depth with which the concept is pursued. WebMCP indispensability belongs under
WebMCP Leverage, NOT here: a project can be extremely creative even if another agent
API could theoretically have enabled something similar.
- 9-10: genuinely novel concept or interaction model; ambitious scope pursued deeply
  and coherently; memorable.
- 7-8: fresh synthesis or notably original interaction; ambitious for a hackathon.
- 5-6: competent take on a known category with a real new angle.
- 3-4: derivative; novelty mostly surface details.
- 1-2: clone/template with trivial changes.
"That the category exists" is never a penalty; judge the submitted concept.

## Potential Impact — credible value for the intended audience
Judge: is there a real audience; is there a real problem, desire, or use case for that
audience; does the demonstrated solution genuinely address it. NO commercial adoption,
monetization, market pull, or startup viability is required. Entertainment, play, art,
creative expression, learning, niche hobby use, experimentation, and community value
are legitimate forms of impact. A tiny audience fully justifies a top score.
- 9-10: specific real audience with a real problem/desire; solution demonstrably
  addresses it; credible value, appeal, usefulness, or meaning for that audience.
- 7-8: clear audience and problem, good fit, credible value.
- 5-6: identifiable audience, looser fit or partial address.
- 3-4: speculative audience or strained fit; impact mostly aspirational.
- 1-2: no coherent audience/problem pairing.
Audience size is NOT a multiplier.

## Aggregate & ranking
aggregate = leverage + execution + impact + creativity (4-40). Nothing else enters.
Tie-breaks: Leverage, then Execution, then Impact, then Creativity, then higher
combined evidence confidence, then slug alphabetical. See PROTOCOL.md Ranking section.

This demo explores user interfaces that adapt to the humans that browse it, and asks what would the web be if you could B-Y-O-U-I: Bring your own user interface. Like Comment Story Updates Inspiration For as much as marketers have optimized for conversion, they haven't been able to cut through the number one reason that people leave open carts. they're not ready to buy. It had just happened to me while shopping for a microphone: I started at one site, then realized I needed more information to determine which was going to be the best fit. What if the shopping interface could have met me where I was at--researching the options, ranking the matches, explaining its rationale, and then designing a search result layout to fit its conclusions? That's just one glimpse of how web-interfaces of the future will adapt to their users' needs through a Live Canvas. What it does Each demo walks you through a conventional user interface, and then cordons off a bounded portion of it as WebMCP to redesign (the Live Canvas) The Storefront demo lets the agent curate the search results with a toolbox of approved card presets and badges. This constrained design is a good fit for the untrusted nature of the public internet, keeping users safe from XSS attacks. The Dashbord demo scales the idea up by giving you an entirely blank page. It imagine how the future of CRM / ERP dashboards might function. Prompt the agent to onboard you, collaborate on a vision with the agent as you would with your analytics team, and the end result is an entirely personal dashboard of controls, visualizations and reports. The Distribution demo scales the idea outwards by asking whether you distribute a Live Canvas as part of conventional component libraries. It demonstrates what a future Datatable component could be, with the ability to generate bespoke reports and save them to your browser - all without backend engineering or ai-vendor lock-in. How we built it The architecture north star is "agent composition, application execution". The application exposes its UI primitives through WebMCP. The agent supplies a composition, and then the live canvas renders that composition using application-owned components. This solution prevents data from leaking out of the organization's security boundary, and also prevents token bloat. Challenges we ran into and accomplishments that we're proud of There are two large challenges that required creativity: The largest challenge was discoverability. WebMCP is not bidirectional: an agent can invoke tools, but there's no mechanism for the website to initiate a conversation. The past 30 years of the web have trained users for the affordance of buttons and forms. I built a new set of visible prompts and verbs to communicate to surface the new modality which was an interesting accomplishment, but does highlight an apparent shortcoming in the standard. The second challenge is the built-in approval flow of agentic browsers. ChatGPT would auto-deny requests to access the user's simulated library to make recommendations, e.g. In the short term, this is solved with instructional text to ask for permission in advance. However, what WebMCP needs is a built in consent layer where tools are tied to required consent artifacts. What we learned WebMCP shows great potential to break interfaces out of the "ai chatbot jail". What's next for Live Canvas The most likely next step is to select an existing open-source component library and use it to build the first end-to-end WebMCP UI framework. That would turn the Live Canvas from just a demo pattern into a production-hardened implementation. Zero site setup. \n\n WebMCP lets agents use a site as tools instead of clicking through the UI. That only works if someone builds the tools. Every site is different — hand-writing and maintaining that for the whole web is the real bottleneck. We flipped it: the person who needs agent tools shouldn’t wait on every company to ship WebMCP. \n\n What it does \n\n Site2WebMCP is a Chrome extension (also available as an agent skill) that reads the forms and buttons already on a live page and exposes them as WebMCP tools. An agent can then run real workflows — search, add to cart, checkout, and so on — without browser control and without the site owner implementing anything. \n\n How we built it \n\n \n A simple webpage with two demo modes and WebMCP for auto-setup of demo 1 — a playground only. The product generates WebMCP for any external site. \n Chrome MV3 extension: discover forms and primary actions from the live DOM, plus registerTool where needed. \n An agent skill that does the same job as the extension — inject into the page, discover DOM actions, register them as WebMCP. \n \n\n Challenges \n\n Getting from “tools appear” to “an agent actually completes a workflow” was the hard part — especially validating the agent-side path, not just the discovery UI. The runtime still leans on e-commerce-shaped heuristics; proving this beyond that shape is the next fight. Hand-written WebMCP will always be sharper for a given site — this is WebMCP for sites that never shipped it, replaceable when they do. \n\n ChatGPT Desktop’s built-in browser is read-only: no extensions, no injected scripts. That blocks dynamically generated WebMCP there. \n\n Chrome Web Store review is slow, so the extension isn’t installable from the store yet. \n\n What’s next \n\n \n Use site JavaScript to find server requests and expose them as tools, with descriptions of what those functions do. \n Crawl support centres and docs for how to use the site, then turn that into tools and skills. \n Use llms.txt to extract tools or workflows (chains of tool calls). \n Use an external or browser-built-in LLM to identify workflows on a site and turn them into tools and skills. \n Measuring the speed and tokens savings compared to a vanila browser use that does clicks. \n \n\n \n\n \n Built With \n\n chrome grok-bot javascript \n \n\n \n Try it out \n\n \n \n \n GitHub Repo \n \n \n \n youtu.be \n \n \n \n\n \n\n \n\n \n \n Submitted to\n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n \n The WebMCP Challenge \n \n\n\n \n\n\n \n\n \n \n\n \n \n Created by\n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n Viacheslav Tradunsky \n \n \n\n \n\n \n \n \n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n\n \n\n\n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n Like\n 1 \n \n \n\n\n \n 1 person likes this:\n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n\n\n \n \n \n \n Share this project:\n \n\n\n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n\n \n \n \n \n\n \n \n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n\n\n\n \n \n \n Updates \n\n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n \n Viacheslav Tradunsky \n posted an update\n \n —\n \n 2 days ago \n \n \n\n Please watch with audio.
