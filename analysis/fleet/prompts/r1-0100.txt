# FROZEN SCORING RUBRIC (pre-registered before any Stage 1 review)

Scores are integers 1-10 on each of the FOUR official criteria. Every score carries:
rationale, evidence_surfaces, confidence (0-1). Reviewers use the full scale and these
band anchors verbatim. Category neutrality is absolute: a game, art piece, music tool,
CRM, weird toy, and developer tool are all eligible for 10/10 on every criterion if they
satisfy that criterion exceptionally well FOR WHAT THEY ARE TRYING TO BE.
No aesthetic penalties. Signs of hurried AI work matter only as actual product-quality
problems (incoherence, broken flow, placeholder content), never as style.

## WebMCP Leverage — what does WebMCP change for this product?
Judge the delta WebMCP makes to reliability, precision, structured state access,
shared human-agent state, repeatability, and capability. Tool COUNT is not leverage.
- 9-10 TRANSFORMATIVE: the intended experience is impractical without the structured
  agent surface; human and agent share live product state; the collaboration could not
  be replicated comparably well by a general agent driving the UI.
- 7-8 MAJOR DELTA: without WebMCP a general agent is markedly less reliable/precise
  here; the agent is central to the product, not a bolt-on.
- 5-6 MEANINGFUL DELTA: existing capability packaged into a genuinely better product
  experience via structured tools; same outcome otherwise possible with real friction.
- 3-4 MINOR DELTA: generic browser automation achieves comparable outcomes; WebMCP is
  a convenience layer.
- 1-2 COSMETIC: exposes an action a UI button already offers; no meaningful change.

## Execution — is the actual product coherent, intentional, complete for its scope?
Stage 1 scores EXECUTION_CLAIMED: coherence/intentionality/completeness as evidenced by
the packet (description + packaging + video frames/transcript). Thin evidence caps the
score (end-to-end credit requires end-to-end evidence) and lowers confidence.
Stage 2 may rescore as EXECUTION_OBSERVED; latest evidence-backed score replaces, never
sums.
- 9-10: all surfaces agree; central workflow demonstrably completes; states handled;
  feels finished for its stated scope.
- 7-8: strong coherence and intentionality; central workflow appears functional; minor
  unfinished edges.
- 5-6: plausibly real with visible gaps; scope unclear or core not demonstrated
  end-to-end.
- 3-4: thin prototype; contradictions between surfaces; placeholder content.
- 1-2: non-functional or incoherent for its claimed scope.

## Creativity & Ambition — novelty of concept and interaction model
Judge ONLY: novelty of the concept, novelty of the interaction model, originality
relative to existing concepts, ambition, memorable/surprising use of the medium, and
the depth with which the concept is pursued. WebMCP indispensability belongs under
WebMCP Leverage, NOT here: a project can be extremely creative even if another agent
API could theoretically have enabled something similar.
- 9-10: genuinely novel concept or interaction model; ambitious scope pursued deeply
  and coherently; memorable.
- 7-8: fresh synthesis or notably original interaction; ambitious for a hackathon.
- 5-6: competent take on a known category with a real new angle.
- 3-4: derivative; novelty mostly surface details.
- 1-2: clone/template with trivial changes.
"That the category exists" is never a penalty; judge the submitted concept.

## Potential Impact — credible value for the intended audience
Judge: is there a real audience; is there a real problem, desire, or use case for that
audience; does the demonstrated solution genuinely address it. NO commercial adoption,
monetization, market pull, or startup viability is required. Entertainment, play, art,
creative expression, learning, niche hobby use, experimentation, and community value
are legitimate forms of impact. A tiny audience fully justifies a top score.
- 9-10: specific real audience with a real problem/desire; solution demonstrably
  addresses it; credible value, appeal, usefulness, or meaning for that audience.
- 7-8: clear audience and problem, good fit, credible value.
- 5-6: identifiable audience, looser fit or partial address.
- 3-4: speculative audience or strained fit; impact mostly aspirational.
- 1-2: no coherent audience/problem pairing.
Audience size is NOT a multiplier.

## Aggregate & ranking
aggregate = leverage + execution + impact + creativity (4-40). Nothing else enters.
Tie-breaks: Leverage, then Execution, then Impact, then Creativity, then higher
combined evidence confidence, then slug alphabetical. See PROTOCOL.md Ranking section.

more than the user's agent. \n\n Businesses and services can participate as active entities in a workflow. \n\n This lets us demonstrate agent-to-business, business-to-supplier, and multi-party coordination rather than treating every external service as a passive database lookup. \n\n WebMCP and tool interaction \n\n Asympta World is designed around the idea that agents should interact with capabilities exposed by the web instead of requiring every website to be manually rewritten into a custom integration. \n\n WebMCP provides an important building block for this model: websites can expose structured capabilities that agents can discover and use. \n\n Asympta World explores what happens when those capabilities exist inside a shared spatial coordination layer. \n\n Instead of thinking: \n\n User → Agent → Website \n\n we started thinking in terms of: \n\n Person ↔ Agent ↔ Business Agent ↔ Services ↔ Other Agents \n\n with the world itself providing the context connecting them. \n\n \n\n Challenges we ran into \n\n Making an agent feel like it actually exists in the world \n\n It is easy to place an agent icon on a map. It is much harder to make the agent's movement correspond meaningfully to what the system is actually doing. \n\n We had to connect task state, destination selection, animation, camera movement, and progress so that they behave as one system. \n\n Showing complex activity without overwhelming the user \n\n A real workflow can involve many simultaneous actions. \n\n Displaying everything creates visual noise. Hiding everything makes the world feel fake. \n\n We experimented with temporary activity indicators, contextual agent status, dialogue bubbles, fading map activity, automatic camera following, and progressive disclosure to find a better balance. \n\n Modeling the supply side \n\n Many agent demonstrations stop once an order is sent. \n\n But the interesting part often begins after that. \n\n A business may need to confirm inventory, prepare materials, coordinate staff, request something from a supplier, schedule fulfillment, and arrange delivery. \n\n Representing these processes forced us to think about the world as a network of interacting systems instead of a single user's workflow. \n\n Performance \n\n A living world means many moving objects, effects, state transitions, and UI updates. \n\n We repeatedly had to reduce unnecessary rendering work, control how much context was displayed at once, and make activity effects temporary rather than permanently accumulating. \n\n The principle became: \n\n \n The world can become more complex without rendering cost growing at the same rate. \n \n\n Localization \n\n Another unexpected challenge was keeping language changes consistent across a live simulation. \n\n Static interface text is straightforwar

Market research on streaming — micro-drama, African OTT, piracy, sports rights. Free WebMCP tools tell an agent what the paid data covers and how fresh it is, without returning a value. Like Comment Story Updates Inspiration We already sell streaming-market research: 17,470 sourced datapoints on micro-drama, African OTT, piracy, the creator economy and sports rights, behind a paid MCP server with 194 tools. So the WebMCP question was never "what demo could we build" — it was sharper and more uncomfortable: what does a paywalled B2B product do when the visitor is an agent? Today, agents scrape sites like ours. They read the marketing pages, miss the data entirely, and invent the numbers. Blocking them is not an answer either: an agent that leaves with nothing never comes back, and never buys. Giving the data away ends the business. WebMCP allows a fourth option, and that is the whole submission: describe the asset precisely, withhold its substance, and always say where the substance lives. What it does Every page of lens.streaming-radar.com — 49 of them — registers tools on document.modelContext . Seven, all free, no key, no account, and no flag for the visitor to enable: we serve a first-party Chrome origin-trial token in <head> . ask_site — returns the page sections that answer a question, with canonical URLs. search_intelligence_archive — searches the published newsletter editions and analyses. check_data_coverage — the one that carries the idea. Ask about any company, country or topic and it returns how many sourced datapoints we hold, which report silos they sit in, which countries and years they cover, the confidence mix, when the most recent one was updated, and one sample datapoint — metric name, unit, source, confidence — with its value withheld . Asked about ReelShort: 408 datapoints, across five silos, 2022 to 2028, the most recent updated this week. get_market_signals — the live monitoring feed; it also re-renders the visible feed on the page. estimate_streaming_pnl — drives the on-page P&L calculator and returns the model output. Registered on /calculator only: a tool with no valid target is a broken contract. request_report_teaser — the conversion step. Requires an explicit consent argument and fills the page's own email gate, so the human sees what the agent did. browse_reports — the catalogue. Then the boundary itself. Until this week, a paid tool called without entitlement answered Authentication required — three words, a dead end. Now the JSON-RPC error carries error.data : what the silo holds (5,306 datapoints, 51 countries, 2004 to 2033 on our vertical-drama silo), one sample with its value withheld, the URLs to buy or authenticate, and the exact instruction to replay the call with a key. The refusal proves possession instead of ending the conversation. The same computation answers the free tool and the refusal, because they answer the same question and must answer identically. How we built it The site is static HTML with no bundler and no package.json , so the SDK is vendored as a pinned ESM asset and the module graph is injected by the site's page producer , never edited page by page. Tools are side-effect-free ES modules, registered once per document, unregistered on pagehide . The tools never reimplement the page. request_report_teaser calls the site's own email-gate API through a small public seam; estimate_streaming_pnl moves the calculator's real controls and lets the page compute; get_market_signals reads the same endpoint the visible feed reads. One behaviour, one implementation, whether a human or an agent triggers it. Agent traffic is measured server-side with a cookieless beacon — no cookie, no identifier, nothing to consent to — because GA4 only loads after a cookie banner and an agent never clicks Accept. Without it, agent traffic would be strictly invisible. Challenges we ran into A cache header that silently ate a release. Two new tools worked on the origin and reached no browser. Cloudflare Pages does not let a more specific _headers rule override a broader one — it concatenates them: max-age=31536000, immutable, public, max-age=300, must-revalidate . Browsers keep the first directive, so the modules stayed immutable for a year. The fix was to move the whole graph out of the /js/ prefix; there is no negation and no override. Short acronyms matching inside words. Our coverage counter used ILIKE '%ott%' and returned 109 rows; word-bounded, 78. Twenty-eight percent phantom matches in a number we use as a commercial argument. The same bug in our signal matcher had been filing Ridley Scott stories into the sports silo — ott inside Scott , mma inside summary . Both are bounded now, with regression tests. Bilingual pages. Our HTML carries French and English in the same DOM, hidden by CSS. In a detached document no CSS applies, so the first version of ask_site handed agents both languages glued together: "En brefTL;DR". A surface that switches off in silence. Without a valid origin-trial token, document.modelContext simply does not exist and the SDK no-ops — no error anywhere. Our pipeline health check now reads the token off the deployed page, decodes its expiry, and goes red fourteen days before. Accomplishments that we're proud of It is not a demo. It is a production B2B product, the surface is measured, and the independent webmcp.com directory grades the implementation A . 1,404 tests green, including three whose only job is to prove no datapoint value can escape through the free surface. What we learned An agent-native paywall is not a wall with an API. It is a boundary that explains itself. Publish the shape, withhold the substance, always say where the substance lives — everything else follows. What's next for Streaming Lens An agent-reachable checkout, so the handoff completes without a human copying a key. And per-call payment at the edge: our MCP already runs on Cloudflare, and a 402 with a machine-readable price is the same envelope we just built, with an amount attached.
