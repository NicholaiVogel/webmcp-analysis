# FROZEN SCORING RUBRIC (pre-registered before any Stage 1 review)

Scores are integers 1-10 on each of the FOUR official criteria. Every score carries:
rationale, evidence_surfaces, confidence (0-1). Reviewers use the full scale and these
band anchors verbatim. Category neutrality is absolute: a game, art piece, music tool,
CRM, weird toy, and developer tool are all eligible for 10/10 on every criterion if they
satisfy that criterion exceptionally well FOR WHAT THEY ARE TRYING TO BE.
No aesthetic penalties. Signs of hurried AI work matter only as actual product-quality
problems (incoherence, broken flow, placeholder content), never as style.

## WebMCP Leverage — what does WebMCP change for this product?
Judge the delta WebMCP makes to reliability, precision, structured state access,
shared human-agent state, repeatability, and capability. Tool COUNT is not leverage.
- 9-10 TRANSFORMATIVE: the intended experience is impractical without the structured
  agent surface; human and agent share live product state; the collaboration could not
  be replicated comparably well by a general agent driving the UI.
- 7-8 MAJOR DELTA: without WebMCP a general agent is markedly less reliable/precise
  here; the agent is central to the product, not a bolt-on.
- 5-6 MEANINGFUL DELTA: existing capability packaged into a genuinely better product
  experience via structured tools; same outcome otherwise possible with real friction.
- 3-4 MINOR DELTA: generic browser automation achieves comparable outcomes; WebMCP is
  a convenience layer.
- 1-2 COSMETIC: exposes an action a UI button already offers; no meaningful change.

## Execution — is the actual product coherent, intentional, complete for its scope?
Stage 1 scores EXECUTION_CLAIMED: coherence/intentionality/completeness as evidenced by
the packet (description + packaging + video frames/transcript). Thin evidence caps the
score (end-to-end credit requires end-to-end evidence) and lowers confidence.
Stage 2 may rescore as EXECUTION_OBSERVED; latest evidence-backed score replaces, never
sums.
- 9-10: all surfaces agree; central workflow demonstrably completes; states handled;
  feels finished for its stated scope.
- 7-8: strong coherence and intentionality; central workflow appears functional; minor
  unfinished edges.
- 5-6: plausibly real with visible gaps; scope unclear or core not demonstrated
  end-to-end.
- 3-4: thin prototype; contradictions between surfaces; placeholder content.
- 1-2: non-functional or incoherent for its claimed scope.

## Creativity & Ambition — novelty of concept and interaction model
Judge ONLY: novelty of the concept, novelty of the interaction model, originality
relative to existing concepts, ambition, memorable/surprising use of the medium, and
the depth with which the concept is pursued. WebMCP indispensability belongs under
WebMCP Leverage, NOT here: a project can be extremely creative even if another agent
API could theoretically have enabled something similar.
- 9-10: genuinely novel concept or interaction model; ambitious scope pursued deeply
  and coherently; memorable.
- 7-8: fresh synthesis or notably original interaction; ambitious for a hackathon.
- 5-6: competent take on a known category with a real new angle.
- 3-4: derivative; novelty mostly surface details.
- 1-2: clone/template with trivial changes.
"That the category exists" is never a penalty; judge the submitted concept.

## Potential Impact — credible value for the intended audience
Judge: is there a real audience; is there a real problem, desire, or use case for that
audience; does the demonstrated solution genuinely address it. NO commercial adoption,
monetization, market pull, or startup viability is required. Entertainment, play, art,
creative expression, learning, niche hobby use, experimentation, and community value
are legitimate forms of impact. A tiny audience fully justifies a top score.
- 9-10: specific real audience with a real problem/desire; solution demonstrably
  addresses it; credible value, appeal, usefulness, or meaning for that audience.
- 7-8: clear audience and problem, good fit, credible value.
- 5-6: identifiable audience, looser fit or partial address.
- 3-4: speculative audience or strained fit; impact mostly aspirational.
- 1-2: no coherent audience/problem pairing.
Audience size is NOT a multiplier.

## Aggregate & ranking
aggregate = leverage + execution + impact + creativity (4-40). Nothing else enters.
Tie-breaks: Leverage, then Execution, then Impact, then Creativity, then higher
combined evidence confidence, then slug alphabetical. See PROTOCOL.md Ranking section.

WebMCP made that possible in a way ad-hoc browser automation never could because a registered tool is structured data , a security layer can actually reason about what a call means before it runs, not just watch pixels get clicked. \n\n What it does \n\n AgentOps takes a plain-language goal "book me a flight to Venice under $600," "order pizza for a get-together" and turns it into a mission: a sequence of real WebMCP tool calls. Every one of those calls passes through Sentinel , our trust/policy layer, before it executes: \n\n \n Low-risk actions (search, compare, read) run automatically. \n Consequential ones (bookings, purchases above a user-set threshold, sending messages) pause and ask for a one-click approval, with a plain-English reason and a risk score. \n A fixed set of categories (credentials, password changes, financial transfers) are never auto-approved, full stop. \n Anything a tool returns is treated as untrusted content if it contains an embedded instruction trying to override the agent (a prompt injection), Sentinel blocks it outright. Not a setting. A boundary. \n \n\n The user sets their own risk tolerance once in a Trust Policy screen; every mission afterward respects it without further supervision. Missions pause and resume in place no restarting and every decision is logged. \n\n How we built it \n\n The frontend is a Vite/React app with a Three.js "Agent Core" that visually reflects the agent's state (idle, thinking, executing, awaiting approval, blocked). Every mission step is registered as a real tool via document.modelContext.registerTool() / navigator.modelContext native on Chrome behind the WebMCP flag, or through the @mcp-b/webmcp-polyfill package everywhere else, so the same code path works across environments. Before executing anything, the mission engine calls getTools() a genuine discovery step, not a hardcoded list. \n\n Sentinel's gate lives inside each tool's execute() , so the enforcement isn't UI-only it applies no matter who calls the tool, our own runner or, in principle, an external agent. Mission plans are generated at runtime by an LLM (via an OpenAI-compatible endpoint) rather than picked from a fixed script, and one mission's flight-search step calls a real (sandboxed) flight-search API through a small serverless proxy the only place any secret key lives, since real commerce/travel APIs explicitly block direct browser calls. \n\n Challenges I ran into \n\n A few things broke in ways that shaped the final architecture: \n\n \n My first pick for real flight data, Amadeus's free self-service tier, was decommissioned mid-build . We moved to Duffel's test-mode API instead. \n Duffel (like most real commercial APIs) explicitly blocks direct browser calls you cannot safely hold a real secret key client-side. That's what pushed me to add a minimal Vercel serverless function as a proxy: the smallest amount of backend that makes a genuinely real API call possible without leaking credentials. \n The LLM provider deprecated the model I was building with mid-hackathon, silently breaking AI planning until I caught it and swapped to their current recommended model. \n The most important bug we found: Sentinel's purchase-risk check used (amount || 0) > threshold , so a step with no amount attached like an AI-planned "order pizza" step that omitted a price silently defaulted to "safe" and auto-approved. I fixed this as defense-in-depth: a missing amount now escalates to "ask" rather than being treated as zero risk, and a second, independent check flags any step whose own description implies a real commitment (ordering, paying, booking, canceling) even if the planner mis-categorized it. Sentinel's judgment isn't purely downstream of a label the LLM chose. \n \n\n Accomplishments that I'm proud of \n\n Getting Sentinel to feel like a real trust boundary rather than a UI decoration the prompt-injection detection in particular, where content a tool returns is scanned as untrusted data before the agent can act on it, not just the request being sent. And getting at least one mission wired to genuinely real external data (not just simulated), despite hitting a dead API provider and browser CORS restrictions along the way, without ever compromising a secret key. \n\n What I learned \n\n WebMCP's ecosystem is still very early almost no production site implements it yet, which shaped an honest choice to have AgentOps register its own actions as tools rather than pretend arbitrary sites already support this. I also learned, the hard way, that any security layer built on top of an LLM's own categorization needs a second, independent check trusting a model to always self-report risk correctly is exactly the kind of single point of failure a "trust boundary" is supposed to prevent. \n\n What's next for AgentOps \n\n Wiring real external data into the Shopping and Finance missions the same way I did for Travel; giving AgentOps a second, fully independent WebMCP surface (a separate site registering its own tools) so the External Agent → Sentinel → WebMCP Tool → Website story is completely literal instead of self-hosted; and letting a real external agent not just our own UI discover and drive AgentOps' tools directly. \n\n \n\n \n Built With \n\n css duffel-api groq html javascript llm lucide-react mcp-b model-context-protocol node.js openai-api-compatible react rest-api serverless-functions three.js vercel vite webmcp \n \n\n \n Try it out \n\n \n \n \n agent-ops-commandcenter.vercel.app \n \n \n \n GitHub Repo \n \n \n \n\n \n\n \n\n \n \n Submitted to\n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n \n The WebMCP Challenge \n \n\n\n \n\n\n \n\n \n \n\n \n \n Created by\n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n Nixphantomz Onuh \n \n \n\n \n\n \n \n \n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n\n \n\n\n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n Like\n \n \n\n\n \n \n \n\n \n \n\n \n\n\n \n \n \n \n Share this project:\n \n\n\n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n\n \n \n \n \n\n \n \n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n\n\n\n \n \n \n Updates \n\n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n \n Nixphantomz Onuh \n started this project\n \n —\n \n 2 days ago \n \n \n\n

An agent-native semantic workspace where ChatGPT proposes validated visual structures through WebMCP—and the human retains authority to accept, export, or undo. Like Comment Story Updates Inspiration Complex systems do not need more interface. They need better structure. Architecture diagrams, workflows, data models, and knowledge graphs already contain meaning, but turning that meaning into a useful visual usually requires authored coordinates, diagram syntax, manually positioned shapes, or generated output that is difficult to inspect. I built Orbweaver around a simpler principle: Understand complex systems while showing less. What it does Orbweaver turns coordinate-free semantic graphs into deterministic, inspectable visual structures. The input describes meaning: what exists; how things relate; which relationships matter; what evidence and claims support the structure. Orbweaver validates that meaning, computes the layout, and renders an accessible SVG artifact. The proposal contains no authored coordinates, SVG, HTML, CSS, or executable markup. The WebMCP playground extends this into a shared human-agent workspace. ChatGPT can read the live workspace contract, create or revise a semantic proposal, inspect an entity, and focus it in the visible diagram. The agent can propose meaning. It cannot accept its own work. Why WebMCP Without WebMCP, an agent would need to infer interface controls and repeatedly manipulate a large JSON editor. That is slow, fragile, and disconnected from the application’s actual domain model. With WebMCP, Orbweaver exposes six bounded, page-local tools: read the current workspace and revision; read the supported semantic contract; create a complete proposal; revise it using conflict-safe operations; inspect semantic identity and provenance; focus an entity in the visible workspace. The tools reuse the same application controller and validation boundary as the human interface. WebMCP does not introduce a second, privileged execution path. The human-agent experience A person describes an order-fulfillment system in ordinary language. ChatGPT discovers Orbweaver’s tools and supplies entities, relationships, groups, claims, and evidence—not pixels or drawing instructions. Orbweaver validates the entire proposal atomically, derives the geometry, and renders revision one as a validated but untrusted preview . The person and agent then work with the same live state. ChatGPT can add Delivery tracking, focus it, and expose its relationships for inspection. Expected-revision checks prevent stale writes when the shared workspace changes. Acceptance remains a human-only decision. The agent cannot accept a revision or enable export. The person can inspect, reject, or undo the change. In the demonstration, I manually undo the agent’s revision and Orbweaver restores the previous graph. How I built it Orbweaver’s core is a provider-neutral TypeScript library. A strict GraphProposal contract represents semantic content without layout coordinates. ELK.js computes the visual structure, while Orbweaver produces accessible SVG, stable semantic identities, selection behavior, provenance, and inspection details. The playground’s WebMCP adapter registers six tools through the browser’s page-local model context. Tool inputs are treated as untrusted data and pass through the existing validation layer. Create and revise operations are atomic, revisions are versioned, and semantic writes never bundle human acceptance. The implementation is tested with Vitest, Playwright, and axe-core accessibility checks. The npm package contains no AI provider, account requirement, telemetry, or cloud runtime dependency. Challenges The hardest problem was not drawing the graph. It was designing the authority boundary. A useful agent needed enough access to create meaningful structures without gaining the ability to silently approve its own output. This required explicit trust states, expected-revision conflict protection, atomic validation, reversible mutations, and accepted-only export. The other challenge was visual restraint: presenting enough information to understand a complex system without turning the workspace into an aircraft cockpit. Orbweaver keeps the interface secondary to the structure being examined. Accomplishments I’m proud of Published a provider-neutral open-source TypeScript library rather than embedding an AI service inside the product. Built six bounded WebMCP tools around the application’s existing controller and validation boundary. Made agent-authored changes versioned, atomic, inspectable, and reversible. Preserved human-only authority over acceptance and export. Produced deterministic, accessible SVG from semantic proposals containing no authored coordinates. Published the beta package, live playground, documentation, source, and reproducible demonstration. What I learned WebMCP is most valuable when it exposes an application’s domain semantics—not when it simply recreates button clicking as tools. The strongest human-agent experience is not full autonomy. It is shared context with asymmetric authority: the agent contributes reach and synthesis; the application enforces structure; the human retains judgment at the moment of consequence. What’s next Orbweaver is open source and currently available as @semanticintent/orbweaver@0.2.0-beta.1 . Next steps include stabilizing the beta API, expanding semantic lenses and comparison views, and continuing to develop portable artifacts that preserve their meaning, provenance, and accessibility outside the original application.
