# FROZEN SCORING RUBRIC (pre-registered before any Stage 1 review)

Scores are integers 1-10 on each of the FOUR official criteria. Every score carries:
rationale, evidence_surfaces, confidence (0-1). Reviewers use the full scale and these
band anchors verbatim. Category neutrality is absolute: a game, art piece, music tool,
CRM, weird toy, and developer tool are all eligible for 10/10 on every criterion if they
satisfy that criterion exceptionally well FOR WHAT THEY ARE TRYING TO BE.
No aesthetic penalties. Signs of hurried AI work matter only as actual product-quality
problems (incoherence, broken flow, placeholder content), never as style.

## WebMCP Leverage — what does WebMCP change for this product?
Judge the delta WebMCP makes to reliability, precision, structured state access,
shared human-agent state, repeatability, and capability. Tool COUNT is not leverage.
- 9-10 TRANSFORMATIVE: the intended experience is impractical without the structured
  agent surface; human and agent share live product state; the collaboration could not
  be replicated comparably well by a general agent driving the UI.
- 7-8 MAJOR DELTA: without WebMCP a general agent is markedly less reliable/precise
  here; the agent is central to the product, not a bolt-on.
- 5-6 MEANINGFUL DELTA: existing capability packaged into a genuinely better product
  experience via structured tools; same outcome otherwise possible with real friction.
- 3-4 MINOR DELTA: generic browser automation achieves comparable outcomes; WebMCP is
  a convenience layer.
- 1-2 COSMETIC: exposes an action a UI button already offers; no meaningful change.

## Execution — is the actual product coherent, intentional, complete for its scope?
Stage 1 scores EXECUTION_CLAIMED: coherence/intentionality/completeness as evidenced by
the packet (description + packaging + video frames/transcript). Thin evidence caps the
score (end-to-end credit requires end-to-end evidence) and lowers confidence.
Stage 2 may rescore as EXECUTION_OBSERVED; latest evidence-backed score replaces, never
sums.
- 9-10: all surfaces agree; central workflow demonstrably completes; states handled;
  feels finished for its stated scope.
- 7-8: strong coherence and intentionality; central workflow appears functional; minor
  unfinished edges.
- 5-6: plausibly real with visible gaps; scope unclear or core not demonstrated
  end-to-end.
- 3-4: thin prototype; contradictions between surfaces; placeholder content.
- 1-2: non-functional or incoherent for its claimed scope.

## Creativity & Ambition — novelty of concept and interaction model
Judge ONLY: novelty of the concept, novelty of the interaction model, originality
relative to existing concepts, ambition, memorable/surprising use of the medium, and
the depth with which the concept is pursued. WebMCP indispensability belongs under
WebMCP Leverage, NOT here: a project can be extremely creative even if another agent
API could theoretically have enabled something similar.
- 9-10: genuinely novel concept or interaction model; ambitious scope pursued deeply
  and coherently; memorable.
- 7-8: fresh synthesis or notably original interaction; ambitious for a hackathon.
- 5-6: competent take on a known category with a real new angle.
- 3-4: derivative; novelty mostly surface details.
- 1-2: clone/template with trivial changes.
"That the category exists" is never a penalty; judge the submitted concept.

## Potential Impact — credible value for the intended audience
Judge: is there a real audience; is there a real problem, desire, or use case for that
audience; does the demonstrated solution genuinely address it. NO commercial adoption,
monetization, market pull, or startup viability is required. Entertainment, play, art,
creative expression, learning, niche hobby use, experimentation, and community value
are legitimate forms of impact. A tiny audience fully justifies a top score.
- 9-10: specific real audience with a real problem/desire; solution demonstrably
  addresses it; credible value, appeal, usefulness, or meaning for that audience.
- 7-8: clear audience and problem, good fit, credible value.
- 5-6: identifiable audience, looser fit or partial address.
- 3-4: speculative audience or strained fit; impact mostly aspirational.
- 1-2: no coherent audience/problem pairing.
Audience size is NOT a multiplier.

## Aggregate & ranking
aggregate = leverage + execution + impact + creativity (4-40). Nothing else enters.
Tie-breaks: Leverage, then Execution, then Impact, then Creativity, then higher
combined evidence confidence, then slug alphabetical. See PROTOCOL.md Ranking section.

Different pages have different purposes, so they can expose different agent contexts. A homepage can explain the business and its services. A service page can expose service-specific knowledge, rules and constraints. A contact page can expose contact options and controlled actions. A page with nothing useful for an agent can expose nothing at all. The website does not need one large AI interface. Every page can become its own AI interface when it makes sense. Why WebMCP WebMCP creates a new interface between websites and AI. Traditional websites expose visual interfaces optimized for humans. AI systems can attempt to interpret those interfaces, but important business meaning may be distributed across navigation structures, text, forms and application logic. WebMCP allows the website to expose that meaning explicitly. For a local business, this can mean exposing: who the business is; what services it provides; where it operates; service-specific knowledge; business rules and constraints; what information an action requires; which actions are available; when human confirmation is required. The result is not about replacing the website. It is about giving the same business two interfaces: one for humans and one for AI. One website. Two interfaces: Humans + AI. We also wanted to explore WebMCP without introducing unnecessary infrastructure. This implementation does not require a separate MCP server for the agent to discover, configure or connect to. An agent should be able to do the tedious 90% (searching the catalog, comparing specs, filling the cart, applying discount codes) autonomously, but the final "place an order with real money" step requires a deliberate, visible human decision. CoShop was born out of the necessity to build a secure, collaborative, and beautiful environment where humans and agents can shop together without liability risks. \n\n What it does \n\n CoShop is a high-performance, stateless e-commerce storefront that exposes its entire architecture to AI agents via WebMCP. \n\n \n Live UI Grounding: When the AI agent interacts with the store (e.g., searching or adding an item to the cart), the human shopper's screen visually reacts. The cart slides open, the product card pulses, and an accessible activity log narrates the action. The AI doesn't work in a black box; the human watches exactly what it is doing. \n Human-in-the-Loop Checkout: When an agent attempts to check out, it is securely gated. The agent can only stage the checkout, which summons a live confirmation banner on the human's screen. The transaction is completely halted until the human physically clicks "Confirm Order." \n \n\n How we built it \n\n We built CoShop using Next.js 16 (App Router), TypeScript, and Tailwind CSS v4, styling it with a premium "Midnight Violet" glassmorphism aesthetic and 8K photorealistic product assets. \n\n The core of our WebMCP integration relies on the use-webmcp-tool React hook. We registered 11 distinct tools to document.modelContext . \n\n The architectural magic happens in our state management. We used Zustand as a single source of truth. When an AI agent executes a WebMCP tool (like add_to_cart ), the callback fires a Zustand state mutation. Because the React UI is listening to that exact same store, the UI instantly animates via Framer Motion to reflect the agent's action to the human user. Furthermore, the cart state is persisted into secure, HTTP-only cookies, ensuring the app remains entirely stateless and scalable on Vercel's Edge network without requiring an external database. \n\n \n\n The WebMCP Fit & User Experience \n\n Why this is a strong fit for WebMCP: \nMost agentic-commerce demos rely on brittle DOM-scraping or disjointed chat widgets that act on a storefront rather than with it. CoShop leverages the Model Context Protocol for Web (WebMCP) to expose 11 schema-defined tools directly to the agent's context window. This eliminates hallucination-the agent doesn't guess how to buy a shoe; it natively calls add_to_cart(productId, size, qty) with 100% deterministic accuracy. \n\n What humans and agents can do together now: \nBefore CoShop, you either shopped yourself, or you handed a task to an autonomous agent and hoped it didn't accidentally drain your credit card. CoShop introduces the Shared Session . A human can manually add a shirt to their cart, and concurrently ask their AI agent to find a matching pair of waterproof boots and add them to the same cart. They are no longer isolated; they are collaborating in real-time. \n\n \n\n Challenges we ran into \n\n The biggest challenge was bridging the gap between the agent's headless execution and the human's visual experience. Initially, when the agent added an item to the cart, the human wouldn't know it happened unless they manually checked. We had to heavily engineer our state synchronization so that WebMCP execution callbacks directly triggered UI animations, sidebars, and focus states, ensuring absolute transparency. \n\n Accomplishments that we're proud of \n\n We are incredibly proud of achieving true Live Agent Grounding .
