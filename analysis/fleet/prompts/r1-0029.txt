# FROZEN SCORING RUBRIC (pre-registered before any Stage 1 review)

Scores are integers 1-10 on each of the FOUR official criteria. Every score carries:
rationale, evidence_surfaces, confidence (0-1). Reviewers use the full scale and these
band anchors verbatim. Category neutrality is absolute: a game, art piece, music tool,
CRM, weird toy, and developer tool are all eligible for 10/10 on every criterion if they
satisfy that criterion exceptionally well FOR WHAT THEY ARE TRYING TO BE.
No aesthetic penalties. Signs of hurried AI work matter only as actual product-quality
problems (incoherence, broken flow, placeholder content), never as style.

## WebMCP Leverage — what does WebMCP change for this product?
Judge the delta WebMCP makes to reliability, precision, structured state access,
shared human-agent state, repeatability, and capability. Tool COUNT is not leverage.
- 9-10 TRANSFORMATIVE: the intended experience is impractical without the structured
  agent surface; human and agent share live product state; the collaboration could not
  be replicated comparably well by a general agent driving the UI.
- 7-8 MAJOR DELTA: without WebMCP a general agent is markedly less reliable/precise
  here; the agent is central to the product, not a bolt-on.
- 5-6 MEANINGFUL DELTA: existing capability packaged into a genuinely better product
  experience via structured tools; same outcome otherwise possible with real friction.
- 3-4 MINOR DELTA: generic browser automation achieves comparable outcomes; WebMCP is
  a convenience layer.
- 1-2 COSMETIC: exposes an action a UI button already offers; no meaningful change.

## Execution — is the actual product coherent, intentional, complete for its scope?
Stage 1 scores EXECUTION_CLAIMED: coherence/intentionality/completeness as evidenced by
the packet (description + packaging + video frames/transcript). Thin evidence caps the
score (end-to-end credit requires end-to-end evidence) and lowers confidence.
Stage 2 may rescore as EXECUTION_OBSERVED; latest evidence-backed score replaces, never
sums.
- 9-10: all surfaces agree; central workflow demonstrably completes; states handled;
  feels finished for its stated scope.
- 7-8: strong coherence and intentionality; central workflow appears functional; minor
  unfinished edges.
- 5-6: plausibly real with visible gaps; scope unclear or core not demonstrated
  end-to-end.
- 3-4: thin prototype; contradictions between surfaces; placeholder content.
- 1-2: non-functional or incoherent for its claimed scope.

## Creativity & Ambition — novelty of concept and interaction model
Judge ONLY: novelty of the concept, novelty of the interaction model, originality
relative to existing concepts, ambition, memorable/surprising use of the medium, and
the depth with which the concept is pursued. WebMCP indispensability belongs under
WebMCP Leverage, NOT here: a project can be extremely creative even if another agent
API could theoretically have enabled something similar.
- 9-10: genuinely novel concept or interaction model; ambitious scope pursued deeply
  and coherently; memorable.
- 7-8: fresh synthesis or notably original interaction; ambitious for a hackathon.
- 5-6: competent take on a known category with a real new angle.
- 3-4: derivative; novelty mostly surface details.
- 1-2: clone/template with trivial changes.
"That the category exists" is never a penalty; judge the submitted concept.

## Potential Impact — credible value for the intended audience
Judge: is there a real audience; is there a real problem, desire, or use case for that
audience; does the demonstrated solution genuinely address it. NO commercial adoption,
monetization, market pull, or startup viability is required. Entertainment, play, art,
creative expression, learning, niche hobby use, experimentation, and community value
are legitimate forms of impact. A tiny audience fully justifies a top score.
- 9-10: specific real audience with a real problem/desire; solution demonstrably
  addresses it; credible value, appeal, usefulness, or meaning for that audience.
- 7-8: clear audience and problem, good fit, credible value.
- 5-6: identifiable audience, looser fit or partial address.
- 3-4: speculative audience or strained fit; impact mostly aspirational.
- 1-2: no coherent audience/problem pairing.
Audience size is NOT a multiplier.

## Aggregate & ranking
aggregate = leverage + execution + impact + creativity (4-40). Nothing else enters.
Tie-breaks: Leverage, then Execution, then Impact, then Creativity, then higher
combined evidence confidence, then slug alphabetical. See PROTOCOL.md Ranking section.

ABOUT TEXT (untrusted evidence):
An infinite board you dump raw material onto — pasted notes, links, photos, sketches, half-thoughts. No folders, no markdown, no structure required. WebMCP lets the agent do the rest. Like Comment Story Updates Inspiration Mundaneum is inspired by Paul Otlet’s original Mundaneum: an ambitious early attempt to collect, connect, and organize the world’s knowledge using millions of cross-referenced index cards. Modern knowledge tools are powerful, but capturing ideas often requires deciding on folders, filenames, and structure too early. We wanted a space where people could first think naturally—dropping in notes, links, files, drawings, equations, and unfinished ideas—and let organization emerge later. We also wanted AI collaboration to feel native to the interface. Instead of making an agent interpret every interaction from screenshots or manipulate the page through coordinates, Mundaneum uses WebMCP to expose the board’s actual content and permitted actions directly to the agent. What it does Mundaneum is an infinite visual research board designed for people and AI agents to work on together. People can add notes, links, images, documents, media, files, widgets, freehand drawings, YouTube videos, Spotify songs, PDFs, Excel, and expandable document canvases. Document canvases combine positioned text, handwriting, shapes, and equations into a single visual document that a multimodal model can inspect as an image. Users can select a card or Shift-click multiple cards to establish the current focus. WebMCP returns those selected items first, allowing ChatGPT to work on a specific group without scanning an entire large board. Selected drawings are provided as a cropped image, while cards retain their structured text and metadata. Users can also copy a card’s exact ID and reference it directly in a prompt. Through WebMCP, an agent can: Read the board and its current selection View a document canvas or the entire board as a PNG Add provisional cards Connect related cards Group and label information Annotate cards visually Identify and merge likely duplicates Request grid, masonry, cluster, tree, row, or column arrangements Create sketches without controlling their final position The central rule is that agents can contribute meaning, but they cannot provide coordinates. The page remains responsible for placement, collision detection, and layout. How we built it Mundaneum is a React and TypeScript application powered by Vite. Board state is stored locally in IndexedDB, while Cloudflare Workers and Durable Objects support sharing, capture, and synchronization. The WebMCP layer registers imperative tools through document.modelContext . The same internal tool definitions are used by the visible application, ensuring that agent actions follow the same rules as human actions. The board performs its own organization work in the browser: Transformers.js generates semantic embeddings in a Web Worker. Pairwise similarity creates a graph of related cards. Louvain community detection identifies clusters. D3 Force calculates spatial arrangements. Collision detection and empty-space placement prevent cards and widgets from overlapping. An RBush spatial index supports efficient selection and region queries. Rich media uses a lightweight facade until the user activates it, preventing large boards from loading many expensive embeds simultaneously. Documents and spreadsheets are parsed through lazy-loaded libraries, while drawing and document canvases can be rasterized into PNG images for multimodal agents. Stylus input uses Pointer Events, pressure data, pointer capture, coalesced events, palm rejection behavior, and hardware-eraser handling to improve writing on tablets. ChatGPT, OpenAI Codex, and Anthropic Claude Code were used during development for research, implementation, debugging, testing, and documentation. Challenges we ran into One of the hardest problems was deciding how much information should be structured data and how much should remain visual. Text cards are easier for an agent to process structurally, but handwriting, equations, diagrams, and overlapping annotations lose meaning when converted into isolated text. Our solution was hybrid: preserve structured card data while supplying selected drawings and document canvases as images. Selection also required careful design. On a large board, returning everything equally can waste the model’s context and make focused requests unreliable. We made human selection an explicit attention signal, returning selected cards first and combining selected drawings into a single cropped image. Spatial layout created another challenge. Simple force-directed layouts often produced overlapping clusters, particularly in masonry, cluster, and tree arrangements. We separated semantic intent from geometry: agents request an arrangement, while the browser calculates positions, measures real card dimensions, and searches for available space. Tablet drawing introduced a different class of input problems. Pen input, touch gestures, palm contact, scrolling, erasing, and card dragging can all compete for the same pointer events. Supporting pressure-sensitive writing without breaking ordinary mouse and touch interaction required dedicated pointer handling and repeated device-oriented testing. Finally, converting the complete board into an agent-readable image was more complicated than returning its SVG representation. We added real PNG rasterization so multimodal models receive an actual screenshot-like image containing cards, links, drawings, and spatial relationships. Accomplishments that we're proud of We are proud that Mundaneum treats WebMCP as part of the product’s interaction model rather than as a thin demonstration layer. The agent operates on meaningful concepts—cards, selections, links, groups, drawings, and arrangements—without controlling screen coordinates. This creates a clear division of responsibility: the person directs attention, the agent contributes understanding, and the application protects the visual workspace. We are also proud of the document canvas. It allows typed text, handwriting, shapes, and equations to coexist in one movable and resizable document while remaining readable by a multimodal agent. Other accomplishments include: Selection-aware agent context Multi-card selection through Shift-click Image-based transfer of selected drawings Whole-board PNG capture Copyable card IDs Non-overlapping card placement Resizable document and widget cards Pressure-sensitive stylus support Multiple browser-computed arrangements A shared tool registry for the interface and WebMCP A public, MIT-licensed implementation What we learned We learned that good agent interfaces are less about exposing every possible action and more about defining strong boundaries. Preventing agents from setting coordinates initially appeared restrictive, but it simplified the tool surface and made agent contributions safer. The agent expresses intent, while deterministic browser code handles geometry. We also learned that visual and structured context should complement each other. Screenshots are excellent for handwriting, diagrams, and spatial meaning, while structured data is better for exact text, IDs, links, and targeted actions. Combining both creates a much stronger interface than relying exclusively on either representation. Human selection proved to be a powerful form of context engineering. A simple Shift-click can communicate scope more reliably than a long prompt explaining which objects matter. Finally, pen input demonstrated how much interaction design depends on hardware. A drawing system that works with a mouse is not automatically usable with a stylus and touchscreen. What's next for Mundaneum Our immediate focus is testing and refining the complete WebMCP workflow across more browsers, board sizes, and tablet devices.
