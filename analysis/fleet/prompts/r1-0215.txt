# FROZEN SCORING RUBRIC (pre-registered before any Stage 1 review)

Scores are integers 1-10 on each of the FOUR official criteria. Every score carries:
rationale, evidence_surfaces, confidence (0-1). Reviewers use the full scale and these
band anchors verbatim. Category neutrality is absolute: a game, art piece, music tool,
CRM, weird toy, and developer tool are all eligible for 10/10 on every criterion if they
satisfy that criterion exceptionally well FOR WHAT THEY ARE TRYING TO BE.
No aesthetic penalties. Signs of hurried AI work matter only as actual product-quality
problems (incoherence, broken flow, placeholder content), never as style.

## WebMCP Leverage — what does WebMCP change for this product?
Judge the delta WebMCP makes to reliability, precision, structured state access,
shared human-agent state, repeatability, and capability. Tool COUNT is not leverage.
- 9-10 TRANSFORMATIVE: the intended experience is impractical without the structured
  agent surface; human and agent share live product state; the collaboration could not
  be replicated comparably well by a general agent driving the UI.
- 7-8 MAJOR DELTA: without WebMCP a general agent is markedly less reliable/precise
  here; the agent is central to the product, not a bolt-on.
- 5-6 MEANINGFUL DELTA: existing capability packaged into a genuinely better product
  experience via structured tools; same outcome otherwise possible with real friction.
- 3-4 MINOR DELTA: generic browser automation achieves comparable outcomes; WebMCP is
  a convenience layer.
- 1-2 COSMETIC: exposes an action a UI button already offers; no meaningful change.

## Execution — is the actual product coherent, intentional, complete for its scope?
Stage 1 scores EXECUTION_CLAIMED: coherence/intentionality/completeness as evidenced by
the packet (description + packaging + video frames/transcript). Thin evidence caps the
score (end-to-end credit requires end-to-end evidence) and lowers confidence.
Stage 2 may rescore as EXECUTION_OBSERVED; latest evidence-backed score replaces, never
sums.
- 9-10: all surfaces agree; central workflow demonstrably completes; states handled;
  feels finished for its stated scope.
- 7-8: strong coherence and intentionality; central workflow appears functional; minor
  unfinished edges.
- 5-6: plausibly real with visible gaps; scope unclear or core not demonstrated
  end-to-end.
- 3-4: thin prototype; contradictions between surfaces; placeholder content.
- 1-2: non-functional or incoherent for its claimed scope.

## Creativity & Ambition — novelty of concept and interaction model
Judge ONLY: novelty of the concept, novelty of the interaction model, originality
relative to existing concepts, ambition, memorable/surprising use of the medium, and
the depth with which the concept is pursued. WebMCP indispensability belongs under
WebMCP Leverage, NOT here: a project can be extremely creative even if another agent
API could theoretically have enabled something similar.
- 9-10: genuinely novel concept or interaction model; ambitious scope pursued deeply
  and coherently; memorable.
- 7-8: fresh synthesis or notably original interaction; ambitious for a hackathon.
- 5-6: competent take on a known category with a real new angle.
- 3-4: derivative; novelty mostly surface details.
- 1-2: clone/template with trivial changes.
"That the category exists" is never a penalty; judge the submitted concept.

## Potential Impact — credible value for the intended audience
Judge: is there a real audience; is there a real problem, desire, or use case for that
audience; does the demonstrated solution genuinely address it. NO commercial adoption,
monetization, market pull, or startup viability is required. Entertainment, play, art,
creative expression, learning, niche hobby use, experimentation, and community value
are legitimate forms of impact. A tiny audience fully justifies a top score.
- 9-10: specific real audience with a real problem/desire; solution demonstrably
  addresses it; credible value, appeal, usefulness, or meaning for that audience.
- 7-8: clear audience and problem, good fit, credible value.
- 5-6: identifiable audience, looser fit or partial address.
- 3-4: speculative audience or strained fit; impact mostly aspirational.
- 1-2: no coherent audience/problem pairing.
Audience size is NOT a multiplier.

## Aggregate & ranking
aggregate = leverage + execution + impact + creativity (4-40). Nothing else enters.
Tie-breaks: Leverage, then Execution, then Impact, then Creativity, then higher
combined evidence confidence, then slug alphabetical. See PROTOCOL.md Ranking section.

AI-native chemical modeling applications (WebMCP native) Like 1 Comment Story Updates COVERS A B What If an Agent Could Look at the Same Atom You Are Looking At? Inspiration We started with a slightly annoying observation. LLMs are becoming very good at operating software. They can call tools. They can edit files. They can write code. They can chain dozens of actions together. But put an agent inside a scientific 3D modeling environment and ask: “Move the molecule to that site.” …and suddenly “that” becomes the hardest word in the sentence. Humans do not think about molecular structures as lists of Cartesian coordinates. We look at a surface and say: “this atom” “the site on the right” “the layer underneath” “rotate it toward that oxygen” “not there — the other bridge site” Scientific modeling is deeply spatial, visual, and interactive. Language models, on the other hand, are usually handed something like: Atom 148: O 3.422 7.183 12.901 Atom 149: H 3.981 7.652 13.441 ... Technically correct. Also technically miserable. So we started asking a different question: What if the 3D viewport itself became a shared workspace between a human and an agent? That question became this project. What We Built We built WebMCP support for a scientific modeling environment covering molecular and condensed-matter structures. The goal is not simply to expose every GUI button as a tool. That would be useful, but it would still leave the agent fundamentally blind. Instead, we treat WebMCP as a bridge between three things: scientific structure → agent reasoning → human attention The agent can inspect the model, reason about its local environment, manipulate geometry, and — importantly — control what the human is looking at. That last part turned out to be surprisingly important. If the agent proposes an adsorption site, it should not respond with: “Use atoms 102, 117 and 118.” It should move the camera there. Highlight the atoms. Mark the candidate site. Then ask: “This one?” If the user says: “No, the one on the right.” the agent should be able to continue from the shared visual context. That is the interaction we wanted. Not human or agent modeling. Human with agent modeling. Shared Spatial Attention The concept we kept coming back to while building the project was something we now call shared spatial attention . An agent needs more than a scene dump. It needs ways to answer questions such as: What structure is currently visible? What has the user selected? Which atoms are nearby? Which direction is the surface normal? Which atoms belong to the top layer? Where are plausible adsorption sites? What did I just highlight? What does “the one beside it” refer to? What changed after my previous operation? And the agent also needs to influence the viewport: observe ↓ understand ↓ focus ↓ propose ↓ human confirms ↓ act ↓ observe again ↓ verify That loop is the core of the project. A successful MCP call does not necessarily mean the task succeeded. The resulting scientific structure still has to make sense. A Tiny Example That Contains the Whole Idea One of our favorite demos is intentionally simple: “Help me adsorb a water molecule on this surface. Ask me before important decisions.” The agent first inspects the surface. It identifies candidate sites. It focuses the viewport on one of them. The user rejects it: “Not this one. The one on the right.” The agent shifts focus to another candidate. The user confirms. The agent places H₂O using the oxygen as an anchor. Then the user says: “Point one hydrogen toward that oxygen.” The nearby oxygen is highlighted. The water molecule rotates. The geometry is checked again. No atom IDs. No coordinates. An autonomous AI coach helping children master handwriting through real-time stroke analysis and adaptive WebMCP tools. Like Comment Story Updates Home Dashboard : Welcome hub with daily handwriting challenges and AI Coach recommendations. Trace Mode (Letters) : Tactile canvas with guided stroke paths, numbered anchors, and 1-Click Agent Demo Numbers Practice : Numeracy tracing for digits 0–9 with stroke-order guidelines. Shapes Practice : Fine-motor coordination exercises for basic geometric shapes. Copy Mode : Freehand copying with faint proportion guides and no training wheels. Memory Mode : Unassisted writing on school guidelines to test active muscle memory. Stroke Analytics : 5-dimension real-time evaluation with circular score and equalizer metrics. WebMCP DevTools : Inspector showcasing 8 registered tools, Gemini 3.8 Flash, and live JSON-RPC logs. AI Remediation Loop : Success modal showing closed-loop AI error detection and adaptive correction. Progress & Analytics : Learner dashboard tracking accuracy heatmaps, streaks, and competency mastery. Rewards & Themes : Gamified achievement center with unlockable badges and colorful pen themes. Settings & Controls : Configuration for voice guidance, learner profiles, and WebMCP agent autonomy. Inspiration Watching young children learn to write can be both fascinating and heartbreaking. I often observed children drawing letters like "D" or "B" from bottom to top, or making awkward reverse loops. Visually, the final result might look somewhat acceptable on a static worksheet, but motor-memory-wise, a bad habit was already taking root. Existing handwriting apps on tablets simply check whether pixels overlap with a template. They don't understand how the child traced it, in which direction, or why they made a mistake. Meanwhile, parents and teachers simply don't have enough hours in the day to sit 1-on-1 for every single stroke. When OpenAI announced the WebMCP standard, a lightbulb went off: what if an AI didn't just passively look at a screenshot, but had direct, structured API tools on the web page to analyze mathematical stroke trajectories and dynamically adapt the canvas with live visual guides? That was the spark that drove me to build TRACEAI. What it does TRACEAI is an AI-native web application designed to act as an attentive, encouraging handwriting coach for kids. Here is how the experience works in practice: Child practices on a joyful, tactile canvas: Children can practice uppercase letters (A-Z), numbers (0-9), and basic geometric shapes across 3 distinct difficulty modes (Trace, Copy, and Memory). Instant 5-Dimension Trajectory Evaluation: As soon as a child lifts their pen or finger, TRACEAI analyzes the stroke geometry across 5 criteria: Direction & Stroke Order, Positioning, Precision, Continuity, and Line Respect. Autonomous AI Coaching via WebMCP: An AI agent inspects the child's learning history through registered WebMCP tools (get_weak_letters(), analyze_trace()). Real-time Interface Adaptation: When the agent detects an issue—for example, struggling with the curved stroke direction on the letter "D"—it calls generate_exercise("D", "direction"). The web page instantly reacts by drawing pulsating directional guide arrows and speaking warm, supportive vocal guidance. Gamified Positive Reinforcement: Points, stars, streak tracking, audio chimes, and confetti celebrations keep the child smiling and motivated. Built-in WebMCP Inspector: For hackathon judges and developers, an inspector panel provides live insight into the agent’s inner monologue, tool calls, JSON payloads, and a 1-click end-to-end demo scenario. How we built it I engineered TRACEAI as a solo project to be ultra-fast, responsive on touch devices (tablets/smartphones), and 100% compliant with the emerging W3C WebMCP standard: WebMCP Standard Tooling: I registered 8 structured tools directly onto document.modelContext.registerTool() with explicit JSON schemas. I also implemented a lightweight fallback polyfill so the app runs smoothly in both WebMCP-enabled browsers and standard modern browsers. Client-Side Geometric Engine (trace-analyzer.js): I built a vector analysis pipeline that calculates directional angle variances, Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) path distances, and bounding-box alignments in under 5ms right in the browser. Reactive Canvas & Visual Overlays (app.js): Built with pure HTML5 Canvas and Vanilla JavaScript. Dynamic arrow overlays and smooth Bezier curve approximations make the interface feel alive without heavy rendering libraries. Multi-Sensory Audio System: Utilizes the Web Speech API for natural text-to-speech coaching advice and the Web Audio API for rewarding synthesizer chimes and fanfares. Modern Glassmorphic UI: Crafted using modern, responsive CSS with cheerful, kid-friendly color palettes, accessible contrasts, and smooth micro-animations. Challenges we ran into Distinguishing Shape from Direction: A child drawing the letter "O" clockwise vs. counter-clockwise results in almost identical static shapes, but completely different motor habits. Designing a lightweight vector angle differential that accurately detects inverted stroke sequences was my trickiest algorithmic hurdle. Designing Agent-Friendly Tools: I initially tried passing raw coordinate streams to the LLM, but quickly realized that overwhelmed the context window and caused unnecessary latency. I shifted to a high-signal tool design where analyze_trace() and get_weak_letters() return clean, structured metrics (0.0 to 1.0 scores and explicit weakness classifications like "direction" vs "precision"), allowing the agent to reason instantly. Rendering Dynamic Guidance on Canvas: Translating the agent's high-level command (generate_exercise('D', 'direction')) into animated, pulsating SVG/Canvas arrows that follow the exact path of each letter required fine-tuning my character vector templates. Accomplishments that we're proud of A Real Closed-Loop Web Agent: Successfully building an AI experience where the agent doesn't just answer questions in a sidebar, but actively manipulates the application state and visuals to solve an educational challenge. Zero-Dependency Performance: Running the entire application (analysis, rendering, speech, tools) smoothly on client-side Vanilla JS with instant load times. The WebMCP Inspector & 1-Click Scenario: Enabling judges and testers to see the agent reasoning in real time and test the entire workflow with a single click. What we learned The sheer power of WebMCP: Moving away from DOM scraping and screenshot interpretation towards clean, declarative web tools is a paradigm shift for web development. It makes AI agents hundreds of times faster, deterministic, and much more resilient. How to bridge early-childhood pedagogy with autonomous agent decision-making. What's next for TRACEAI — AI Handwriting Coach powered by WebMCP Cursive Writing Support: Extending my vector stroke engine to support connected cursive handwriting and full words. Early Dysgraphia & Motor-Skill Insights: Providing anonymized progress reports that parents and educators can share with pediatric specialists to identify fine-motor coordination difficulties early. Multi-Child Classroom Dashboard: Enabling a teacher's AI copilot to monitor an entire classroom's practice sessions simultaneously and flag who needs personal attention.
