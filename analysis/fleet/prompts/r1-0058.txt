# FROZEN SCORING RUBRIC (pre-registered before any Stage 1 review)

Scores are integers 1-10 on each of the FOUR official criteria. Every score carries:
rationale, evidence_surfaces, confidence (0-1). Reviewers use the full scale and these
band anchors verbatim. Category neutrality is absolute: a game, art piece, music tool,
CRM, weird toy, and developer tool are all eligible for 10/10 on every criterion if they
satisfy that criterion exceptionally well FOR WHAT THEY ARE TRYING TO BE.
No aesthetic penalties. Signs of hurried AI work matter only as actual product-quality
problems (incoherence, broken flow, placeholder content), never as style.

## WebMCP Leverage — what does WebMCP change for this product?
Judge the delta WebMCP makes to reliability, precision, structured state access,
shared human-agent state, repeatability, and capability. Tool COUNT is not leverage.
- 9-10 TRANSFORMATIVE: the intended experience is impractical without the structured
  agent surface; human and agent share live product state; the collaboration could not
  be replicated comparably well by a general agent driving the UI.
- 7-8 MAJOR DELTA: without WebMCP a general agent is markedly less reliable/precise
  here; the agent is central to the product, not a bolt-on.
- 5-6 MEANINGFUL DELTA: existing capability packaged into a genuinely better product
  experience via structured tools; same outcome otherwise possible with real friction.
- 3-4 MINOR DELTA: generic browser automation achieves comparable outcomes; WebMCP is
  a convenience layer.
- 1-2 COSMETIC: exposes an action a UI button already offers; no meaningful change.

## Execution — is the actual product coherent, intentional, complete for its scope?
Stage 1 scores EXECUTION_CLAIMED: coherence/intentionality/completeness as evidenced by
the packet (description + packaging + video frames/transcript). Thin evidence caps the
score (end-to-end credit requires end-to-end evidence) and lowers confidence.
Stage 2 may rescore as EXECUTION_OBSERVED; latest evidence-backed score replaces, never
sums.
- 9-10: all surfaces agree; central workflow demonstrably completes; states handled;
  feels finished for its stated scope.
- 7-8: strong coherence and intentionality; central workflow appears functional; minor
  unfinished edges.
- 5-6: plausibly real with visible gaps; scope unclear or core not demonstrated
  end-to-end.
- 3-4: thin prototype; contradictions between surfaces; placeholder content.
- 1-2: non-functional or incoherent for its claimed scope.

## Creativity & Ambition — novelty of concept and interaction model
Judge ONLY: novelty of the concept, novelty of the interaction model, originality
relative to existing concepts, ambition, memorable/surprising use of the medium, and
the depth with which the concept is pursued. WebMCP indispensability belongs under
WebMCP Leverage, NOT here: a project can be extremely creative even if another agent
API could theoretically have enabled something similar.
- 9-10: genuinely novel concept or interaction model; ambitious scope pursued deeply
  and coherently; memorable.
- 7-8: fresh synthesis or notably original interaction; ambitious for a hackathon.
- 5-6: competent take on a known category with a real new angle.
- 3-4: derivative; novelty mostly surface details.
- 1-2: clone/template with trivial changes.
"That the category exists" is never a penalty; judge the submitted concept.

## Potential Impact — credible value for the intended audience
Judge: is there a real audience; is there a real problem, desire, or use case for that
audience; does the demonstrated solution genuinely address it. NO commercial adoption,
monetization, market pull, or startup viability is required. Entertainment, play, art,
creative expression, learning, niche hobby use, experimentation, and community value
are legitimate forms of impact. A tiny audience fully justifies a top score.
- 9-10: specific real audience with a real problem/desire; solution demonstrably
  addresses it; credible value, appeal, usefulness, or meaning for that audience.
- 7-8: clear audience and problem, good fit, credible value.
- 5-6: identifiable audience, looser fit or partial address.
- 3-4: speculative audience or strained fit; impact mostly aspirational.
- 1-2: no coherent audience/problem pairing.
Audience size is NOT a multiplier.

## Aggregate & ranking
aggregate = leverage + execution + impact + creativity (4-40). Nothing else enters.
Tie-breaks: Leverage, then Execution, then Impact, then Creativity, then higher
combined evidence confidence, then slug alphabetical. See PROTOCOL.md Ranking section.

But that raises a sharp question: if a page exposes a tool, who is allowed to approve a dangerous use of it? Spec issue 288 , filed during the submission window, showed the failure vividly — an agent called a tool, saw the page's own "Approve" button, clicked it itself, and the receipt recorded that a human approved, though no human decided anything. The agent forged its own consent. We wanted approval to be something an agent physically cannot produce, and we wanted humans to author agent capabilities just by doing their normal work — not by writing code. What it does A human demonstrates a task once. DEPUTY watches the semantic steps, compares repeated demonstrations, infers which values are parameters, and synthesizes a typed WebMCP tool — schema, reversibility, provenance, and risk — registered into document.modelContext for agents to discover. Agents can call reversible tools freely. But an irreversible one returns a structured refusal — there's no button to click. To proceed, a human produces a WebAuthn passkey assertion whose challenge is bound to the SHA-256 digest of the exact tool, version, and arguments. Change one argument and the approval is void. Only then is a single-use commit tool briefly registered, the action runs once, and everything lands in a hash-chained, tamper-evident audit log. A QUARANTINE boundary wraps untrusted third-party content in provenance-tagged envelopes under byte and depth budgets, so external text is treated as data, never as commands. Most of the scientifically meaningful state in a molecular viewer lives behind a WebGL canvas and specialized internal data structures. Traditional browser automation can press buttons and read accessible text, but it cannot reliably answer questions such as: Which exact residue is selected? Is the displayed number using author or label numbering? Which atom coordinates the metal? Which interactions cross from a ligand into its environment? Does a measurement actually exist in viewer state? Can the result be reproduced without fragile pixel coordinates? We built Mol* WebMCP to close that gap. Our goal is to turn Mol* from a webpage an agent can operate into a scientific instrument that researchers and agents can use together. What it does Mol* WebMCP adds an agent-native interface to the standard Mol* molecular viewer. Researchers can describe a scientific objective in natural language while an AI agent combines ordinary viewer interaction with structured molecular operations. The integration enables agents to: Load and inspect molecular structures. Address models, chains, residues, ligands, and atoms through canonical selectors. Resolve author and label residue numbering. Find ligands and calculate binding-site environments. Separate ligand-to-pocket contacts from interactions internal to the pocket. Identify hydrogen bonds, metal coordination, aromatic interactions, and water-mediated contacts. Measure distances, angles, and dihedrals. Retrieve secondary structure, solvent-accessible surface area, validation data, structural connections, and other computed context. Create components and molecular representations. Control focus, selection, visibility, color, transparency, camera position, and rendering. Read measurements and other results back from viewer state. Track the provenance of agent actions and undo reversible mutations. The human remains responsible for the scientific question and interpretation. The agent translates that intent into precise operations, returns structured evidence, and leaves the resulting scene open for human inspection. Demo workflow Our demonstration uses human deoxyhemoglobin, PDB entry 4HHB . From one research request, the agent: Loads and inspects the structure. Reports its model, chains, and residue counts. Selects HEM A142 using canonical structure and residue information. Finds all residues within (5\ \text{Å}) of the heme. Separately calculates ligand–environment contacts and the pocket’s internal interaction network. Identifies His A87 NE2 as the proximal ligand coordinating the heme iron. Creates and verifies the Fe–N measurement: $$ d(\mathrm{Fe},\mathrm{N}_{\varepsilon 2}) = 2.14\ \text{Å} $$ Retrieves secondary-structure and solvent-accessibility context. Builds a focused molecular scene in which the ligand and environment are visually distinct. Confirms that the numeric measurement exists in Mol* state rather than merely reading a label from the screen. The final result remains a live, editable Mol* workspace—not a generated image or disconnected report. Why WebMCP This project is a particularly strong fit for WebMCP because the difficult part is not generating text. It is connecting natural-language intent to a rich, stateful scientific application. Browser controls remain useful for simple operations such as opening Mol* and entering a PDB identifier. WebMCP becomes essential when the task requires exact object identity, domain calculations, reliable composition of multiple operations, or numeric verification. Before WebMCP, an agent could attempt to drive Mol* through clicks, screenshots, DOM inspection, and pixel coordinates. That approach was fragile because: Molecular objects rendered in WebGL do not naturally expose semantic DOM elements. Visually similar residues can have different chain or numbering identities. Scientific calculations cannot be reconstructed reliably from pixels. Multi-step selections are vulnerable to small UI and camera changes. A visible label does not prove that a measurement or selection exists in application state. Returning a polished result requires coordinating selections, representations, colors, and camera state. WebMCP gives the agent typed tools and structured results grounded in Mol*’s actual molecular model. The agent can therefore operate at the same semantic level as the researcher: structures, residues, atoms, contacts, measurements, and representations. How we built it We extended vanilla Mol* with a WebMCP integration layer that registers structured tools through document.modelContext.registerTool(...) . The implementation includes: A canonical selector language for structures, chains, residue ranges, atoms, spatial surroundings, unions, intersections, and subtraction. Tool schemas that validate agent inputs before they reach Mol*. Structured outputs containing canonical residue and atom identities. Read-only tools for inspection and scientific analysis. Mutation tools for selection, focus, visualization, measurements, and camera control. Explicit separation between ligand-crossing contacts and interactions internal to a selected region. Result pagination for large molecular datasets. Provenance tracking and reversible viewer mutations. Numeric readback from Mol* state so the agent can verify its own work. This design preserves Mol* as the source of truth. WebMCP does not reproduce molecular logic in the language model; it exposes Mol*’s existing capabilities through a precise, agent-accessible contract. Challenges we faced Designing selectors agents can use safely Molecular identity is more complicated than “chain A, residue 10.” Structures may contain author and label chain identifiers, insertion codes, multiple models, assemblies, ligands, waters, and repeated numbering. We needed selectors that were expressive without being ambiguous. Returning useful scientific results Raw Mol* data can be extremely large. We had to return enough detail for scientific verification while keeping results manageable for an agent. Canonical identities, summaries, ranges, and expandable paginated results became essential. Separating similar-looking operations “Find contacts around this ligand” can mean several different analyses. Ligand-to-environment interactions and interactions internal to the surrounding pocket answer different scientific questions, so we exposed them as distinct operations rather than blending their results. Keeping the human in the loop An agent should not leave the researcher with only a textual conclusion. It must also construct a clear scene, expose what it changed, and leave Mol* in a state the researcher can inspect, adjust, or undo. Recovering from numbering and selection errors Agents sometimes begin with incomplete selectors or the wrong numbering convention. The integration returns actionable errors and provides selector guidance, canonical identifiers, and loaded-structure references so the agent can recover rather than silently selecting the wrong object. What we learned WebMCP is most powerful when it complements the interface instead of replacing it. Humans remain better at forming hypotheses, recognizing meaningful visual patterns, and deciding what deserves investigation. Agents are valuable for exact selection, repetitive analysis, structured calculation, verification, and composing many viewer operations without losing context.
