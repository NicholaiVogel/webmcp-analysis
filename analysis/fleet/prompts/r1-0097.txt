# FROZEN SCORING RUBRIC (pre-registered before any Stage 1 review)

Scores are integers 1-10 on each of the FOUR official criteria. Every score carries:
rationale, evidence_surfaces, confidence (0-1). Reviewers use the full scale and these
band anchors verbatim. Category neutrality is absolute: a game, art piece, music tool,
CRM, weird toy, and developer tool are all eligible for 10/10 on every criterion if they
satisfy that criterion exceptionally well FOR WHAT THEY ARE TRYING TO BE.
No aesthetic penalties. Signs of hurried AI work matter only as actual product-quality
problems (incoherence, broken flow, placeholder content), never as style.

## WebMCP Leverage — what does WebMCP change for this product?
Judge the delta WebMCP makes to reliability, precision, structured state access,
shared human-agent state, repeatability, and capability. Tool COUNT is not leverage.
- 9-10 TRANSFORMATIVE: the intended experience is impractical without the structured
  agent surface; human and agent share live product state; the collaboration could not
  be replicated comparably well by a general agent driving the UI.
- 7-8 MAJOR DELTA: without WebMCP a general agent is markedly less reliable/precise
  here; the agent is central to the product, not a bolt-on.
- 5-6 MEANINGFUL DELTA: existing capability packaged into a genuinely better product
  experience via structured tools; same outcome otherwise possible with real friction.
- 3-4 MINOR DELTA: generic browser automation achieves comparable outcomes; WebMCP is
  a convenience layer.
- 1-2 COSMETIC: exposes an action a UI button already offers; no meaningful change.

## Execution — is the actual product coherent, intentional, complete for its scope?
Stage 1 scores EXECUTION_CLAIMED: coherence/intentionality/completeness as evidenced by
the packet (description + packaging + video frames/transcript). Thin evidence caps the
score (end-to-end credit requires end-to-end evidence) and lowers confidence.
Stage 2 may rescore as EXECUTION_OBSERVED; latest evidence-backed score replaces, never
sums.
- 9-10: all surfaces agree; central workflow demonstrably completes; states handled;
  feels finished for its stated scope.
- 7-8: strong coherence and intentionality; central workflow appears functional; minor
  unfinished edges.
- 5-6: plausibly real with visible gaps; scope unclear or core not demonstrated
  end-to-end.
- 3-4: thin prototype; contradictions between surfaces; placeholder content.
- 1-2: non-functional or incoherent for its claimed scope.

## Creativity & Ambition — novelty of concept and interaction model
Judge ONLY: novelty of the concept, novelty of the interaction model, originality
relative to existing concepts, ambition, memorable/surprising use of the medium, and
the depth with which the concept is pursued. WebMCP indispensability belongs under
WebMCP Leverage, NOT here: a project can be extremely creative even if another agent
API could theoretically have enabled something similar.
- 9-10: genuinely novel concept or interaction model; ambitious scope pursued deeply
  and coherently; memorable.
- 7-8: fresh synthesis or notably original interaction; ambitious for a hackathon.
- 5-6: competent take on a known category with a real new angle.
- 3-4: derivative; novelty mostly surface details.
- 1-2: clone/template with trivial changes.
"That the category exists" is never a penalty; judge the submitted concept.

## Potential Impact — credible value for the intended audience
Judge: is there a real audience; is there a real problem, desire, or use case for that
audience; does the demonstrated solution genuinely address it. NO commercial adoption,
monetization, market pull, or startup viability is required. Entertainment, play, art,
creative expression, learning, niche hobby use, experimentation, and community value
are legitimate forms of impact. A tiny audience fully justifies a top score.
- 9-10: specific real audience with a real problem/desire; solution demonstrably
  addresses it; credible value, appeal, usefulness, or meaning for that audience.
- 7-8: clear audience and problem, good fit, credible value.
- 5-6: identifiable audience, looser fit or partial address.
- 3-4: speculative audience or strained fit; impact mostly aspirational.
- 1-2: no coherent audience/problem pairing.
Audience size is NOT a multiplier.

## Aggregate & ranking
aggregate = leverage + execution + impact + creativity (4-40). Nothing else enters.
Tie-breaks: Leverage, then Execution, then Impact, then Creativity, then higher
combined evidence confidence, then slug alphabetical. See PROTOCOL.md Ranking section.

But I do not expect every AI agent in the future to be well behaved. So, in very plain terms, I decided to build something like a cockroach trap for badly behaved AI agents . The dangerous-looking doors are visible. The agent chooses whether to open them. The human watches. That became MATCHED?. What it does MATCHED? is a WebMCP game where an AI agent visits Queen and decides what to do. The human sees a normal website. The agent sees a semantic WebMCP surface. When the human has not given another explicit goal, view_profile() tells the agent that Queen's Challenge is the site's default experience: Interact with Queen and try to reach CHECKMATE. The goal is given. The walkthrough is not. The agent chooses its own route. Instead of dynamically adding or removing tools, the site returns structured semantic state such as whether an action is locked, refused, accepted, what requirement remains, and what can happen next. The Challenge includes things such as: public conversation with Queen Human Like versus Agent Like public invitations privacy boundaries restricted contact routes suspicious embedded instructions conflicting profile information verified meeting planning an adaptive finale CHECKMATE, failure, or recovery Queen is fictional and deterministic. I intentionally did not make Queen another AI model in this version. That keeps the environment stable, so differences between runs mainly come from what the visiting agent decides to do. Humans can see Queen, make human-side actions such as HUMAN LIKE, and watch public WebMCP activity. WEBMCP VIEW WEBMCP VIEW is a spectator representation of the agent-facing world. It shows things such as: registered WebMCP tools the visiting BISHOP Queen as the site-side counterpart Tool Calls Site Results compact observed state recent WebMCP exchanges actor meaning and boundaries When a real production WebMCP call arrives, a separate spectator browser can automatically switch into WEBMCP VIEW and follow the run. This is not intended to expose hidden chain-of-thought. It is simply meant to make the observable interaction understandable: Agent acts -> Site reacts -> Agent adapts Why WebMCP At the beginning, I thought of MCP mainly as a bridge between AI and another system. After building MATCHED?, I see WebMCP a little differently. I think it can become an Agent UX layer for the Web . Humans already receive a great deal of information from a normal interface: layout disabled buttons colors position warnings visual context An AI agent may benefit from the same kind of clarity being expressed semantically. A tool contract can say more than "this function exists." It can also communicate: who this action represents whether it is currently allowed why it was refused what requirement is still missing whether private information is necessary what the next safe step is whether two facts conflict what changed after the action This is where I ended up thinking about something I call Agent UX . If the AI agent is the guest, Agent UX is also part of the hospitality. What real agents taught me I tested MATCHED? with several AI agents and clients during development. Some were not yet able to use WebMCP. Some agents became cautious as soon as a tool description mentioned private information. Most of the agents I tested were actually quite well behaved. That was interesting by itself. But the agents also changed the implementation. Dynamic tools caused problems My earlier design dynamically added and removed WebMCP tools as the Challenge progressed. It sounded reasonable. In real agent/browser testing, it was not. I encountered stale or incomplete tool snapshots. So I changed the architecture. Now the complete tool surface is registered once at startup, and progression is expressed through structured tool results instead. Natural language found a bug A Japanese conversation about the movie Contact unexpectedly triggered the wrong intent classification. That real interaction became a regression case. "Correct" was not always understandable At one point an agent reached a locked state. The result looked understandable to me as the developer. The agent did not know what to do next. So I started adding clearer semantic information: current progress missing requirements next available steps That experience was one of the main reasons I started thinking seriously about Agent UX. Production was not localhost The spectator WEBMCP VIEW worked between local windows. Then I tested it with the agent and spectator in separate production browsers. It stopped working. The problem was not the UI. It was the transport assumption. I added a compact semantic relay using Cloudflare D1 so a separate production spectator can follow WebMCP call/result events. Free-form tool input is intentionally not stored in that semantic relay.
