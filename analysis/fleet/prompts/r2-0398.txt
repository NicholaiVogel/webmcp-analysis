Scores are integers 1-10 on each of the FOUR official criteria. Every score carries:
rationale, evidence_surfaces, confidence (0-1). Reviewers use the full scale and these
band anchors verbatim. Category neutrality is absolute: a game, art piece, music tool,
CRM, weird toy, and developer tool are all eligible for 10/10 on every criterion if they

No aesthetic penalties. Signs of hurried AI work matter only as actual product-quality
problems (incoherence, broken flow, placeholder content), never as style.

## WebMCP Leverage — what does WebMCP change for this product?
Judge the delta WebMCP makes to reliability, precision, structured state access,
shared human-agent state, repeatability, and capability. Tool COUNT is not leverage.
- 9-10 TRANSFORMATIVE: the intended experience is impractical without the structured
  agent surface; human and agent share live product state; the collaboration could not
  be replicated comparably well by a general agent driving the UI.
- 7-8 MAJOR DELTA: without WebMCP a general agent is markedly less reliable/precise
  here; the agent is central to the product, not a bolt-on.
- 5-6 MEANINGFUL DELTA: existing capability packaged into a genuinely better product
  experience via structured tools; same outcome otherwise possible with real friction.
- 3-4 MINOR DELTA: generic browser automation achieves comparable outcomes; WebMCP is
  a convenience layer.
- 1-2 COSMETIC: exposes an action a UI button already offers; no meaningful change.

## Execution — is the actual product coherent, intentional, complete for its scope?
Stage 1 scores EXECUTION_CLAIMED: coherence/intentionality/completeness as evidenced by
the packet (description + packaging + video frames/transcript). Thin evidence caps the
score (end-to-end credit requires end-to-end evidence) and lowers confidence.
Stage 2 may rescore as EXECUTION_OBSERVED; latest evidence-backed score replaces, never
sums.
- 9-10: all surfaces agree; central workflow demonstrably completes; states handled;
  feels finished for its stated scope.
- 7-8: strong coherence and intentionality; central workflow appears functional; minor
  unfinished edges.
- 5-6: plausibly real with visible gaps; scope unclear or core not demonstrated
  end-to-end.
- 3-4: thin prototype; contradictions between surfaces; placeholder content.
- 1-2: non-functional or incoherent for its claimed scope.

## Creativity & Ambition — novelty of concept and interaction model
Judge ONLY: novelty of the concept, novelty of the interaction model, originality
relative to existing concepts, ambition, memorable/surprising use of the medium, and
the depth with which the concept is pursued. WebMCP indispensability belongs under
WebMCP Leverage, NOT here: a project can be extremely creative even if another agent
API could theoretically have enabled something similar.
- 9-10: genuinely novel concept or interaction model; ambitious scope pursued deeply
  and coherently; memorable.
- 7-8: fresh synthesis or notably original interaction; ambitious for a hackathon.
- 5-6: competent take on a known category with a real new angle.
- 3-4: derivative; novelty mostly surface details.
- 1-2: clone/template with trivial changes.
"That the category exists" is never a penalty; judge the submitted concept.

## Potential Impact — credible value for the intended audience
Judge: is there a real audience; is there a real problem, desire, or use case for that

Audience size is NOT a multiplier.

What Vouchsafe is Two origins. Your file lives on one; a letting agency lives on the other. Your facts never leave your browser. The agency asks a question through WebMCP and gets one word back. The interesting part is what happens when you change your mind. Withdraw a permission and the tool is not disabled on the agency's side, it is deregistered . Authority here is not a permission check. It is whether the tool exists. A withheld permission is not a guarded tool that refuses; there is no tool, so it cannot be called, guessed at, or talked into existence by anything written into a page or a prompt. How it is built Vanilla ES modules. No build step, no framework, no runtime dependencies, and zero third-party requests from either origin, which is enforced by a test rather than asserted in a README. The cross-origin round trip is the part we could not find anyone else doing in full: Your file registers each allowed question with exposedTo naming the agency's origin. The agency calls getTools({fromOrigins}) and discovers them. It re-registers each discovered tool as a local tool, so an agent sees one flat toolset and never has to know two websites were involved. On toolchange , it diffs and tears down proxies for anything that has stopped being offered. Step 3 and step 4 are what make revocation land mid-conversation. Things we found out about Chrome's implementation We wrote these down with reproduction steps because we could not find them documented anywhere. getTools() hands back inputSchema as a JSON string , but registerTool() demands an object . Re-registering a discovered tool fails silently until you parse it. getTools({fromOrigins}) also returns your own tools, so a naive proxy loop registers proxies for its own proxies. A cross-origin iframe's tools do not appear in the host's plain getTools() . You have to ask for them by origin. Federation is scoped to the frame tree, not the browser. toolchange fires once per registration, not once per batch, so a nine permission grant is nine events and the handler has to be idempotent. Revocation crosses the origin boundary with no channel of our own. The browser does it. The declarative form API derives the schema from the form's own inputs. A governed proceeding is a state machine with sharp edges: you may seal a position now and not open it; you may open it now and not seal a new one; a ruling may be ratified only once and only from Ruling. Every other agent integration expresses that as validation — a tool that exists always and refuses most of the time. WebMCP lets us express it as existence . In Commit, commit_to_round is registered and reveal_in_round is not. Advance, and commit_to_round is gone — unregistered by aborting its AbortSignal , not hidden, not stubbed, not returning an error. Where the browser fires toolchange the badge moves with it; where it does not — ChatGPT's in-app browser has no addEventListener on modelContext — the tool list still changes, because the surface genuinely changed and the render does not depend on the event. The page says which case it is in rather than implying the stronger one. That is a rule an agent cannot argue with, cannot be prompted past, and does not have to be told about. It reads the tools it has. The second fit is subtler and is why this project exists. When an agent rides your browser session, its acts and yours arrive inside the same authenticated session . Existing audit tooling records one agent's actions for its own operator. Nothing records acts between parties in a shared venue — and at the WebMCP door, for the first time, a page can see which path an act arrived by. That is not full attribution. It is more than anyone had, and it is exactly enough to record honestly. How it creates a better user experience By refusing to overstate what it knows. Every ledger entry carries three fields: seat identity , ingress path , and an evidence grade — client-asserted , server-observed , or inherited . The grade is derived from the door the act came through, never passed in by the caller, and the interface never renders an attribution stronger than its grade . An act arriving with no grade is refused outright. You can watch this work rather than take it on faith: The commit-reveal check runs off-page in a Cloudflare Pages Function, so the entry is graded server-observed . When that function is unreachable, the check falls back into the page, grades itself down to client-asserted , and states the reason in its own output. A silent fallback would let a strong label survive a check that never left the browser. Every status chip on the page says what it cannot do. Cross-origin exposure unavailable in this browser? The chip says so, and no cross-org claim is made. Verification runs in your browser, over the whole chain, and can fail: the test suite includes altered content, a severed link, and the rehash-one-entry repair attempt that still breaks every entry after it. A verifier that cannot fail proves nothing. The result is an interface a sceptical person can use as an instrument instead of as a claim. That is the better experience: not more confidence, but confidence you can check. What people and agents can do together that was difficult before Share a venue and leave a record neither party controls. Before WebMCP, an agent acting on a web app was indistinguishable from its human at the door — the session was the identity, and the page had no view of the route. Take Five demonstrates three things that become possible when the page declares the surface: An agent cannot read the room without leaving a mark. The read tools are themselves acts. Ask the agent to read the ledger and the read appears on the ledger , graded and attributed, between the two calls it made. The ask and the answer stay separate. ratify_ruling is registered only in Ruling and cannot ratify . It raises a request and blocks on a human. Two entries land, never one — the ask is the agent's, through the WebMCP door; the answer is the host's, through the UI door. Collapsing them would credit an agent with a decision it only asked for. A declined ratification stays on the chain permanently. The division of labour is legible on the record, not just in the prose: the human sets the question, advances the phases and rules; the agent acts through the tools; the chain records both, each through its own door. Neither party can perform the other's acts without the ledger saying which door it came through. A claim can cross an organisational boundary and stay labelled as borrowed.
