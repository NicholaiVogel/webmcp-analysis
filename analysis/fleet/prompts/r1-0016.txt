# FROZEN SCORING RUBRIC (pre-registered before any Stage 1 review)

Scores are integers 1-10 on each of the FOUR official criteria. Every score carries:
rationale, evidence_surfaces, confidence (0-1). Reviewers use the full scale and these
band anchors verbatim. Category neutrality is absolute: a game, art piece, music tool,
CRM, weird toy, and developer tool are all eligible for 10/10 on every criterion if they
satisfy that criterion exceptionally well FOR WHAT THEY ARE TRYING TO BE.
No aesthetic penalties. Signs of hurried AI work matter only as actual product-quality
problems (incoherence, broken flow, placeholder content), never as style.

## WebMCP Leverage — what does WebMCP change for this product?
Judge the delta WebMCP makes to reliability, precision, structured state access,
shared human-agent state, repeatability, and capability. Tool COUNT is not leverage.
- 9-10 TRANSFORMATIVE: the intended experience is impractical without the structured
  agent surface; human and agent share live product state; the collaboration could not
  be replicated comparably well by a general agent driving the UI.
- 7-8 MAJOR DELTA: without WebMCP a general agent is markedly less reliable/precise
  here; the agent is central to the product, not a bolt-on.
- 5-6 MEANINGFUL DELTA: existing capability packaged into a genuinely better product
  experience via structured tools; same outcome otherwise possible with real friction.
- 3-4 MINOR DELTA: generic browser automation achieves comparable outcomes; WebMCP is
  a convenience layer.
- 1-2 COSMETIC: exposes an action a UI button already offers; no meaningful change.

## Execution — is the actual product coherent, intentional, complete for its scope?
Stage 1 scores EXECUTION_CLAIMED: coherence/intentionality/completeness as evidenced by
the packet (description + packaging + video frames/transcript). Thin evidence caps the
score (end-to-end credit requires end-to-end evidence) and lowers confidence.
Stage 2 may rescore as EXECUTION_OBSERVED; latest evidence-backed score replaces, never
sums.
- 9-10: all surfaces agree; central workflow demonstrably completes; states handled;
  feels finished for its stated scope.
- 7-8: strong coherence and intentionality; central workflow appears functional; minor
  unfinished edges.
- 5-6: plausibly real with visible gaps; scope unclear or core not demonstrated
  end-to-end.
- 3-4: thin prototype; contradictions between surfaces; placeholder content.
- 1-2: non-functional or incoherent for its claimed scope.

## Creativity & Ambition — novelty of concept and interaction model
Judge ONLY: novelty of the concept, novelty of the interaction model, originality
relative to existing concepts, ambition, memorable/surprising use of the medium, and
the depth with which the concept is pursued. WebMCP indispensability belongs under
WebMCP Leverage, NOT here: a project can be extremely creative even if another agent
API could theoretically have enabled something similar.
- 9-10: genuinely novel concept or interaction model; ambitious scope pursued deeply
  and coherently; memorable.
- 7-8: fresh synthesis or notably original interaction; ambitious for a hackathon.
- 5-6: competent take on a known category with a real new angle.
- 3-4: derivative; novelty mostly surface details.
- 1-2: clone/template with trivial changes.
"That the category exists" is never a penalty; judge the submitted concept.

## Potential Impact — credible value for the intended audience
Judge: is there a real audience; is there a real problem, desire, or use case for that
audience; does the demonstrated solution genuinely address it. NO commercial adoption,
monetization, market pull, or startup viability is required. Entertainment, play, art,
creative expression, learning, niche hobby use, experimentation, and community value
are legitimate forms of impact. A tiny audience fully justifies a top score.
- 9-10: specific real audience with a real problem/desire; solution demonstrably
  addresses it; credible value, appeal, usefulness, or meaning for that audience.
- 7-8: clear audience and problem, good fit, credible value.
- 5-6: identifiable audience, looser fit or partial address.
- 3-4: speculative audience or strained fit; impact mostly aspirational.
- 1-2: no coherent audience/problem pairing.
Audience size is NOT a multiplier.

## Aggregate & ranking
aggregate = leverage + execution + impact + creativity (4-40). Nothing else enters.
Tie-breaks: Leverage, then Execution, then Impact, then Creativity, then higher
combined evidence confidence, then slug alphabetical. See PROTOCOL.md Ranking section.

I wanted to explore a different relationship. What if the human and the AI controlled different sides of the same software? Games and simulations made that idea immediately visible. The engine keeps the rules. What it does LIFE//SHIFT is an adaptive life simulation built around one shared beginning and many possible lives. Every player starts as the same fictional 18-year-old: a coffee-shop barista in the small town of Vesper Beach. From there, four chapters carry that life from 18 to 60: 18–28 · 28–40 · 40–50 · 50–60 In each chapter, the player makes one decision in Work , one in Growth , and one in Life . Those choices do not come from a static decision tree. The available decisions evolve with the life being built. Growth and Life choices change with age and history. Work options can narrow as the decades pass because decisions accumulate and some exits become harder later in life. The direct consequences are deterministic. Cash, income, freedom, stability, wellbeing, skills, career progression, and future eligibility are calculated by the simulation engine. But the world around that life is adaptive. After a chapter resolves, a Director Cycle opens. An external AI Director can use native WebMCP tools to inspect what happened and decide whether the world should respond. It may introduce at most one bounded external change: a job opportunity, setback, financial shock or windfall, a door opened by another person, a housing event, a business opportunity, or a broader shift in conditions such as hiring, housing, or demand. The AI chooses the context and reason. The engine chooses the consequences. There is no setLifeState . The Director cannot choose the player's Work, Growth, or Life decisions. It cannot invent a salary or directly rewrite the simulation. And sometimes the correct response is no response at all. Observe-only is a valid Director outcome. The collaboration is intentionally asymmetric: The human owns the decisions. The AI owns bounded circumstances. The deterministic engine owns the rules. Same beginning. Different decisions. Different life. How we built it LIFE//SHIFT separates authority rather than giving the AI control of the entire simulation. The deterministic engine owns authoritative state, chapter progression, eligibility, economics, compounding consequences, and which decisions can appear next. WebMCP is the capability layer for the AI Director. The page registers native, schema-defined WebMCP tools that expose specific capabilities rather than requiring the agent to navigate or reverse-engineer the interface. Read tools expose structured context including: life state and history decision profile open threads finances and career world conditions available opportunities Director budget the pending Director Cycle Mutation tools expose a deliberately small set of world interventions. Every mutation is bound to the current cycleId and paid from a Director Budget. Inputs are validated, qualities and severities are constrained to defined values, and the deterministic engine maps the Director's semantic choice to actual numerical effects. After each chapter resolves, the Director Cycle becomes available. In a WebMCP-capable host such as ChatGPT, the agent can inspect get_pending_director_cycle , gather whatever additional context it needs through the read tools, and then choose zero or one intervention. If everything were pre-authored, the rules would be reliable, but the experience would eventually collapse into another branching decision tree. The solution was to split authority. Player decisions stay human. Their direct consequences stay deterministic. The AI Director receives a bounded surface for influencing external circumstances, with at most one intervention per chapter and a limited budget. Another challenge came directly from the WebMCP interaction model: exposing tools does not automatically wake an external agent when application state changes. We solved that with the Director Cycle. When a chapter resolves, LIFE//SHIFT creates an explicit cycle containing the new state and the boundary for the Director's turn. The places worth your time, now agent-native. A scored city guide where people and their agents plan a night out together, in the same page. Like Comment Story Updates What it is Dim Hour is a city guide - the places worth your time, scored, not starred - covering more than 19,000 restaurants, bars, hotels, and venues across 24 cities. For this challenge, dimhour.com became agent-native: the page itself registers five WebMCP tools, so an agent browsing the site can search the catalog, read a venue, check what is open tonight, draft an itinerary, and hand back a booking link - without scraping a pixel. Why this use case is a strong fit for WebMCP Planning a night out is one of the most common things people already ask an agent to help with, but today the agent scrapes pages and guesses at what the site already knows. WebMCP lets dimhour.com hand the agent its real search, scoring, and booking data as structured tools, in the same tab where the person is browsing. The catalog is the product; WebMCP makes it callable. Because the tools are thin adapters over a production venue API, every agent answer is grounded in live data instead of page scraping. How it creates a better user experience One page, two users. A person browsing the Dallas guide and their agent now work on the same surface. The agent calls the site's own tools and gets back the same scored venues, hours, and booking links the UI shows - no copy-pasting between a chat window and a browser, and no agent inventing hours, addresses, or availability. The person keeps the judgment (taste, swaps, vetoes); the agent does the legwork (searching, filtering, checking, assembling). The site stays the source of truth for both of them, and saving a plan stays a human action - the tools read, they never write. What people and agents can do together Co-plan a night in one sitting, in one page. "Dinner in Deep Ellum for four on Friday, then cocktails within walking distance." The agent searches the scored catalog, filters to what is actually open tonight using the same rule the site's own tonight strip uses, pulls full venue records, and drafts an ordered itinerary with real booking links (Resy, OpenTable, SevenRooms, Tock) while the person watches - then interrupts: "swap the second stop for something quieter," and the agent re-plans around the constraint. Before WebMCP, the agent could read the page but not act through the site's own logic, and the person could use the site but not delegate the legwork. Now both happen at once, and the result is a plan with real reservation links attached, not a list of suggestions. How we implemented WebMCP The WebMCP layer is one feature-detected script, webmcp.js, loaded by the site's index.html. Each of the five tools is a thin adapter that POSTs JSON-RPC tools/call to Dim Hour's existing MCP server (mcp.dimhour.com) and passes the result straight through: search_venues - one strong keyword plus city, neighborhood, cuisine, score floor, and price ceiling filters get_venue - the full record: hours, score, booking path, awards find_open_tonight - venues with a happy hour or late kitchen, best score first, using the same definition the site's own "tonight" strip uses, so the agent and the page agree build_itinerary - an ordered multi-stop plan with why and how to book each stop; saving stays a human action at /trip.html get_booking_link - the reservation platform and direct booking URL for a venue, returned for the agent to present; it never opens or books anything itself The script handles the in-flight standard rename (document.modelContext first, navigator.modelContext fallback) and is a silent no-op where no WebMCP host exists. No authentication; the tools expose only public, read-only catalog data. What predates the hackathon: the venue catalog, scoring pipeline, MCP server, and site. What was built during the submission window: webmcp.js, the tool schemas, and the index.html include - the public repo's README draws that line explicitly, with dated commits. Challenges we hit Search results deliberately do not carry hours - only full venue records do - so "open tonight" meant ranking candidates first, then reading the top few individually rather than filtering a field that is not there. Booking links live in the page's own catalog rather than the API, so the booking tool merges both sources and says so honestly when a city is not loaded. The verification suite now asserts substance (tonight actually finds venues; a booking link equals the catalog's own URL), not just shape - because an early version passed every check while two tools quietly returned nothing.
