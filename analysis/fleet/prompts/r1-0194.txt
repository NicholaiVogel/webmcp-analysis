# FROZEN SCORING RUBRIC (pre-registered before any Stage 1 review)

Scores are integers 1-10 on each of the FOUR official criteria. Every score carries:
rationale, evidence_surfaces, confidence (0-1). Reviewers use the full scale and these
band anchors verbatim. Category neutrality is absolute: a game, art piece, music tool,
CRM, weird toy, and developer tool are all eligible for 10/10 on every criterion if they
satisfy that criterion exceptionally well FOR WHAT THEY ARE TRYING TO BE.
No aesthetic penalties. Signs of hurried AI work matter only as actual product-quality
problems (incoherence, broken flow, placeholder content), never as style.

## WebMCP Leverage — what does WebMCP change for this product?
Judge the delta WebMCP makes to reliability, precision, structured state access,
shared human-agent state, repeatability, and capability. Tool COUNT is not leverage.
- 9-10 TRANSFORMATIVE: the intended experience is impractical without the structured
  agent surface; human and agent share live product state; the collaboration could not
  be replicated comparably well by a general agent driving the UI.
- 7-8 MAJOR DELTA: without WebMCP a general agent is markedly less reliable/precise
  here; the agent is central to the product, not a bolt-on.
- 5-6 MEANINGFUL DELTA: existing capability packaged into a genuinely better product
  experience via structured tools; same outcome otherwise possible with real friction.
- 3-4 MINOR DELTA: generic browser automation achieves comparable outcomes; WebMCP is
  a convenience layer.
- 1-2 COSMETIC: exposes an action a UI button already offers; no meaningful change.

## Execution — is the actual product coherent, intentional, complete for its scope?
Stage 1 scores EXECUTION_CLAIMED: coherence/intentionality/completeness as evidenced by
the packet (description + packaging + video frames/transcript). Thin evidence caps the
score (end-to-end credit requires end-to-end evidence) and lowers confidence.
Stage 2 may rescore as EXECUTION_OBSERVED; latest evidence-backed score replaces, never
sums.
- 9-10: all surfaces agree; central workflow demonstrably completes; states handled;
  feels finished for its stated scope.
- 7-8: strong coherence and intentionality; central workflow appears functional; minor
  unfinished edges.
- 5-6: plausibly real with visible gaps; scope unclear or core not demonstrated
  end-to-end.
- 3-4: thin prototype; contradictions between surfaces; placeholder content.
- 1-2: non-functional or incoherent for its claimed scope.

## Creativity & Ambition — novelty of concept and interaction model
Judge ONLY: novelty of the concept, novelty of the interaction model, originality
relative to existing concepts, ambition, memorable/surprising use of the medium, and
the depth with which the concept is pursued. WebMCP indispensability belongs under
WebMCP Leverage, NOT here: a project can be extremely creative even if another agent
API could theoretically have enabled something similar.
- 9-10: genuinely novel concept or interaction model; ambitious scope pursued deeply
  and coherently; memorable.
- 7-8: fresh synthesis or notably original interaction; ambitious for a hackathon.
- 5-6: competent take on a known category with a real new angle.
- 3-4: derivative; novelty mostly surface details.
- 1-2: clone/template with trivial changes.
"That the category exists" is never a penalty; judge the submitted concept.

## Potential Impact — credible value for the intended audience
Judge: is there a real audience; is there a real problem, desire, or use case for that
audience; does the demonstrated solution genuinely address it. NO commercial adoption,
monetization, market pull, or startup viability is required. Entertainment, play, art,
creative expression, learning, niche hobby use, experimentation, and community value
are legitimate forms of impact. A tiny audience fully justifies a top score.
- 9-10: specific real audience with a real problem/desire; solution demonstrably
  addresses it; credible value, appeal, usefulness, or meaning for that audience.
- 7-8: clear audience and problem, good fit, credible value.
- 5-6: identifiable audience, looser fit or partial address.
- 3-4: speculative audience or strained fit; impact mostly aspirational.
- 1-2: no coherent audience/problem pairing.
Audience size is NOT a multiplier.

## Aggregate & ranking
aggregate = leverage + execution + impact + creativity (4-40). Nothing else enters.
Tie-breaks: Leverage, then Execution, then Impact, then Creativity, then higher
combined evidence confidence, then slug alphabetical. See PROTOCOL.md Ranking section.

ABOUT TEXT (untrusted evidence):
Brand Portals explain brands to humans working in marketing, who then prompt their AI systems. In this project we expose the brand rules directly via WebMCP to agents as tools they can directly call. Like Comment Story Updates Inspiration Brand portals are built for human reading. When someone wants an AI assistant to apply their guidance, the usual workflow is surprisingly fragile: find the relevant page, interpret it, copy fragments into a prompt, and hope the important context survives. This especially leads to errors and failures when external suppliers, such as marketing agencies interpret the Brand Portal of their clients. Enactive Brand Portal explores a different model. The website remains a real, human-readable brand book, while WebMCP makes the active page available to a visiting assistant as structured capability. The goal is to remove the repeated manual retransmission of brand guidance without removing human intent, factual responsibility, or judgment. What it does The public site presents Enactive Wonderland, an original Carroll-inspired fictional creative house. Visitors can browse its brand foundation, core voice, six character communication modes, and pen-and-ink image world like an ordinary brand portal. On applicable pages, a compatible browser exposes Site Tools derived from that exact page. For the canonical language journey, the person opens the Cheshire Cat page and supplies a communication goal and source facts. The assistant: retrieves the active-page guidance; prepares a structured Language Studio brief; writes one complete draft; stages the result back in the portal. The Studio then displays the original source, proposed draft, material changes, applied rules, rationale, warnings, and ordinary human controls. The person can edit, keep, discard, copy, or reset the work. No tool approves or publishes anything. The same pattern is implemented for visual work. The assistant can prepare and stage a generator-neutral image prompt package containing positive guidance, negative constraints, composition, alt text, and applied rules. The portal does not pretend that it generated or approved an image. Why WebMCP WebMCP tools change the ontological status of the brand rules from static text to executable affordances by the agent. These can be offered context dependent, so that the right tool is available on the right page of the brand portal. Opening Cheshire Cat provides strategic reframing. Opening White Rabbit provides lucid urgency. Opening Image World provides linework, composition, colour, and provenance guidance. WebMCP lets the assistant use that structured active-page context without DOM scraping or requiring the person to reconstruct the brand rules in every prompt. Page and session state determine which tools are available. After a copy brief is prepared, the staging tool appears as the next session affordance. Without Site Tools, the website remains a useful brand book and manual briefing and review surface. With Site Tools, the assistant can perform the substantive transformation and return it to the same browser session for visible human review. How we built it Enactive Brand Portal is a static React and Vite application written in TypeScript. Its source of truth is a four-layer JSON-LD knowledge model: Brand meaning; portal-edition content; generic product and WebMCP contracts; traceability and acceptance evidence. The top-level page registers exactly nine browser Site Tools using: document.modelContext.registerTool(...) Five tools provide read-only brand and guidance access. Four prepare or stage session-local work. Local-effect tools update visible React state and sessionStorage only. There is no remote MCP server, backend, API route, database, CMS, authentication layer, embedded model, or runtime network dependency. Challenges I ran into The hardest product challenge was keeping the brand and the technology separate. An earlier version technically worked but made the public experience feel like a WebMCP demonstrator rather than a useful brand portal. I rebuilt the content architecture so that Enactive Wonderland remains the subject and WebMCP appears only as a restrained application capability. Other challenges included route-specific tool availability, dynamically adding the staging affordance after preparation, cancellation handling, output-size limits in a real host, preserving context across Studio navigation, and distinguishing native browser evidence from a local handler harness. I also had to keep every claim honest: deterministic copy checks are partial, staged image work is a prompt package rather than an image, and the assistant proposes while the person decides. Accomplishments A coherent human-facing brand portal that works without WebMCP. Nine page- and session-aware browser Site Tools. A real compatible-host copy journey covering discovery, guidance retrieval, brief preparation, dynamic staging, visible review, and reload persistence. A fully static architecture with immutable brand knowledge. Human-readable source, diff, rule, rationale, and warning inspection. Automated graph, schema, architecture, layer-separation, unit, accessibility, and browser checks. A public Vercel deployment with ten working direct routes. A public Apache-2.0 repository with recorded content and asset provenance. What I learned WebMCP is most valuable when the active page is not merely navigation but meaningful task context. The strongest interaction was not “an agent can click the website.” It was that the website could expose purpose-built, state-aware affordances while remaining understandable and useful to a person. We also learned that local tool execution is not equivalent to native browser proof, and that agent capability should stop before consequential human decisions such as approval or publication. What's next Next steps include reproducing the complete image-prompt journey in additional native hosts, testing compatibility across more WebMCP-enabled browsers, extracting the generic portal engine for other governed knowledge systems, and measuring the effect on task time, omitted rules, correction count, and reviewer confidence. The same pattern could be applied to design systems, policy portals, learning systems, editorial standards, and other websites whose knowledge currently stops at reading and downloading.
