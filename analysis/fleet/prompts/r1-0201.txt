# FROZEN SCORING RUBRIC (pre-registered before any Stage 1 review)

Scores are integers 1-10 on each of the FOUR official criteria. Every score carries:
rationale, evidence_surfaces, confidence (0-1). Reviewers use the full scale and these
band anchors verbatim. Category neutrality is absolute: a game, art piece, music tool,
CRM, weird toy, and developer tool are all eligible for 10/10 on every criterion if they
satisfy that criterion exceptionally well FOR WHAT THEY ARE TRYING TO BE.
No aesthetic penalties. Signs of hurried AI work matter only as actual product-quality
problems (incoherence, broken flow, placeholder content), never as style.

## WebMCP Leverage — what does WebMCP change for this product?
Judge the delta WebMCP makes to reliability, precision, structured state access,
shared human-agent state, repeatability, and capability. Tool COUNT is not leverage.
- 9-10 TRANSFORMATIVE: the intended experience is impractical without the structured
  agent surface; human and agent share live product state; the collaboration could not
  be replicated comparably well by a general agent driving the UI.
- 7-8 MAJOR DELTA: without WebMCP a general agent is markedly less reliable/precise
  here; the agent is central to the product, not a bolt-on.
- 5-6 MEANINGFUL DELTA: existing capability packaged into a genuinely better product
  experience via structured tools; same outcome otherwise possible with real friction.
- 3-4 MINOR DELTA: generic browser automation achieves comparable outcomes; WebMCP is
  a convenience layer.
- 1-2 COSMETIC: exposes an action a UI button already offers; no meaningful change.

## Execution — is the actual product coherent, intentional, complete for its scope?
Stage 1 scores EXECUTION_CLAIMED: coherence/intentionality/completeness as evidenced by
the packet (description + packaging + video frames/transcript). Thin evidence caps the
score (end-to-end credit requires end-to-end evidence) and lowers confidence.
Stage 2 may rescore as EXECUTION_OBSERVED; latest evidence-backed score replaces, never
sums.
- 9-10: all surfaces agree; central workflow demonstrably completes; states handled;
  feels finished for its stated scope.
- 7-8: strong coherence and intentionality; central workflow appears functional; minor
  unfinished edges.
- 5-6: plausibly real with visible gaps; scope unclear or core not demonstrated
  end-to-end.
- 3-4: thin prototype; contradictions between surfaces; placeholder content.
- 1-2: non-functional or incoherent for its claimed scope.

## Creativity & Ambition — novelty of concept and interaction model
Judge ONLY: novelty of the concept, novelty of the interaction model, originality
relative to existing concepts, ambition, memorable/surprising use of the medium, and
the depth with which the concept is pursued. WebMCP indispensability belongs under
WebMCP Leverage, NOT here: a project can be extremely creative even if another agent
API could theoretically have enabled something similar.
- 9-10: genuinely novel concept or interaction model; ambitious scope pursued deeply
  and coherently; memorable.
- 7-8: fresh synthesis or notably original interaction; ambitious for a hackathon.
- 5-6: competent take on a known category with a real new angle.
- 3-4: derivative; novelty mostly surface details.
- 1-2: clone/template with trivial changes.
"That the category exists" is never a penalty; judge the submitted concept.

## Potential Impact — credible value for the intended audience
Judge: is there a real audience; is there a real problem, desire, or use case for that
audience; does the demonstrated solution genuinely address it. NO commercial adoption,
monetization, market pull, or startup viability is required. Entertainment, play, art,
creative expression, learning, niche hobby use, experimentation, and community value
are legitimate forms of impact. A tiny audience fully justifies a top score.
- 9-10: specific real audience with a real problem/desire; solution demonstrably
  addresses it; credible value, appeal, usefulness, or meaning for that audience.
- 7-8: clear audience and problem, good fit, credible value.
- 5-6: identifiable audience, looser fit or partial address.
- 3-4: speculative audience or strained fit; impact mostly aspirational.
- 1-2: no coherent audience/problem pairing.
Audience size is NOT a multiplier.

## Aggregate & ranking
aggregate = leverage + execution + impact + creativity (4-40). Nothing else enters.
Tie-breaks: Leverage, then Execution, then Impact, then Creativity, then higher
combined evidence confidence, then slug alphabetical. See PROTOCOL.md Ranking section.

A human first edits one product in the main catalog. ShowBranch records the complete before-and-after product state as human activity. The browser agent can then use WebMCP to: read products and human activity; create an isolated preview branch; propose updates to related products; read human corrections made inside that branch; revise proposals based on those corrections; and inspect the final Current vs Preview diff. Why WebMCP The key idea is not simply giving an agent functions to call. It is letting the human UI and the browser agent collaborate over the same application state . Without WebMCP, an agent would need to scrape rendered UI text or depend on a separate API that could drift from what the human actually sees and edits. ShowBranch exposes exactly five WebMCP tools: list_products get_activity create_preview_branch apply_branch_updates get_branch_diff These tools let the agent work directly with products, human activity, preview branches, and diffs. Human edits store complete before-and-after snapshots. Today that is roughly none of them. We measured three real open-source products, 522k lines of code, 429 places where a tool obviously belongs, and zero WebMCP registrations. So we asked a different question. What if the user adopts WebMCP for the site? The reframe WebMCP turns the login you already have into an API. A signed-in tab already carries every credential and permission the human has. If that tab registers tools with document.modelContext.registerTool , the agent gets a typed, narrow, inspectable interface to the site, and the vendor never has to ship a line of code. Adoption inverts. It no longer waits on ten thousand vendors. It happens one user, one tab, one reviewed pack at a time. What Agentready Network does Agentready Network is a local-first registry and runtime of reviewed action packs . A pack describes what a site can do, on exactly which origin, with which risk labels. The runtime turns that pack into live WebMCP tools inside the user's own signed-in session, then wraps every call in the safety the vendor never provided: Exact origins. A pack binds to one host and a reviewed fingerprint. Drift fails closed. Declarative, never executable. Manifests are JSON. The registry cannot ship code into your browser. Bounded, untrusted output. Results are allowlist-projected, byte-capped, and labeled contentTrust: "untrusted" . Tool output is content, not instruction. Human authority on consequences. Reads flow. Writes need a preview. Consequential actions need one exact, single-use approval bound to origin, tool, and normalized arguments. Change an argument and the grant is void. Verified closure. After a consequential action an inspector reads authoritative state and issues a receipt with no cookies, secrets, or raw bodies. Unknown outcome is reported as in_doubt , never as success. One concrete job, end to end The demo is not a feature tour. It is one incident. An alert fires in Grafana. Customer bookings in Cal.com are about to be affected. Neither site has WebMCP. The agent discovers the reviewed Grafana and Cal.com packs, installs them for this origin, and reads authoritative state. It never receives a credential. The agent prepares the exact continuity action and states what will change and how it will be inspected. The incident is still investigating . The human grants that exact action once, from a control the agent cannot reach. The agent executes once. The inspector verifies and issues one receipt. The incident is resolved . We poison the fingerprint. The pack refuses to run. We export the recorder draft. Every secret is gone before it can be published. Agents investigate. Humans authorize. Outcomes are verified. That is the whole product. What people and agents can now do together that they could not before Work a real incident across two unrelated vendor sites with no API contract between them. The user is the integration layer. Let an agent read freely and prepare freely, while every consequential step waits for a human decision the agent physically cannot forge. Hand a reviewer a receipt, not a screenshot, as proof of what changed. Teach a site once by observing its own traffic, then reuse that pack every session. Why WebMCP, specifically Screenshot-and-click pays the full cost on every step: a screenshot, a vision pass, a click that misses when the viewport shifts. We measured it. Creating one Grafana data source took nine browser actions and 55 seconds by DOM driving, and one WebMCP tool call in 20 milliseconds. A whole diagramming task took 44 calls versus 5. These are single-trial measurements from our audit work, not a benchmark suite, but the shape is unmistakable. The cost moves from per-action to per-app, and it never comes back. WebMCP also gives us the one thing a bookmarklet never had: a standard discovery surface. Any agent that speaks document.modelContext finds our tools without knowing we exist. How we built it Registry, Pack Lab, and runtime in plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. No framework. Every tool is registered with the current imperative API, document.modelContext.registerTool(tool, { signal }) , so tools unregister cleanly when a view unmounts. A Chrome MV3 extension injects reviewed read tools into the exact controlled Cal.com and Grafana origins and bridges receipts back to the registry. Reviewed adapters for Cal.com and Grafana were reverse-engineered on the wire, not from source. The recorder wraps fetch and XMLHttpRequest , clusters observed traffic, drafts tools, auto-flags mutating endpoints, and strips secrets before anything is published. Approval, inspection, drift, and redaction are enforced in the shared runtime, so every pack inherits them and no pack can opt out. Node's built-in test runner covers the trust boundaries. All tests pass on the submitted commit. What we deliberately did not claim The public judge path is a deterministic fixture so nobody needs an account. The same runtime drives the real Cal.com and Grafana adapters against controlled localhost instances; the video shows the controlled Grafana and Cal.com state alongside the public lab. The extension checkpoint is read-only. Remembered approvals, arbitrary-site install, generic DOM automation, and automatic rollback are out of scope. Figma taught us the frontier. Its control plane is reachable in-session; its canvas rides a binary WebSocket inside a Worker that no page-context patch can see. We published only what we proved and marked canvas manipulation unsupported. What we learned Discovery is observation, not code reading. Every tool we guessed from source was wrong. Every tool we drafted from the wire worked. Minification cannot rename what crosses the network. Injection inverts the safety burden onto the installer. The vendor never audited these tools, so we had to. That is why approval, inspection, and redaction live in the runtime and not in the pack. What is next Persist packs across sessions in the extension. Chain packs across sites so one agent can read portal A and act in portal B. Generalize the approval contract into a policy layer: read anything, never pay, never cancel, never touch what a human pinned.
