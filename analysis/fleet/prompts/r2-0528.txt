# FROZEN SCORING RUBRIC (pre-registered before any Stage 1 review)

Scores are integers 1-10 on each of the FOUR official criteria. Every score carries:
rationale, evidence_surfaces, confidence (0-1). Reviewers use the full scale and these
band anchors verbatim. Category neutrality is absolute: a game, art piece, music tool,
CRM, weird toy, and developer tool are all eligible for 10/10 on every criterion if they
satisfy that criterion exceptionally well FOR WHAT THEY ARE TRYING TO BE.
No aesthetic penalties. Signs of hurried AI work matter only as actual product-quality
problems (incoherence, broken flow, placeholder content), never as style.

## WebMCP Leverage — what does WebMCP change for this product?
Judge the delta WebMCP makes to reliability, precision, structured state access,
shared human-agent state, repeatability, and capability. Tool COUNT is not leverage.
- 9-10 TRANSFORMATIVE: the intended experience is impractical without the structured
  agent surface; human and agent share live product state; the collaboration could not
  be replicated comparably well by a general agent driving the UI.
- 7-8 MAJOR DELTA: without WebMCP a general agent is markedly less reliable/precise
  here; the agent is central to the product, not a bolt-on.
- 5-6 MEANINGFUL DELTA: existing capability packaged into a genuinely better product
  experience via structured tools; same outcome otherwise possible with real friction.
- 3-4 MINOR DELTA: generic browser automation achieves comparable outcomes; WebMCP is
  a convenience layer.
- 1-2 COSMETIC: exposes an action a UI button already offers; no meaningful change.

## Execution — is the actual product coherent, intentional, complete for its scope?
Stage 1 scores EXECUTION_CLAIMED: coherence/intentionality/completeness as evidenced by
the packet (description + packaging + video frames/transcript). Thin evidence caps the
score (end-to-end credit requires end-to-end evidence) and lowers confidence.
Stage 2 may rescore as EXECUTION_OBSERVED; latest evidence-backed score replaces, never
sums.
- 9-10: all surfaces agree; central workflow demonstrably completes; states handled;
  feels finished for its stated scope.
- 7-8: strong coherence and intentionality; central workflow appears functional; minor
  unfinished edges.
- 5-6: plausibly real with visible gaps; scope unclear or core not demonstrated
  end-to-end.
- 3-4: thin prototype; contradictions between surfaces; placeholder content.
- 1-2: non-functional or incoherent for its claimed scope.

## Creativity & Ambition — novelty of concept and interaction model
Judge ONLY: novelty of the concept, novelty of the interaction model, originality
relative to existing concepts, ambition, memorable/surprising use of the medium, and
the depth with which the concept is pursued. WebMCP indispensability belongs under
WebMCP Leverage, NOT here: a project can be extremely creative even if another agent
API could theoretically have enabled something similar.
- 9-10: genuinely novel concept or interaction model; ambitious scope pursued deeply
  and coherently; memorable.
- 7-8: fresh synthesis or notably original interaction; ambitious for a hackathon.
- 5-6: competent take on a known category with a real new angle.
- 3-4: derivative; novelty mostly surface details.
- 1-2: clone/template with trivial changes.
"That the category exists" is never a penalty; judge the submitted concept.

## Potential Impact — credible value for the intended audience
Judge: is there a real audience; is there a real problem, desire, or use case for that
audience; does the demonstrated solution genuinely address it. NO commercial adoption,
monetization, market pull, or startup viability is required. Entertainment, play, art,
creative expression, learning, niche hobby use, experimentation, and community value
are legitimate forms of impact. A tiny audience fully justifies a top score.
- 9-10: specific real audience with a real problem/desire; solution demonstrably
  addresses it; credible value, appeal, usefulness, or meaning for that audience.
- 7-8: clear audience and problem, good fit, credible value.
- 5-6: identifiable audience, looser fit or partial address.
- 3-4: speculative audience or strained fit; impact mostly aspirational.
- 1-2: no coherent audience/problem pairing.
Audience size is NOT a multiplier.

## Aggregate & ranking
aggregate = leverage + execution + impact + creativity (4-40). Nothing else enters.
Tie-breaks: Leverage, then Execution, then Impact, then Creativity, then higher
combined evidence confidence, then slug alphabetical. See PROTOCOL.md Ranking section.

Three decisions there are worth naming. Agent-backed tools return the site's own voice, not a status. send_message and show_collection hand the request to the merchant's AI and resolve with its finished text. The operator contract, served to the calling agent at runtime through window.webmcp.instructions , tells it to relay that verbatim: "You operate the tools; the site AI is the voice." The merchant's brand voice survives the round trip instead of being paraphrased by somebody else's model. A turn that outruns the await window returns status: "running" — the caller polls get_status and reads latestResponse . The contract is that the site's voice reaches the agent, not that it arrives within a fixed budget. The cart is addressed by selections, never by variant id. get_products deliberately returns no variant id: those ids are unstable across the optimistic-write window, so an id an external agent held across a turn could aim a write at a line that has already been replaced. Cart verbs take a product plus {axis: value} selections and bind late, against live state — a stale reference can only miss , and fail loudly. This replaced a first version where add_to_cart fell back to "first in-stock variant", so a shopper who asked for size 8 got whatever was in stock first. add_to_cart books itself as a third actor — not the agent, not the visitor, but origin: 'webmcp' . Folding a WebMCP-driven add into either of the others would lie about which surface earned it, and that is the number a merchant asks about first. For agents that do not speak WebMCP natively, appending ?webmcp=1 installs a real host plus a window.webmcp client, so a JS-eval-capable browser agent can call the identical surface. It never overrides a real host — if Chrome or a bridge extension already provided navigator.modelContext , the sim is a no-op. That same flag also makes the URL a self-contained demo: it renders what the page is, and a prompt to paste into an agent. No repo to clone and nothing to send alongside the link. How we built it The storefront is composed, not served. A tool call arrives carrying an intent, and one fixed two-call flow runs: Reasoning — a multi-round tool loop bounded by step count and wall clock. The model fires tool calls as a parallel batch, sees the results and may fire another batch to refine. No prose, no layout. Compose — one streamText call with tools omitted entirely and the reasoning step's tool history flattened to plain text. It streams a jsonl-elements fence that parses line by line into RFC 6902 patch operations against the layout spec. The tool-free compose call is structural, not stylistic. A single tool-bearing call that also composes cannot guarantee output: the model emits a phantom functionCall , finishes with finishReason='tool-calls' and the visitor gets a blank canvas. Strip the tool registry and a phantom call has nothing to execute — the model is forced to compose. It is the most important architectural line in the system. The model split is not the one you would guess. Both calls run Gemini 3.6 Flash by default. The performance split is structural — two calls plus per-step reasoning budgets — not cheap-planner and expensive-composer. Reasoning runs at a minimal thinking floor because a fuller budget measured 13–24s per turn against ~4.6s warm: tried, measured, rejected. Future Doors turns a saved opportunity into a source-checked career path that a person and an agent can build together. The problem People who are exploring more than one career direction constantly save internships, fellowships, challenges, research calls, and communities from Instagram, LinkedIn, and group chats. Those posts disappear into a camera roll. Even when someone returns to one, they still have to find the official page, interpret eligibility and dates, check whether it fits their schedule, and understand what the experience would actually add to their longer-term direction. Most job products optimize for one role and one application. Future Doors is designed for people whose experience does not fit one label: a builder may also be a technical storyteller, researcher, or future education founder. What it does Future Doors turns scattered opportunity clues into a small, explainable plan: A person shares a saved screenshot or link. The agent finds the original official HTTPS source and stages the deadline, relevant rules, schedule, format, concrete outputs, and one unresolved fact for review. The same profile can hold several possible futures. AI product, hardware storytelling, and learning, for example, produce genuinely different recommended work. Source-checked cards are grouped as activities, projects, research, or credentials. Fit hearts represent verifiable alignment with the person's facts and preferences—not acceptance or hiring odds. The person chooses up to three near-term moves. The plan shows timing, prerequisite relationships, schedule conflicts, and which goal-specific evidence each move can create. Ineligible or unresolved opportunities remain visibly blocked. The agent can stage a separate source-backed path, but it cannot pretend that the original eligibility was restored. The result is not another ranked list. It is a visual evidence deck: what to do, when it fits, what it can produce, and what is still missing. Why WebMCP is essential Future Doors is a shared stateful surface, not a chatbot wrapper. Through WebMCP, an agent can read the same profile, futures, saved opportunities, constraints, timing, and proof gaps that the person sees. It can then focus a route, compare constrained paths, check a saved post, surface one missing fact, or stage a source-backed change directly on the page instead of guessing through visual controls. The live app registers 15 structured tools with document.modelContext.registerTool . Tool inputs are schema-validated, read-only operations are identified, externally sourced text is treated as untrusted, outputs are bounded, and invalid dates or IDs return explicit errors. Most importantly, there is no WebMCP approval tool. An agent may prepare profile facts, an opportunity, a priority plan, an alternative route, or a work link, but the person must approve every consequential change in the interface. Source facts, agent inference, planned work, saved links, and verified proof are never presented as the same state. That boundary is the product: the agent prepares; the person decides. What is new Future Doors combines three ideas that are normally separated: a scrapbook for opportunities discovered outside job platforms; official-source and eligibility checking by an agent; a multi-future portfolio planner that shows how selected work can create reusable evidence. It does not force a premature career choice and does not manufacture a success score. It helps someone make a bounded decision across several plausible futures while keeping every important claim inspectable.
