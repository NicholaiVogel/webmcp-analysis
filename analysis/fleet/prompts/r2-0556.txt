# FROZEN SCORING RUBRIC (pre-registered before any Stage 1 review)

Scores are integers 1-10 on each of the FOUR official criteria. Every score carries:
rationale, evidence_surfaces, confidence (0-1). Reviewers use the full scale and these
band anchors verbatim. Category neutrality is absolute: a game, art piece, music tool,
CRM, weird toy, and developer tool are all eligible for 10/10 on every criterion if they
satisfy that criterion exceptionally well FOR WHAT THEY ARE TRYING TO BE.
No aesthetic penalties. Signs of hurried AI work matter only as actual product-quality
problems (incoherence, broken flow, placeholder content), never as style.

## WebMCP Leverage — what does WebMCP change for this product?
Judge the delta WebMCP makes to reliability, precision, structured state access,
shared human-agent state, repeatability, and capability. Tool COUNT is not leverage.
- 9-10 TRANSFORMATIVE: the intended experience is impractical without the structured
  agent surface; human and agent share live product state; the collaboration could not
  be replicated comparably well by a general agent driving the UI.
- 7-8 MAJOR DELTA: without WebMCP a general agent is markedly less reliable/precise
  here; the agent is central to the product, not a bolt-on.
- 5-6 MEANINGFUL DELTA: existing capability packaged into a genuinely better product
  experience via structured tools; same outcome otherwise possible with real friction.
- 3-4 MINOR DELTA: generic browser automation achieves comparable outcomes; WebMCP is
  a convenience layer.
- 1-2 COSMETIC: exposes an action a UI button already offers; no meaningful change.

## Execution — is the actual product coherent, intentional, complete for its scope?
Stage 1 scores EXECUTION_CLAIMED: coherence/intentionality/completeness as evidenced by
the packet (description + packaging + video frames/transcript). Thin evidence caps the
score (end-to-end credit requires end-to-end evidence) and lowers confidence.
Stage 2 may rescore as EXECUTION_OBSERVED; latest evidence-backed score replaces, never
sums.
- 9-10: all surfaces agree; central workflow demonstrably completes; states handled;
  feels finished for its stated scope.
- 7-8: strong coherence and intentionality; central workflow appears functional; minor
  unfinished edges.
- 5-6: plausibly real with visible gaps; scope unclear or core not demonstrated
  end-to-end.
- 3-4: thin prototype; contradictions between surfaces; placeholder content.
- 1-2: non-functional or incoherent for its claimed scope.

## Creativity & Ambition — novelty of concept and interaction model
Judge ONLY: novelty of the concept, novelty of the interaction model, originality
relative to existing concepts, ambition, memorable/surprising use of the medium, and
the depth with which the concept is pursued. WebMCP indispensability belongs under
WebMCP Leverage, NOT here: a project can be extremely creative even if another agent
API could theoretically have enabled something similar.
- 9-10: genuinely novel concept or interaction model; ambitious scope pursued deeply
  and coherently; memorable.
- 7-8: fresh synthesis or notably original interaction; ambitious for a hackathon.
- 5-6: competent take on a known category with a real new angle.
- 3-4: derivative; novelty mostly surface details.
- 1-2: clone/template with trivial changes.
"That the category exists" is never a penalty; judge the submitted concept.

## Potential Impact — credible value for the intended audience
Judge: is there a real audience; is there a real problem, desire, or use case for that
audience; does the demonstrated solution genuinely address it. NO commercial adoption,
monetization, market pull, or startup viability is required. Entertainment, play, art,
creative expression, learning, niche hobby use, experimentation, and community value
are legitimate forms of impact. A tiny audience fully justifies a top score.
- 9-10: specific real audience with a real problem/desire; solution demonstrably
  addresses it; credible value, appeal, usefulness, or meaning for that audience.
- 7-8: clear audience and problem, good fit, credible value.
- 5-6: identifiable audience, looser fit or partial address.
- 3-4: speculative audience or strained fit; impact mostly aspirational.
- 1-2: no coherent audience/problem pairing.
Audience size is NOT a multiplier.

## Aggregate & ranking
aggregate = leverage + execution + impact + creativity (4-40). Nothing else enters.
Tie-breaks: Leverage, then Execution, then Impact, then Creativity, then higher
combined evidence confidence, then slug alphabetical. See PROTOCOL.md Ranking section.

That's what the certificate is for, and it turned out to matter more than I expected when I started. Also, WebMCP turns out to be a gift for legacy systems. Once the idea is validated, I'll start charging for it. The long game is a neutral table for every two-party document: leases, offer letters, creator deals. The idea for AppraiseAI started when I saw OpenAI’s tweet about WebMCP. I was curious, so I began reading more about it. The more I learned, the more I saw how well WebMCP could fit this problem. Instead of making appraisers copy information between a website and a chatbot, I wanted to create one shared workspace where people and AI agents could work together while keeping the final decision firmly in human hands. What it does AppraiseAI turns unstructured field notes and object images into an organized, evidence-backed appraisal draft, saving professionals valuable time to focus on expert judgment and final decisions. An AI agent can: Read the active appraisal context Turn field notes into structured appraisal items Search a catalog of synthetic comparable sales Link relevant evidence to each item Draft supported valuation ranges Check whether the appraisal is ready for review Hand the completed draft back to the appraiser Every agent action immediately appears in the visible interface, updates the appraisal version, and is recorded in an activity ledger. How I built it AppraiseAI is built with React, Vite, and TypeScript. The application uses a shared, typed state layer persisted in localStorage . The visible interface and WebMCP tools both use the same domain operations and validation rules, ensuring that an agent cannot bypass restrictions enforced by the application. The page exposes eight focused WebMCP tools through document.modelContext.registerTool() : get_appraisal_context add_appraisal_items update_appraisal_item search_comparable_sales link_comparable_sale set_item_valuation get_appraisal_readiness mark_appraisal_ready_for_review Every successful mutation returns a verification snapshot so the agent can confirm what changed. The demo requires no backend, accounts, uploads, or external APIs. All cases, objects, images, comparable sales, people, organizations, dates, and values were created specifically for this challenge and are entirely fictional. Challenges I ran into Finding out in two days what WebMCP is and what WebMCP does. Maintaining an agent and an interface that always work together. Each action on the tool would trigger a new state version, update the workspace at once, save the change locally, and generate a traceable activity entry. Another crucial point was ensuring the application would keep working in browsers without WebMCP support. Lastly, making sure the demo video is less than three minutes long. Accomplishments that i'm proud of I am especially proud that AppraiseAI demonstrates a complete human-agent workflow rather than a collection of disconnected tool calls. The agent can structure notes, research evidence, draft valuation ranges, identify missing work, and prepare the appraisal for review all inside the same interface the appraiser uses. What I learned WebMCP tools are surprisingly easy to build, yet they can save people enormous amounts of time. They can act as a second brain or personal assistant, taking care of repetitive work so people can focus on what actually requires their expertise. What's next for AppraiseAI The next step is to explore how AppraiseAI could support larger, more flexible appraisal workflows while preserving the same principles of human control. This could include additional components for customer communication, report generation, and further automation of repetitive tasks. A job site any WebMCP agent can operate end to end - find, compare, keep watching, apply - with the person in control. Like Comment Story Updates A job site that serves people through its pages and agents through its tools. The site announces 29 actions; any WebMCP client discovers them on every page, with nothing to install. One request becomes one typed call: search_jobs runs and the page follows with the filters the agent set. Quiet reads leave the page still; open_job_details moves it. Every call stays visible to the person. Three roles compared on the facts the site returns, in a single call. Saved once. The review waits for the person's decision; then the server keeps checking after the tab closes. An agent-prepared application: read-only while it works, with the exact review of what would be sent. The web is getting its agent interface Websites were built for people: a page to read, buttons to click, forms to fill. An agent can drive that interface too, the way a person would, but it is slow, brittle and blind to what the site actually offers. That is why agentic APIs appeared: MCP gave agents proper, typed tools. It came with a catch. Every service needed its own server, every person needed to install and configure it, and an agent only knew about the handful of services someone had wired up in advance. WebMCP closes that gap. The website itself publishes its tools, right on the page, and any WebMCP-capable client discovers them the moment it arrives: ChatGPT's built-in browser today, Chrome with WebMCP, external agent clients that speak the protocol. No setup, no separate server, no integration to maintain. An agent moving through the web meets, on every site that adopts it, an interface made for agents: typed actions with clear inputs and results, instead of a screen to interpret. That is a different web. Call it headless: the same site serves people through its pages and agents through its tools, from one place, with one set of rules. Three things follow: Agents get a native interface to the web. Actions, not pixels. Reliable enough to hand real work to. It scales like the web does. One protocol; every site that adopts it extends every agent at once, with nothing to install. Delegation becomes possible. A person states an outcome, the agent operates the site, and the site keeps the person in the loop exactly where it matters. Jobbbler is what that looks like on one concrete, everyday task. One sentence, a whole job search Looking for a Product Designer position, remote, $90k+. Compare options, pick the top 2, and apply. Schedule for daily updates (every morning). My CV is attached. Said to a person, that is a week of open tabs. Said to an assistant on Jobbbler, it becomes a sequence of site actions: Find. The assistant asks Jobbbler which filters it accepts, then runs one search: product designer, remote, $90k and up. The results page follows along: URL, filters and matches update in front of you. Compare. It opens the strongest roles and compares them on the facts the site returns: compensation, location, seniority, why each one matches. Apply, with control. It prepares two applications on its own: your request to apply is the authority to prepare. It proposes answers that stay marked as its own and requests one exact review of what would be sent. The page turns read-only while it works. Nothing leaves until you decide, in your client. Keep watching. It asks Jobbbler to monitor the search. The site states what it will check, how often, where the updates go and how to stop. You approve once. Then the tab can close: Jobbbler's server does the morning check, and the next time you ask, you hear only what changed. Every step is a real tool call, visible in the Agent activity panel on the page. That panel is for people watching (you, or a judge); the agent never needs it. Two kinds of tools, on purpose Some site actions should be silent, others should be seen. Jobbbler separates them: Quiet tools return structured facts and leave your page alone: search, read a role, compare, check what changed. The assistant can do most of the work without disturbing what you are looking at. Follow tools move the real page: open a role, show the results, open the comparison. Say "show me" and the site follows the agent, so you can watch instead of trusting. The assistant picks the tool. The person can always see what was done. Where control lives Discovery is automatic, but tool access is not authority. Two things always need the person: an email destination, and the final submission of the exact application to its recipient. Each is a request-bound decision: the site issues an expiring request, the client presents it, the person decides, and the server checks that the decision matches the exact request it issued. Consent can be withdrawn in one call. This is the part that makes delegation worth trusting, and it is the part most agent demos skip. Why this is bigger than jobs Nothing in this design is specific to job search. A shop, a clinic's booking page, a documentation site: any data-rich website can publish its actions the same way and become operable by the assistant a person already uses. Jobbbler is one worked example, end to end, of a web that works for people and their agents at the same time. Under the hood 29 WebMCP tools registered directly in the page, the same set on every route. Private or stage-specific tools answer with the next safe step instead of pretending to succeed. One command layer, two adapters: the visible interface and the tools share the same server commands, so the page and the agent never disagree. Honest continuation: WebMCP lives while the page is open; durable monitoring runs on Jobbbler's server with a leased worker. No second model behind the site: understanding and drafting stay with the person's client; Jobbbler supplies typed data, deterministic search, workflow state and enforced authority. TypeScript monorepo: Next.js, domain packages, SQLite for local runs and PostgreSQL (Supabase) in production, Resend-compatible email, a small design system. Production runs at jobbbler.com under the Chrome WebMCP origin trial and is verified with unit, contract and browser suites plus real tool runs in ChatGPT Desktop's browser.
