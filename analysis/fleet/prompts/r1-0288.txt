=== RUBRIC (frozen) ===
# FROZEN SCORING RUBRIC (pre-registered before any Stage 1 review)

Scores are integers 1-10 on each of the FOUR official criteria. Every score carries:
rationale, evidence_surfaces, confidence (0-1). Reviewers use the full scale and these
band anchors verbatim. Category neutrality is absolute: a game, art piece, music tool,
CRM, weird toy, and developer tool are all eligible for 10/10 on every criterion if they
satisfy that criterion exceptionally well FOR WHAT THEY ARE TRYING TO BE.
No aesthetic penalties. Signs of hurried AI work matter only as actual product-quality
problems (incoherence, broken flow, placeholder content), never as style.

## WebMCP Leverage — what does WebMCP change for this product?
Judge the delta WebMCP makes to reliability, precision, structured state access,
shared human-agent state, repeatability, and capability. Tool COUNT is not leverage.
- 9-10 TRANSFORMATIVE: the intended experience is impractical without the structured
  agent surface; human and agent share live product state; the collaboration could not
  be replicated comparably well by a general agent driving the UI.
- 7-8 MAJOR DELTA: without WebMCP a general agent is markedly less reliable/precise
  here; the agent is central to the product, not a bolt-on.
- 5-6 MEANINGFUL DELTA: existing capability packaged into a genuinely better product
  experience via structured tools; same outcome otherwise possible with real friction.
- 3-4 MINOR DELTA: generic browser automation achieves comparable outcomes; WebMCP is
  a convenience layer.
- 1-2 COSMETIC: exposes an action a UI button already offers; no meaningful change.

## Execution — is the actual product coherent, intentional, complete for its scope?
Stage 1 scores EXECUTION_CLAIMED: coherence/intentionality/completeness as evidenced by
the packet (description + packaging + video frames/transcript). Thin evidence caps the
score (end-to-end credit requires end-to-end evidence) and lowers confidence.
Stage 2 may rescore as EXECUTION_OBSERVED; latest evidence-backed score replaces, never
sums.
- 9-10: all surfaces agree; central workflow demonstrably completes; states handled;
  feels finished for its stated scope.
- 7-8: strong coherence and intentionality; central workflow appears functional; minor
  unfinished edges.
- 5-6: plausibly real with visible gaps; scope unclear or core not demonstrated
  end-to-end.
- 3-4: thin prototype; contradictions between surfaces; placeholder content.
- 1-2: non-functional or incoherent for its claimed scope.

## Creativity & Ambition — novelty of concept and interaction model
Judge ONLY: novelty of the concept, novelty of the interaction model, originality
relative to existing concepts, ambition, memorable/surprising use of the medium, and
the depth with which the concept is pursued. WebMCP indispensability belongs under
WebMCP Leverage, NOT here: a project can be extremely creative even if another agent
API could theoretically have enabled something similar.
- 9-10: genuinely novel concept or interaction model; ambitious scope pursued deeply
  and coherently; memorable.
- 7-8: fresh synthesis or notably original interaction; ambitious for a hackathon.
- 5-6: competent take on a known category with a real new angle.
- 3-4: derivative; novelty mostly surface details.
- 1-2: clone/template with trivial changes.
"That the category exists" is never a penalty; judge the submitted concept.

## Potential Impact — credible value for the intended audience
Judge: is there a real audience; is there a real problem, desire, or use case for that
audience; does the demonstrated solution genuinely address it. NO commercial adoption,
monetization, market pull, or startup viability is required. Entertainment, play, art,
creative expression, learning, niche hobby use, experimentation, and community value
are legitimate forms of impact. A tiny audience fully justifies a top score.
- 9-10: specific real audience with a real problem/desire; solution demonstrably
  addresses it; credible value, appeal, usefulness, or meaning for that audience.
- 7-8: clear audience and problem, good fit, credible value.
- 5-6: identifiable audience, looser fit or partial address.
- 3-4: speculative audience or strained fit; impact mostly aspirational.
- 1-2: no coherent audience/problem pairing.
Audience size is NOT a multiplier.

## Aggregate & ranking
aggregate = leverage + execution + impact + creativity (4-40). Nothing else enters.
Tie-breaks: Leverage, then Execution, then Impact, then Creativity, then higher
combined evidence confidence, then slug alphabetical. See PROTOCOL.md Ranking section.

The agent interprets. Poolwart calculates. You verify. Like Comment Story Updates Poolwart turns an agent request into a visible, editable and safety-aware water-care calculation. Inspiration Pool and hot-tub owners often have three disconnected pieces of information: a water volume, a test result, and an active-chlorine percentage printed somewhere on a product label. Translating those values into a dose is tedious, and a generic assistant should not improvise safety-sensitive water-care arithmetic in chat. The useful division of work is different: an agent is good at understanding the messy request and extracting the inputs; a specialist site is good at deterministic, tested calculation; the owner must remain the person who verifies and acts. What it does Poolwart exposes two imperative WebMCP tools: calculate_chlor_topup calculates a dry-granule top-up from a measured free-chlorine value to the target range of the currently open Poolwart segment. calculate_chlor_shock calculates Poolwart's fixed +10 mg/l dry-granule shock increase. Calling a tool does not leave the answer trapped in an invisible tool response. It switches the visible calculator to the right mode, fills every input, and renders the normal dose, warnings, provenance, re-entry guidance, and disclaimer. The person can inspect or change every value and recalculate through the form. Poolwart calculates only. It never controls equipment, purchases a product, or physically doses water. How we built it The site uses imperative top-level registration through document.modelContext.registerTool . Each tool has a narrow JSON Schema with numeric limits and additionalProperties: false , followed by defensive runtime validation. Both the manual form and WebMCP call one shared typed adapter, which delegates the actual arithmetic to the existing, tested @poolwart/wasser package. WebMCP is registered only after client mount and receives an AbortSignal , so tools are removed when the component unmounts or the page changes. Unsupported browsers keep the complete ordinary calculator. The challenge extension adds focused tests for calculation parity, boundaries, schemas, registration, lifecycle signals, result size, invalid inputs, unusually large volumes, and above-target measurements. How WebMCP improves the experience Without WebMCP, a user or browser agent must choose a form mode and transcribe each value. An assistant may instead calculate in chat, losing the specialist formula, segment-specific targets, warnings, and an editable verification surface. With WebMCP, the agent invokes a named capability on the page. Poolwart validates and calculates, then makes the handoff visible to the human. This is a genuine shared workspace: the agent can prepare the calculation, the site can prove what it used, and the person stays in control. What struck me is that the constraint is not really about privacy. It is about what an agent actually needs in order to be useful . An agent reasoning about a liability clause does not need to know the counterparty is Marceline Dubreuil. It needs to know that the party in section 2 is the party in section 7. That is a structural fact, not an identity. Separate those two and the impossible task becomes possible: the agent gets the substance, never the identities. WebMCP is what makes that separation enforceable rather than aspirational, because the tools run in the page, next to the person who owns the document. What it does GuacaDoc opens a document in your tab, parses it there (TXT, MD, PDF, DOCX), scans it locally for identifying values, and turns it into five WebMCP tools an agent can call. The agent outlines, searches and reads the document, and never receives a name, an account number or an address from it. You review what was found and choose, per value or per type, whether it goes out as written , as a stable token , or never . Bank details default to never, because an account number carries no analytical value. Names come back as [PERSON_01] , stable across the whole document. That stability is what keeps the analysis useful: the agent can reason that the signatory of section 2 is the person named in section 7 without knowing who either is. When it answers in tokens, you paste its answer into a decode panel and get the real names back, assembled in your tab from your key. Withheld values have no token, so they are not reversible by construction. A running counter shows exactly how many characters the agent has read, and a strip lists every chunk that left the tab, in order, with its tool and timestamp. The handler has no say in it. The alternative, asking every handler to remember to redact, is a design that fails the first time someone adds a sixth tool at 2 a.m. Here a leak requires defeating the scrubber, not forgetting to call it. The page ships connect-src 'none' , so it cannot open a network connection: no fetch, no XHR, no WebSocket, no beacon. Detection is local and deliberately blunt: regex plus a capitalisation heuristic, tuned to over-detect, because a false positive costs one click and a false negative silently leaves a name in the outbound text. A tool is withdrawn by aborting the signal it was registered with. Without handling that, a second mount re-offers five names the browser already holds, every call is rejected, and the page advertises tools while the browser answers an older set of closures. Registration is therefore generational. Trusting the browser over myself. I keep my own registry of tools and I do not believe it. The header count comes from the browser's own getTools() , because the browser's answer is the only one that cannot be wrong. "5 tools" above an empty record is made structurally impossible. The screen is a channel WebMCP does not govern. My tool surface is sealed, but an agent that can screenshot the tab is not reading through my tools at all. So the token map is absent from the DOM by default rather than blurred , revealed on demand for 45 seconds with a visible countdown, ending immediately on blur , pagehide or visibilitychange , which are facts the browser states rather than infers. The activity bar deliberately does not claim the page is being read, because a screenshot is invisible to the page and a bar that lit up for it would be guessing. Tool-mediated disclosure is what WebMCP governs, and that is what this enforces. The rest is written into the interface itself, because a threat model you hide is not a threat model.
