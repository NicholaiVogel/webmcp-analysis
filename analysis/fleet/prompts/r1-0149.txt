=== RUBRIC (frozen) ===
# FROZEN SCORING RUBRIC (pre-registered before any Stage 1 review)

Scores are integers 1-10 on each of the FOUR official criteria. Every score carries:
rationale, evidence_surfaces, confidence (0-1). Reviewers use the full scale and these
band anchors verbatim. Category neutrality is absolute: a game, art piece, music tool,
CRM, weird toy, and developer tool are all eligible for 10/10 on every criterion if they
satisfy that criterion exceptionally well FOR WHAT THEY ARE TRYING TO BE.
No aesthetic penalties. Signs of hurried AI work matter only as actual product-quality
problems (incoherence, broken flow, placeholder content), never as style.

## WebMCP Leverage — what does WebMCP change for this product?
Judge the delta WebMCP makes to reliability, precision, structured state access,
shared human-agent state, repeatability, and capability. Tool COUNT is not leverage.
- 9-10 TRANSFORMATIVE: the intended experience is impractical without the structured
  agent surface; human and agent share live product state; the collaboration could not
  be replicated comparably well by a general agent driving the UI.
- 7-8 MAJOR DELTA: without WebMCP a general agent is markedly less reliable/precise
  here; the agent is central to the product, not a bolt-on.
- 5-6 MEANINGFUL DELTA: existing capability packaged into a genuinely better product
  experience via structured tools; same outcome otherwise possible with real friction.
- 3-4 MINOR DELTA: generic browser automation achieves comparable outcomes; WebMCP is
  a convenience layer.
- 1-2 COSMETIC: exposes an action a UI button already offers; no meaningful change.

## Execution — is the actual product coherent, intentional, complete for its scope?
Stage 1 scores EXECUTION_CLAIMED: coherence/intentionality/completeness as evidenced by
the packet (description + packaging + video frames/transcript). Thin evidence caps the
score (end-to-end credit requires end-to-end evidence) and lowers confidence.
Stage 2 may rescore as EXECUTION_OBSERVED; latest evidence-backed score replaces, never
sums.
- 9-10: all surfaces agree; central workflow demonstrably completes; states handled;
  feels finished for its stated scope.
- 7-8: strong coherence and intentionality; central workflow appears functional; minor
  unfinished edges.
- 5-6: plausibly real with visible gaps; scope unclear or core not demonstrated
  end-to-end.
- 3-4: thin prototype; contradictions between surfaces; placeholder content.
- 1-2: non-functional or incoherent for its claimed scope.

## Creativity & Ambition — novelty of concept and interaction model
Judge ONLY: novelty of the concept, novelty of the interaction model, originality
relative to existing concepts, ambition, memorable/surprising use of the medium, and
the depth with which the concept is pursued. WebMCP indispensability belongs under
WebMCP Leverage, NOT here: a project can be extremely creative even if another agent
API could theoretically have enabled something similar.
- 9-10: genuinely novel concept or interaction model; ambitious scope pursued deeply
  and coherently; memorable.
- 7-8: fresh synthesis or notably original interaction; ambitious for a hackathon.
- 5-6: competent take on a known category with a real new angle.
- 3-4: derivative; novelty mostly surface details.
- 1-2: clone/template with trivial changes.
"That the category exists" is never a penalty; judge the submitted concept.

## Potential Impact — credible value for the intended audience
Judge: is there a real audience; is there a real problem, desire, or use case for that
audience; does the demonstrated solution genuinely address it. NO commercial adoption,
monetization, market pull, or startup viability is required. Entertainment, play, art,
creative expression, learning, niche hobby use, experimentation, and community value
are legitimate forms of impact. A tiny audience fully justifies a top score.
- 9-10: specific real audience with a real problem/desire; solution demonstrably
  addresses it; credible value, appeal, usefulness, or meaning for that audience.
- 7-8: clear audience and problem, good fit, credible value.
- 5-6: identifiable audience, looser fit or partial address.
- 3-4: speculative audience or strained fit; impact mostly aspirational.
- 1-2: no coherent audience/problem pairing.
Audience size is NOT a multiplier.

## Aggregate & ranking
aggregate = leverage + execution + impact + creativity (4-40). Nothing else enters.
Tie-breaks: Leverage, then Execution, then Impact, then Creativity, then higher
combined evidence confidence, then slug alphabetical. See PROTOCOL.md Ranking section.

Inspiration Revelus Dermatology has been serving patients in Austin since 2014. Over that time, we have built a large patient-facing website with information about medical conditions, cosmetic treatments, providers, pricing, insurance, and scheduling. The challenge is that having the information available does not always make it easy to use. Patients rarely arrive knowing the exact medical term, provider, or appointment type they need. They ask questions like: "My hair is thinning. Who should I see?" "Can I get a skin cancer screening and talk about another concern at the same visit?" "Do you treat rosacea?" "Who performs Mohs surgery?" "What appointments are available next week?" A traditional website can contain the answers to all of those questions while still requiring the patient to search multiple pages, interpret practice-specific scheduling rules, and choose from a long list of appointment types. We built Revelus.ai to explore a different model. WebMCP is a strong fit because the problem is not a lack of information, but giving agents structured access to trusted practice content, scheduling rules, and live availability without requiring them to interpret the website like a human. The goal was not to build another healthcare chatbot. It was to make an existing healthcare website more useful to both patients and AI agents while keeping the practice itself as the source of truth. What we built Revelus.ai uses WebMCP to help an agent move from a natural-language dermatology question to a useful next step. The experience can: Search published Revelus Dermatology content about conditions, treatments, providers, pricing, insurance, and practice information Return answers grounded in that published content Translate everyday patient language into the appropriate appointment path Recognize when two concerns require two different appointments Identify provider-specific scheduling options Retrieve real-time appointment availability from our live scheduling system Provide the appropriate booking path while leaving the final scheduling decision with the patient Recognize requests that would require diagnostic inference and redirect them toward clinician evaluation rather than assigning a condition Reject requests for private medical information that should not be handled through a public website The live availability is not a demonstration dataset. It reflects actual appointment inventory from our scheduling system and can change as appointments are booked or released. The sample questions on the live Revelus.ai experience are examples, not its full coverage. The live deployment uses a broader private Revelus corpus. For reproducibility without publishing proprietary practice data, the public challenge repository includes a deliberately limited ten-query synthetic fixture that exercises the same WebMCP architecture. The application registers three native WebMCP tools through document.modelContext : revelus.search_information Searches published Revelus Dermatology information and returns the complete source-backed page card, including answer evidence, FAQs, relevant providers, and response guidance. revelus.resolve_visit_path Applies Revelus-specific, non-diagnostic scheduling logic to determine an appropriate appointment path using structured, non-identifying inputs. revelus.get_availability Reads current provider and appointment-time availability for a resolved path and returns the appropriate booking options. Search now returns the complete answer card in a single response, so a separate native answer tool is no longer necessary. The underlying shared action layer still supports answer behavior for the visible interface, but the final WebMCP implementation exposes three focused native tools. The system can therefore move through a sequence such as: Patient question → source-grounded information → appointment routing → live availability → patient-controlled booking We intentionally do not allow the tools to reserve or finalize an appointment. The patient leaves the WebMCP experience and completes that step through our existing secure scheduling workflow. We developed and tested the WebMCP experience in Google Chrome 149+ with WebMCP testing enabled and the ChatGPT desktop app's in-app browser , verifying that an AI agent could discover the tools, call them, combine their results, and work through questions that were not specifically scripted for the demo. Challenges we faced The most difficult part of the project was not exposing information. It was deciding how an agent should safely use that information in a real healthcare environment. Patient language is messy Patients do not necessarily use the terminology found on a medical website. They use misspellings, symptoms, partial descriptions, and conversational questions. The system needed to understand requests such as "rosecea," "my face gets really red and hot," or "my hair is thinning" without pretending that it had diagnosed the patient. Our approach was to surface relevant published information and recommend an appropriate evaluation rather than turning the matching system into a diagnostic tool. Scheduling logic is more complex than search A strong information match does not always equal the correct appointment. Some concerns require a dedicated visit.
