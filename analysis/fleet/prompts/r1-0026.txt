# FROZEN SCORING RUBRIC (pre-registered before any Stage 1 review)

Scores are integers 1-10 on each of the FOUR official criteria. Every score carries:
rationale, evidence_surfaces, confidence (0-1). Reviewers use the full scale and these
band anchors verbatim. Category neutrality is absolute: a game, art piece, music tool,
CRM, weird toy, and developer tool are all eligible for 10/10 on every criterion if they
satisfy that criterion exceptionally well FOR WHAT THEY ARE TRYING TO BE.
No aesthetic penalties. Signs of hurried AI work matter only as actual product-quality
problems (incoherence, broken flow, placeholder content), never as style.

## WebMCP Leverage — what does WebMCP change for this product?
Judge the delta WebMCP makes to reliability, precision, structured state access,
shared human-agent state, repeatability, and capability. Tool COUNT is not leverage.
- 9-10 TRANSFORMATIVE: the intended experience is impractical without the structured
  agent surface; human and agent share live product state; the collaboration could not
  be replicated comparably well by a general agent driving the UI.
- 7-8 MAJOR DELTA: without WebMCP a general agent is markedly less reliable/precise
  here; the agent is central to the product, not a bolt-on.
- 5-6 MEANINGFUL DELTA: existing capability packaged into a genuinely better product
  experience via structured tools; same outcome otherwise possible with real friction.
- 3-4 MINOR DELTA: generic browser automation achieves comparable outcomes; WebMCP is
  a convenience layer.
- 1-2 COSMETIC: exposes an action a UI button already offers; no meaningful change.

## Execution — is the actual product coherent, intentional, complete for its scope?
Stage 1 scores EXECUTION_CLAIMED: coherence/intentionality/completeness as evidenced by
the packet (description + packaging + video frames/transcript). Thin evidence caps the
score (end-to-end credit requires end-to-end evidence) and lowers confidence.
Stage 2 may rescore as EXECUTION_OBSERVED; latest evidence-backed score replaces, never
sums.
- 9-10: all surfaces agree; central workflow demonstrably completes; states handled;
  feels finished for its stated scope.
- 7-8: strong coherence and intentionality; central workflow appears functional; minor
  unfinished edges.
- 5-6: plausibly real with visible gaps; scope unclear or core not demonstrated
  end-to-end.
- 3-4: thin prototype; contradictions between surfaces; placeholder content.
- 1-2: non-functional or incoherent for its claimed scope.

## Creativity & Ambition — novelty of concept and interaction model
Judge ONLY: novelty of the concept, novelty of the interaction model, originality
relative to existing concepts, ambition, memorable/surprising use of the medium, and
the depth with which the concept is pursued. WebMCP indispensability belongs under
WebMCP Leverage, NOT here: a project can be extremely creative even if another agent
API could theoretically have enabled something similar.
- 9-10: genuinely novel concept or interaction model; ambitious scope pursued deeply
  and coherently; memorable.
- 7-8: fresh synthesis or notably original interaction; ambitious for a hackathon.
- 5-6: competent take on a known category with a real new angle.
- 3-4: derivative; novelty mostly surface details.
- 1-2: clone/template with trivial changes.
"That the category exists" is never a penalty; judge the submitted concept.

## Potential Impact — credible value for the intended audience
Judge: is there a real audience; is there a real problem, desire, or use case for that
audience; does the demonstrated solution genuinely address it. NO commercial adoption,
monetization, market pull, or startup viability is required. Entertainment, play, art,
creative expression, learning, niche hobby use, experimentation, and community value
are legitimate forms of impact. A tiny audience fully justifies a top score.
- 9-10: specific real audience with a real problem/desire; solution demonstrably
  addresses it; credible value, appeal, usefulness, or meaning for that audience.
- 7-8: clear audience and problem, good fit, credible value.
- 5-6: identifiable audience, looser fit or partial address.
- 3-4: speculative audience or strained fit; impact mostly aspirational.
- 1-2: no coherent audience/problem pairing.
Audience size is NOT a multiplier.

## Aggregate & ranking
aggregate = leverage + execution + impact + creativity (4-40). Nothing else enters.
Tie-breaks: Leverage, then Execution, then Impact, then Creativity, then higher
combined evidence confidence, then slug alphabetical. See PROTOCOL.md Ranking section.

Ask it for the answer and it refuses, leading with the reason (the answer is in no tool it can call), then asks one smaller question instead. Capability is the guardrail. advance_module does not exist until the gate opens; it materializes in the agent's tool list when mastery is earned. Exam mode revokes the coaching toolset entirely: train aided, test unaided. On screen, the Tool Roster reads 13 tools with the gate closed, 15 when it opens, 2 during the exam . The site's decisions are visible to learner and agent alike. The rubric interview is the centerpiece. The AI does what only AI can; the site does what AI must not. Zero AI infrastructure: static site, no backend, no API keys. The visitor brings the intelligence; the site brings the authority. Like Comment Story Updates Inspiration Architecture decisions usually fragment across diagramming tools, spreadsheets, cloud pricing tabs, chat transcripts, and ADR documents. The diagram stops matching the estimate; the rationale disappears; and the final artifacts are trapped in somebody else's database. We built WorkSpec Studio to make architecture a shared, inspectable workflow between a person and an AI agent, with portable files as the durable result. What it does WorkSpec Studio takes a project through one connected journey: Design the application as an interactive C4 model. Derive an editable, provider-neutral infrastructure shopping list from its deployable elements. Map the same requirements to Azure and AWS and compare deterministic monthly planning estimates. Prepare and review the cloud-platform decision. Record the approved choice as an architecture decision record. Download the complete .workspec workspace as a ZIP. A user can start blank, load an example, or import an existing .workspec bundle. The C4 canvas supports context, logical-container, and deployment views. People can create, move, connect, and edit elements directly; the agent can propose or arrange the same model through WebMCP. The resulting workspace is schema-validated YAML that can be reviewed in the browser, committed to Git, used by WorkSpec command-line tools, or consumed by a hosted WorkSpec Enterprise installation. Why WebMCP Architecture work combines two different strengths. Agents are good at turning intent into a coherent first draft and tracing consequences across many artifacts. People are responsible for understanding the system, correcting assumptions, weighing trade-offs, and approving durable decisions. Without WebMCP, an agent must manipulate a complex infinite canvas with fragile pointer automation or edit files somewhere outside the UI the person is reviewing. WorkSpec's WebMCP tools connect both participants to the same live browser-local workspace. The agent can create a structured C4 proposal in one operation while the person immediately sees a real diagram they can drag and edit. The infrastructure list is derived from that same model, the provider comparison uses that same list, and the ADR is generated only from the visible draft the person has approved. Every successful agent operation appears in a collapsible Agent activity history. How we built it The workbench is a TypeScript and React monorepo composed from reusable WorkSpec packages for C4 schemas and rendering, infinite-canvas interaction, topology planning, provider mapping, decisions, ZIP workspaces, and the shared Studio shell. The page registers eleven bounded tools with document.modelContext.registerTool(). They let an agent inspect the workspace, navigate the gated workflow, control relevant sidebars, create a complete C4 architecture, inspect and pin diagram layout, update infrastructure requirements, compare providers, prepare an unsaved decision, record a reviewed decision, and export the workspace ZIP. All tool inputs use closed JSON schemas. Handlers resolve the current application state through stable action references, and registrations share an AbortController so React Strict Mode cannot leave duplicate tools behind. Human UI actions and agent tools operate on the same canonical MemoryWorkspace. The cloud estimates are deterministic planning figures rather than live quotes, keeping the comparison reproducible. Provider-specific choices do not leak into the source architecture or neutral requirements. Human review is part of the protocol The decision flow is intentionally split into two tools. prepare_cloud_decision opens the Decision page and fills a visible, editable provider and rationale draft. record_cloud_decision rejects calls until that draft exists and is used only after explicit human approval. This makes review a real product boundary, not a sentence in an agent prompt. The export tool returns the exact current workspace as a bounded base64 ZIP. That gives agents a reliable handoff even in browser environments where clicking a download button is awkward, while people retain the ordinary visible download control. Challenges we faced The biggest challenge was making an infinite visual canvas genuinely agent-operable without taking control away from the person. We added semantic architecture and layout tools rather than exposing cursor coordinates, while keeping every generated node editable and draggable. We also had to keep one canonical state across four routed stages, preserve unknown files during ZIP round trips, prevent stale React closures in registered tools, and make irreversible decision recording impossible before a visible draft exists. Accomplishments we're proud of One continuous Design → Infrastructure → Compare → Decision story. A real C4 canvas that supports both semantic WebMCP operations and direct human editing. Provider-neutral requirements with traceability back to architecture elements. Like-for-like Azure and AWS mappings and monthly estimates. A visible approval gate before ADR generation. Complete .workspec ZIP import and export. Eleven WebMCP tools plus a clear Agent activity history. More than two thousand automated tests across the WorkSpec packages and applications. What we learned The best WebMCP tools describe meaningful product operations, not DOM gestures. A tool such as "prepare a decision for review" is more robust and more understandable than a sequence of clicks, and it lets the UI enforce the same rules for both humans and agents. We also learned that shared visibility matters as much as capability. The strongest moments are not when the agent finishes everything invisibly; they are the pauses where a proposal appears in the real UI, the person edits or approves it, and both continue from the same state. What's next Azure and AWS are the first provider adapters. The provider-neutral model is designed for additional clouds, on-premises targets, live pricing catalogs, policy checks, and organization-specific mapping rules. We also plan to reuse the extracted Studio packages directly inside WorkSpec Enterprise.
