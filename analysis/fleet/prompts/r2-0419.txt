=== RUBRIC (frozen) ===
# FROZEN SCORING RUBRIC (pre-registered before any Stage 1 review)

Scores are integers 1-10 on each of the FOUR official criteria. Every score carries:
rationale, evidence_surfaces, confidence (0-1). Reviewers use the full scale and these
band anchors verbatim. Category neutrality is absolute: a game, art piece, music tool,
CRM, weird toy, and developer tool are all eligible for 10/10 on every criterion if they
satisfy that criterion exceptionally well FOR WHAT THEY ARE TRYING TO BE.
No aesthetic penalties. Signs of hurried AI work matter only as actual product-quality
problems (incoherence, broken flow, placeholder content), never as style.

## WebMCP Leverage — what does WebMCP change for this product?
Judge the delta WebMCP makes to reliability, precision, structured state access,
shared human-agent state, repeatability, and capability. Tool COUNT is not leverage.
- 9-10 TRANSFORMATIVE: the intended experience is impractical without the structured
  agent surface; human and agent share live product state; the collaboration could not
  be replicated comparably well by a general agent driving the UI.
- 7-8 MAJOR DELTA: without WebMCP a general agent is markedly less reliable/precise
  here; the agent is central to the product, not a bolt-on.
- 5-6 MEANINGFUL DELTA: existing capability packaged into a genuinely better product
  experience via structured tools; same outcome otherwise possible with real friction.
- 3-4 MINOR DELTA: generic browser automation achieves comparable outcomes; WebMCP is
  a convenience layer.
- 1-2 COSMETIC: exposes an action a UI button already offers; no meaningful change.

## Execution — is the actual product coherent, intentional, complete for its scope?
Stage 1 scores EXECUTION_CLAIMED: coherence/intentionality/completeness as evidenced by
the packet (description + packaging + video frames/transcript). Thin evidence caps the
score (end-to-end credit requires end-to-end evidence) and lowers confidence.
Stage 2 may rescore as EXECUTION_OBSERVED; latest evidence-backed score replaces, never
sums.
- 9-10: all surfaces agree; central workflow demonstrably completes; states handled;
  feels finished for its stated scope.
- 7-8: strong coherence and intentionality; central workflow appears functional; minor
  unfinished edges.
- 5-6: plausibly real with visible gaps; scope unclear or core not demonstrated
  end-to-end.
- 3-4: thin prototype; contradictions between surfaces; placeholder content.
- 1-2: non-functional or incoherent for its claimed scope.

## Creativity & Ambition — novelty of concept and interaction model
Judge ONLY: novelty of the concept, novelty of the interaction model, originality
relative to existing concepts, ambition, memorable/surprising use of the medium, and
the depth with which the concept is pursued. WebMCP indispensability belongs under
WebMCP Leverage, NOT here: a project can be extremely creative even if another agent
API could theoretically have enabled something similar.
- 9-10: genuinely novel concept or interaction model; ambitious scope pursued deeply
  and coherently; memorable.
- 7-8: fresh synthesis or notably original interaction; ambitious for a hackathon.
- 5-6: competent take on a known category with a real new angle.
- 3-4: derivative; novelty mostly surface details.
- 1-2: clone/template with trivial changes.
"That the category exists" is never a penalty; judge the submitted concept.

## Potential Impact — credible value for the intended audience
Judge: is there a real audience; is there a real problem, desire, or use case for that
audience; does the demonstrated solution genuinely address it. NO commercial adoption,
monetization, market pull, or startup viability is required. Entertainment, play, art,
creative expression, learning, niche hobby use, experimentation, and community value
are legitimate forms of impact. A tiny audience fully justifies a top score.
- 9-10: specific real audience with a real problem/desire; solution demonstrably
  addresses it; credible value, appeal, usefulness, or meaning for that audience.
- 7-8: clear audience and problem, good fit, credible value.
- 5-6: identifiable audience, looser fit or partial address.
- 3-4: speculative audience or strained fit; impact mostly aspirational.
- 1-2: no coherent audience/problem pairing.
Audience size is NOT a multiplier.

## Aggregate & ranking
aggregate = leverage + execution + impact + creativity (4-40). Nothing else enters.
Tie-breaks: Leverage, then Execution, then Impact, then Creativity, then higher
combined evidence confidence, then slug alphabetical. See PROTOCOL.md Ranking section.

Inspiration Construction field reviews are done standing on a slab with a tablet, a set of PDF drawings, and last visit's open items. The record that comes out of that walk is a professional document. A generic assistant can summarise notes, but it cannot safely touch the same numbered record the reviewer is looking at. WebMCP is the first thing that lets the page itself hand the agent a small, typed set of actions bound to the project already open, under the reviewer's own identity. What it does PunchPilot is a full field-review workspace on Cloudflare Workers: projects with disciplines and a filed drawing set, numbered reviews per discipline, findings pinned as page-aware percentages on the PDF, photos, status history, per-review checklists, and a frozen issued report for each visit. Every project screen registers six WebMCP Site tools with document.modelContext.registerTool : get_field_review_context reads the open project, discipline, latest review, and unresolved carry-forward findings. list_open_findings reads the work list. start_or_resume_field_review resumes the active review or opens the next numbered one for a valid floor, discipline, and drawing. create_draft_finding saves a draft pin at a coordinate on the active sheet and selects it on screen. update_or_verify_finding updates a finding; verification is refused unless the item is already corrected, a human confirms it on site, and a note is given. prepare_field_review_briefing assembles a live-data briefing of raised, revisited, and carried findings and issues nothing. There is deliberately no tool that verifies on the agent's word, completes a review, or issues a report. Why WebMCP is the right fit The value is in the binding, not the chat. The tools are registered by the page that already knows which project, discipline, review, and sheet are open, so ChatGPT never has to guess an identifier or scrape a screenshot. Each tool calls the same authenticated API route the buttons call, with the same validation, the same audit trail, and the same identity the worker resolved at the edge. The drawing, the finding log, and the history update in front of the reviewer as the agent works, so every action is visible and checkable on the spot. That is the kind of trust a professional record needs, and it is exactly what a page-registered tool gives that an external API integration would not. How WebMCP makes the experience better Without it, the reviewer types findings on a keyboard while holding a tablet, re-reads last visit's list to know what to revisit, and rebuilds the briefing by hand at the truck. With it, they say "draft a finding at 62 across, 49 down, fire-rated door closer missing at corridor 205, warning, assign the site super" and the pin lands on the sheet with a number. They ask "what is still open on Level 2 electrical" and get the real list. They ask for the briefing and get raised, revisited, and carried counts from live data. Fewer taps in gloves, no retyping, and nothing invented, because every answer comes from the record. What people and agents accomplish together The agent does retrieval, consistent numbering, structured drafts, and the briefing. The human does what only a person on site can: decide what is physically there, whether a correction is acceptable, and whether the record is ready to issue. The tool schemas encode that split. Verification requires humanConfirmedOnSite , a corrected status, and a note. Completion and issuance are buttons, not tools. The briefing is marked draft and carries externalAction: false . Design tomorrow’s living spaces, one room and invention at a time. Like Comment Story Updates 1 Design future living spaces through natural conversation. A user-created future floor plan, edited through WebMCP. Export the current floor plan exactly as it appears. Inspect, propose, validate, and apply floor-plan changes. Inspiration When I first used a computer as a child, there was something I wanted even more than a game: software that would let me design my own floor plans. I dreamed of moving walls, expanding rooms, and placing devices that did not yet exist—as naturally as drawing ideas on paper. Today, many floor-planning tools are available. However, I wondered whether designing a home could become more accessible and imaginative if people could simply describe what they wanted instead of learning specialized controls. That idea inspired me to build HABITAT//NULL, a future floor-plan simulator powered by WebMCP. What It Does HABITAT//NULL is a floor-plan simulator where users and AI collaboratively imagine future living spaces. Users can describe changes in natural language, such as: “Make the kitchen larger.” “Split this room at a 60/40 ratio.” “Turn this area into a parking space.” Through WebMCP, the AI can inspect the current floor plan, propose a change, validate it, and apply it to the design. The project is not limited to conventional furniture. It is also designed to let users invent and place their own futuristic tools, devices, and living systems. Completed floor plans can be saved as PNG or PDF files exactly as they appear on the screen. Rather than simply optimizing existing homes, HABITAT//NULL aims to turn imagined ways of living—things that do not yet exist—into visible spaces. How I Built It The floor plan is managed as structured data representing rooms, walls, openings, furniture, and equipment. The visual floor plan is generated from this underlying data. I created WebMCP tools that allow AI to inspect the current layout, propose modifications, validate the result, and apply an approved proposal. This allows the AI to understand and manipulate the structure of the floor plan instead of relying on screen coordinates or button positions. Changes are first created as proposals rather than being applied immediately. After the proposal has been reviewed, the exact change can be committed to the current floor plan. The visual design combines understated science fiction with the nostalgic feeling of a hand-drawn architectural plan. I wanted the interface to suggest a quiet, deteriorated future where advanced equipment still survives among worn materials and old records. Challenges The most difficult challenge was translating ambiguous natural-language requests into precise floor-plan operations. For example, “Make the kitchen larger” raises several questions: Which wall should move? How much space should be taken from the neighboring room? What should happen to doors, furniture, and connected spaces? Changing a room’s size requires more than updating its dimensions. The relationships among walls, openings, routes, and placed objects must remain consistent. Another challenge was making the entire workflow possible through WebMCP. Creating a proposal was not enough—the AI also needed to inspect, validate, and apply the change without requiring unnecessary manual interaction. It was also important to avoid adding too many unrelated features. I continually returned to the project’s essential experience: letting users reshape a floor plan through conversation. What I Learned This project taught me that WebMCP is more than a way to automate browser interactions. It can become a structured communication layer between an AI agent and a web application. When the AI understands the meaning of rooms, walls, and openings—not just their appearance on the screen—users can express their intentions naturally without learning specialized software controls. I also learned that giving an AI freedom is not enough to create a trustworthy experience. Users need to see proposed changes, verify the results, and clearly understand what will be applied. A proposal-and-approval workflow makes collaboration with AI both safer and easier to understand. What’s Next Next, I want to give users more freedom to create and place their own furniture, machines, and imagined future systems. I would also like to support multi-story buildings and unconventional environments such as fantasy homes, spacecraft, and underground shelters. My goal is to develop HABITAT//NULL into more than a tool for selecting or editing existing floor plans. I want it to become a place where users and AI can invent new ways of living together.
