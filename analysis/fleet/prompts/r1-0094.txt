# FROZEN SCORING RUBRIC (pre-registered before any Stage 1 review)

Scores are integers 1-10 on each of the FOUR official criteria. Every score carries:
rationale, evidence_surfaces, confidence (0-1). Reviewers use the full scale and these
band anchors verbatim. Category neutrality is absolute: a game, art piece, music tool,
CRM, weird toy, and developer tool are all eligible for 10/10 on every criterion if they
satisfy that criterion exceptionally well FOR WHAT THEY ARE TRYING TO BE.
No aesthetic penalties. Signs of hurried AI work matter only as actual product-quality
problems (incoherence, broken flow, placeholder content), never as style.

## WebMCP Leverage — what does WebMCP change for this product?
Judge the delta WebMCP makes to reliability, precision, structured state access,
shared human-agent state, repeatability, and capability. Tool COUNT is not leverage.
- 9-10 TRANSFORMATIVE: the intended experience is impractical without the structured
  agent surface; human and agent share live product state; the collaboration could not
  be replicated comparably well by a general agent driving the UI.
- 7-8 MAJOR DELTA: without WebMCP a general agent is markedly less reliable/precise
  here; the agent is central to the product, not a bolt-on.
- 5-6 MEANINGFUL DELTA: existing capability packaged into a genuinely better product
  experience via structured tools; same outcome otherwise possible with real friction.
- 3-4 MINOR DELTA: generic browser automation achieves comparable outcomes; WebMCP is
  a convenience layer.
- 1-2 COSMETIC: exposes an action a UI button already offers; no meaningful change.

## Execution — is the actual product coherent, intentional, complete for its scope?
Stage 1 scores EXECUTION_CLAIMED: coherence/intentionality/completeness as evidenced by
the packet (description + packaging + video frames/transcript). Thin evidence caps the
score (end-to-end credit requires end-to-end evidence) and lowers confidence.
Stage 2 may rescore as EXECUTION_OBSERVED; latest evidence-backed score replaces, never
sums.
- 9-10: all surfaces agree; central workflow demonstrably completes; states handled;
  feels finished for its stated scope.
- 7-8: strong coherence and intentionality; central workflow appears functional; minor
  unfinished edges.
- 5-6: plausibly real with visible gaps; scope unclear or core not demonstrated
  end-to-end.
- 3-4: thin prototype; contradictions between surfaces; placeholder content.
- 1-2: non-functional or incoherent for its claimed scope.

## Creativity & Ambition — novelty of concept and interaction model
Judge ONLY: novelty of the concept, novelty of the interaction model, originality
relative to existing concepts, ambition, memorable/surprising use of the medium, and
the depth with which the concept is pursued. WebMCP indispensability belongs under
WebMCP Leverage, NOT here: a project can be extremely creative even if another agent
API could theoretically have enabled something similar.
- 9-10: genuinely novel concept or interaction model; ambitious scope pursued deeply
  and coherently; memorable.
- 7-8: fresh synthesis or notably original interaction; ambitious for a hackathon.
- 5-6: competent take on a known category with a real new angle.
- 3-4: derivative; novelty mostly surface details.
- 1-2: clone/template with trivial changes.
"That the category exists" is never a penalty; judge the submitted concept.

## Potential Impact — credible value for the intended audience
Judge: is there a real audience; is there a real problem, desire, or use case for that
audience; does the demonstrated solution genuinely address it. NO commercial adoption,
monetization, market pull, or startup viability is required. Entertainment, play, art,
creative expression, learning, niche hobby use, experimentation, and community value
are legitimate forms of impact. A tiny audience fully justifies a top score.
- 9-10: specific real audience with a real problem/desire; solution demonstrably
  addresses it; credible value, appeal, usefulness, or meaning for that audience.
- 7-8: clear audience and problem, good fit, credible value.
- 5-6: identifiable audience, looser fit or partial address.
- 3-4: speculative audience or strained fit; impact mostly aspirational.
- 1-2: no coherent audience/problem pairing.
Audience size is NOT a multiplier.

## Aggregate & ranking
aggregate = leverage + execution + impact + creativity (4-40). Nothing else enters.
Tie-breaks: Leverage, then Execution, then Impact, then Creativity, then higher
combined evidence confidence, then slug alphabetical. See PROTOCOL.md Ranking section.

The seat was available. The journey was not. WebMCP plans the route, lift, companion seat and assistance as one revalidated bundle. Like Comment Story Updates The venue knew. The booking didn't. A wheelchair space can be available even when the journey to it is not usable. A route can contain steps. A lift can go out of service. The adjacent companion seat or entrance-assistance capacity can change before the booking is confirmed. That mismatch became the design question behind this prototype: what if the unit of booking were not the seat alone, but the accessible journey that makes the seat usable? No Seat Without a Route links the route, lift status, wheelchair space, adjacent companion seat and entrance assistance in one plan. At confirmation, the server evaluates that plan again against the current venue state. It either commits the wheelchair space, companion seat and assistance together, or reserves none of them. Federal ADA ticketing regulations require covered public entities and public accommodations that sell tickets for a single event or series of events to give individuals with disabilities an equal opportunity to purchase accessible seating, including during the same hours and through the same distribution methods used for other seating. Upon inquiry, entities that sell or distribute those tickets must identify and describe available accessible seating in enough detail for an individual with a disability to assess whether a particular seating location meets their accessibility needs ( DOJ ticket-sales guidance , 28 CFR § 35.138 and 28 CFR § 36.302(f) ). This prototype explores a product opportunity in that context. It does not claim that the ADA requires this route-and-booking model, or that the prototype establishes legal compliance. The failure is the demo A visitor asks their browser agent for a step-free route for a 72 cm wheelchair, under 80 metres, with one companion, a quieter entrance and somebody waiting at the door. The page exposes structured WebMCP tools. The agent reads the current synthetic venue state and stages a complete plan through East Entrance and East Lift L2. The route and its named dependencies appear on the page for review. Planning reserves nothing. The demo then introduces a race condition between review and confirmation. A visible control arms a deterministic fault on East Lift L2, but the lift remains operational until the next valid confirmation attempt. When the visible confirmation control is pressed, the server applies the outage, advances the venue revision and refuses the now-stale plan with STALE_RESOURCE_VERSION . The result shows 0 partial reservations : no wheelchair space, companion seat or assistance slot was held on its own. The agent calls explain_access_refusal . It receives the failed LIFT_OPERATIONAL rule, the old and current venue revisions, the zero-partial-reservation result and the Garden route that remains available. It replans through Garden Entrance and Garden Lift L4 without requiring the six inputs again. The replacement is reviewed and confirmed through the visible page. The server checks the current venue state once more, then commits wheelchair space W12, companion seat W13 and entrance assistance in one transition. A happy path would show only that a plan can become a booking. The demonstrated failure path shows the harder part: stale state books nothing, the refusal explains what changed, and the agent has a structured way to recover. Why WebMCP fits The booking page is both the human-facing interface and the agent surface. WebMCP gives the browser agent structured, site-authored tools. In the demonstrated stale-plan flow, the staged plan, refusal and replacement are rendered in that same page for review. Tool availability follows the booking state. Search and staging are removed when they no longer apply. After the demonstrated stale-plan refusal, explanation and replanning become available. Once a booking exists, the visitor tool surface is read-only. No registered WebMCP tool can prepare or commit a booking confirmation. In the intended page flow, confirmation is a visible page action. This is a tool-surface boundary, not an identity guarantee: the prototype has no user or operator identity authentication and does not prove who pressed a control. The ordinary form and buttons complete the same visitor flow when WebMCP is unavailable. This shared page state is why I built the project with WebMCP instead of placing an agent beside a separate booking API. The structured actions, map, failure explanation and confirmation control stay in one interface instead of becoming two experiences that need to be kept in sync. How I built it The client is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript. A zero-dependency Node.js service holds synthetic venue state in memory and runs on Render. The visitor code defines nine imperative tools and registers only the subset valid in the current state. A separate operator demo page defines three facility tools over the same synthetic venue state. It has no operator identity authentication and is not an authorization boundary. In the Chromium hosts I tested that exposed the declarative contract, the visible requirements form also appeared as a tool, deliberately without automatic submission. Planning, explanation and final revalidation use the same route-evaluation implementation. Every plan records the venue revision against which it was built. Confirmation checks that revision again and moves every reservable resource in one synchronous in-memory state transition. For expected browser-facing domain refusals, the first-party pages request a typed ok: false envelope. The result keeps its error code and original domain status, while the expected safety decision does not appear as a failed network request. Raw API clients still receive conventional 4xx responses, and unexpected failures remain 5xx. What broke, and what I learned During earlier testing in Chrome 151, I observed that unregistering a tool while its own call was returning could cancel the in-flight call. I fixed that by delaying the tool-surface update until the execution had settled. Across the hosts I tested, I also observed different enforcement of required fields at the host boundary. The tool wrapper, visible form and server therefore each validate the input that reaches them. While auditing the regression suite, I found a test that removed the very counterexample it was meant to detect before asserting that the list was empty. The test passed while its claim was false. Since then, for release-critical repairs, I have restored the defect deliberately, confirmed that the regression test fails, and only then put the fix back. I manually exercised the deployed app in ChatGPT desktop's in-app browser on 2 September 2026. The automated browser run used Chrome 152.0.7977.66 and also exercised Edge 152.0.4191.53. Firefox 155 was checked only as a manual, non-WebMCP fallback. These are observations from those specific versions, not a claim of compatibility with every browser or agent. The current release gate passed 740/740 Node tests and 389/389 browser checks . Current scope This is a focused synthetic prototype: two routes, one event, in-memory inventory, no payment, no real ticket and no user or operator identity authentication. The visitor and operator labels describe demo roles, not authenticated identities. The operator page is an open demo control surface, not a production authorization boundary. Production would need durable storage, real authentication and authorization, live ticketing and facility feeds, outbound notifications, and testing with wheelchair users and venue access teams. The same transaction pattern may be worth testing in other journeys where one missing dependency can make the rest of a reservation unusable, such as rail transfers, airports, hotels or hospitals. Before they ever go live, they may have to manage chat commands, alerts, timed promotions, overlays, clipping highlights for TikTok, and countless other pieces of production across a dozen different tabs. Meanwhile, AI agents are still largely stuck outside the product. They screen-scrape interfaces, guess which buttons to click, and operate without a real understanding of the application's state. We wanted to change that. StreamBot is a real streamer dashboard already in production use. The WebMCP Challenge gave us the missing piece: instead of bolting an AI chatbot onto the side of the product, we gave the agent a first-class seat inside the application . The agent uses the same tools as the streamer, operates against the same state, and leaves the same audit trail. That led to the question driving this project: What does setup feel like when a streamer and their AI agent share one workspace, while the human remains in control of anything that matters? What It Does Three experiences define StreamBot Agent Edition. 1. Propose, Don't Just Do The agent isn't simply given unrestricted control. A streamer can say: "Set up my horror night." Instead of immediately changing their dashboard, the agent creates a complete themed proposal: !scary and !brave commands 👻 Follow alerts Timed promotional messages A JUMPSCARE channel-point reward A follower goal bar Other relevant stream configuration The entire setup appears as a review card . The streamer can inspect the proposal, remove anything they don't want, and then hit Approve . Only after approval does the agent execute the changes. Proposal → Review → Approval → Execution It's collaboration with a veto, rather than blind automation. 2. Guides That Teach on the Live UI Agents shouldn't teach users from outdated screenshots or hallucinated interfaces. If a streamer asks: "How do chat alerts work?" The agent first uses discover_targets to inspect the actual DOM and identify the elements currently available on the page. It then uses compose_tour to build a spotlight walkthrough directly against those discovered elements. The result is a guide that operates on the real product, in its current state . No stale screenshots. No guessed buttons. No hallucinated UI. The agent discovers the interface first, then teaches from reality. 3. Skilled Hands, Not Chat The agent isn't limited to answering questions. Inside StreamBot's ClipIt editor, it can stage vertical social edits directly against the mounted editor state. That includes: Vertical layouts Facecam resizing Caption placement Real transcribed captions Designer-created caption presets Editor configuration The agent isn't pretending to edit a clip through text. It's operating the actual editor. How We Built It with WebMCP Route-Scoped Tool Suites Each StreamBot dashboard route registers the tools relevant to the feature currently being viewed. Examples include: Commands Alerts Timed Messages Rewards Canvas ClipIt This is implemented through src/webmcp/suites.ts . The agent only sees tools that are valid for the current context and available to its seat. The Agent Uses the Same API as the UI We didn't create a separate "AI version" of StreamBot's functionality. Every agent tool calls the same endpoints used by the human interface . That means the resulting state is identical regardless of who performed the action. For example: Agent creates !scary → the command immediately appears in the streamer's command list. There is no synchronization layer or second source of truth. Agent actions and human actions operate on the same application state. Tool executions are also written to the account's audit log, just like actions performed by other members of the team. A Bridge into Live Editors For ClipIt, we needed the agent to interact with a mounted editor rather than a traditional API. We built mountClipItEditorCommands , which connects the WebMCP suite directly to the ClipIt store. The bridge dispatches real reducer actions and waits for state convergence before reporting success. That distinction matters. We don't report success because an action was dispatched. We report success when the editor has actually reached the requested state. Human-in-the-Loop Primitives Human approval is built into the tool architecture itself. propose_stream_setup opens a PrepProposalCard and blocks execution until the streamer makes a decision. Our guide tools work similarly: they compose tours from curated block types that can only be placed on targets discovered from the current page. That seemed reasonable until we encountered a simple case: A facecam was already in the requested position. The operation succeeded, but because the pixels didn't change, the bridge waited 12 seconds and eventually reported a timeout. We rewrote the system around state convergence instead. Every walkthrough begins with discover_targets . The tour is then composed against what actually exists. What We Learned The biggest lesson was simple: Agents Don't Need More Autonomy. They Need Better Seats. Once the agent shared the human's: Tools Application state Permissions Audit trail Context the interaction fundamentally changed. It stopped feeling like: "Prompt and pray." And started feeling like genuine teamwork: Proposal → Review → Approval → Execution The guardrails aren't friction. Scoped tool suites, approval cards, permission boundaries, and discover-before-teach aren't there to make the agent weaker. They're what make the agent trustworthy enough to delegate to . That's the direction we believe the agent-native web should move toward: AI agents shouldn't sit outside applications, guessing how to use them. They should have a real seat inside the product with the right tools, the right context, and a human who stays in control. What it does MarketPane is a WebMCP-powered stock screener and research workbench shared between a human and an agent. Results land in a panel grid: a results table the agent or the human can inspect, and — via drag-and-drop or a tool call — individual rows become live price charts on the same canvas. How we built it The frontend is SvelteKit 5 (SPA) with a browser-side panel/grid system and a workspace document that both the UI and the WebMCP tool layer read and write. Like 1 Comment Story Updates ForgeMCP — Multi-Agent Research & Game Studio ForgeMCP is an agent-native web platform where people and AI agents use real browser-native WebMCP tools to observe Earth, test a deterministic 8×8×8 chess engine, create verifiable 3D systems, and keep consequential decisions under human control. Core workflow HUMAN → COORDINATOR → SPECIALIST AGENTS → WEBMCP TOOLS → REAL DATA / ENGINE → VERIFICATION → HUMAN DECISION What it does ForgeMCP brings three working systems into one visible, auditable workflow: OBSERVE — Terra Observation System A user can select or search for an area of interest and investigate environmental change using public Earth-observation sources. Terra can retrieve location context, bounded research areas, representative satellite imagery, terrain/elevation context, hydrology signals and public hazard observations. Results return structured provenance, timestamps, confidence, uncertainty and a verification state. Terra never presents an AI-generated picture as satellite evidence. Missing or failed provider responses remain INSUFFICIENT_DATA or NOT_CONNECTED . Observations, anomalies, hypotheses, preliminary risk alerts and verified findings are kept separate. A hypothesis is not promoted to a fact without supporting evidence and, where required, field or in-situ verification. LEARN & COMPETE — Cube Chess 512 AI Cube Chess 512 is a deterministic three-dimensional chess laboratory with an 8×8×8 board and 512 addressable fields. ForgeMCP uses a pinned rules engine to execute a small candidate-versus-baseline benchmark with paired seeds and side swaps. The records contain replayable legal moves, results, termination state, legality checks, a deterministic material proxy and engine provenance. The application does not invent Elo ratings, game counts or training results. A candidate cannot be promoted merely because an agent recommends it: legality and regression gates must pass, a recorded result must exist, and literal human approval is required. CREATE — Game and 3D Creation Studio The Game Studio lets a person and agents configure procedural station concepts, generate deterministic low-poly glTF assets and PNG textures, inspect the exact generated geometry, compare visible results, run QA and export a manifest. It also includes a deterministic basic Capture Chess blueprint and a separate link to the playable Cube Chess 512 deployment. Concept artwork is labelled as concept artwork. A generated prototype is not described as manufactured hardware, an original satellite product or a production-ready model. How we built it The public application is built with React, TypeScript, Vite and browser-based 3D rendering. Real tools are registered through: document.modelContext.registerTool(...) The coordinator is deterministic and does not claim that an LLM ran when it did not. The live tool inventory covers system status, place and AOI handling, Terra observation and verification workflows, the pinned Cube benchmark and promotion gates, visual QA, reversible actions and guarded commerce drafts. The dashboard exposes the human request, coordinator decision, active specialist, WebMCP tool, validated input, real source or engine, structured output, provenance, confidence, uncertainty, verification result, proposed action and human approval state. The hardest challenges The hardest problem was not adding more AI. It was defining where AI must stop. For Terra, the authority must be the original observation, provider metadata, measurement method and verification record. For Cube, the authority must be the deterministic rules engine, legal execution and saved game result. For mutations, the authority remains the human. A second challenge was integrating two projects that existed before the competition without presenting older work as new. The public repository therefore separates: PRE-CHALLENGE WORK: earlier Terra data integrations, Earth-observation research, Cube rules engine, self-play experiments, 3D models and game UI. NEW WEBMCP CHALLENGE WORK: ForgeMCP coordinator, browser-native tool registration, integrated Terra/Cube/Create workflows, structured provenance, verification timeline, approval gates, public dashboard, judge path and demo. A collaborative educational tool to learn system design Like Comment Story Updates The studio The simulation with the result Agent modifying the canvas on the web app using web MCP end points Testing whether a specific system design solution scales with the help of the agent and websit And finally the fix Finding bottleneck Inspiration System design interviews still happen on a whiteboard. You draw boxes, a senior engineer pokes holes, and you learn by defending the sketch under load. We wanted that loop in a tab . Archflow is the whiteboard. The agent is the senior — or the teacher — who can read the same diagram, ask about the box you just clicked, suggest a cache, and say “run it again.” Web MCP becomes a perfect fit here because the agent with its vast knowledge can view the whiteboard that you are using and guide you when needed. By exposing the whiteboard along with the capability to edit it, you have the perfect sparring partner while learning system design. Not a chatbot next to a picture. A practice studio for the interview: you sketch, they guide, the SLO keeps score. What it does You pick a use case (URL shortener, news feed, realtime chat, video streaming). Each one drops you into a naive first sketch that misses its SLO — the same kind of incomplete design an interviewer might start you from. Drag blocks like a whiteboard: clients, CDNs, APIs, caches, queues, databases. Hit Run . Traffic walks the graph. Overflow becomes errors; p99 is the hottest sync path; robustness is $0$–$100$. Ask the agent to play senior: “What’s wrong with this shortener?” or “Help me pass the SLO without exploding cost.” It uses WebMCP tools on the live canvas ( get_architecture , get_selected_node , connect , run_simulation , …) so guidance shows up as rewires and a second run — not a paragraph of advice you have to copy by hand. Change the graph, run again. The inspector tells you if the design got better or worse — the same feedback a good interviewer gives, with numbers. How we built it Next.js, React Flow, Zustand, usewebmcp . No backend: the tab is the session , like a physical whiteboard that happens to simulate. Archflow registers 16 WebMCP tools on the page. Read • get_architecture — Current graph: challenge, node ids, edges, call trees, last simulation. • get_catalog — Block types you can add, default capacity, and connection rules. • get_selected_node — The block the human has selected, what it means, neighbors, and last-run stats. • get_robustness — Last run’s robustness score (0–100) and whether the latest change helped or hurt. • get_simulation — Last run: utilization, p99, errors, bottlenecks, SLO, robustness, and delta. • list_challenges — The four system-design use cases. Change the board • load_challenge — Reset the canvas to a starter sketch (url-shortener, news-feed, realtime-chat, video-streaming). • add_node — Add a block; optional id, label, and nearId for placement. • connect — Connect two blocks left-to-right (queue inbound defaults to async). • disconnect — Remove the edge between two blocks. • set_node_props — Change replicas, RPS capacity, cache hit rate, or lock a node. • annotate_node — Pin a finding on a block so it shows on the graph. • remove_node — Remove a block and its edges (asks the human to confirm). • repair_graph — Drop dangling edges and broken/unknown blocks so the graph can run. • run_simulation — Push traffic through the graph, animate packets, and score the SLO. Batch • apply_plan — Apply a list of ops in order (add_node, connect, disconnect, set_node_props, remove_node) after one confirmation. What we learned The agent is useful as a teacher only if it shares the board. Taking a screenshot and pasting every time you want to show the agent that something has changed is painful. Web MCP becomes a perfect fit in such situations since the agent gets first hand view of what is happening without the user having to intervene. Challenges Identifying what are the key capabilities to be exposed as web MCP end points required some serious thinking.
