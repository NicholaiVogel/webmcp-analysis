# FROZEN SCORING RUBRIC (pre-registered before any Stage 1 review)

Scores are integers 1-10 on each of the FOUR official criteria. Every score carries:
rationale, evidence_surfaces, confidence (0-1). Reviewers use the full scale and these
band anchors verbatim. Category neutrality is absolute: a game, art piece, music tool,
CRM, weird toy, and developer tool are all eligible for 10/10 on every criterion if they
satisfy that criterion exceptionally well FOR WHAT THEY ARE TRYING TO BE.
No aesthetic penalties. Signs of hurried AI work matter only as actual product-quality
problems (incoherence, broken flow, placeholder content), never as style.

## WebMCP Leverage — what does WebMCP change for this product?
Judge the delta WebMCP makes to reliability, precision, structured state access,
shared human-agent state, repeatability, and capability. Tool COUNT is not leverage.
- 9-10 TRANSFORMATIVE: the intended experience is impractical without the structured
  agent surface; human and agent share live product state; the collaboration could not
  be replicated comparably well by a general agent driving the UI.
- 7-8 MAJOR DELTA: without WebMCP a general agent is markedly less reliable/precise
  here; the agent is central to the product, not a bolt-on.
- 5-6 MEANINGFUL DELTA: existing capability packaged into a genuinely better product
  experience via structured tools; same outcome otherwise possible with real friction.
- 3-4 MINOR DELTA: generic browser automation achieves comparable outcomes; WebMCP is
  a convenience layer.
- 1-2 COSMETIC: exposes an action a UI button already offers; no meaningful change.

## Execution — is the actual product coherent, intentional, complete for its scope?
Stage 1 scores EXECUTION_CLAIMED: coherence/intentionality/completeness as evidenced by
the packet (description + packaging + video frames/transcript). Thin evidence caps the
score (end-to-end credit requires end-to-end evidence) and lowers confidence.
Stage 2 may rescore as EXECUTION_OBSERVED; latest evidence-backed score replaces, never
sums.
- 9-10: all surfaces agree; central workflow demonstrably completes; states handled;
  feels finished for its stated scope.
- 7-8: strong coherence and intentionality; central workflow appears functional; minor
  unfinished edges.
- 5-6: plausibly real with visible gaps; scope unclear or core not demonstrated
  end-to-end.
- 3-4: thin prototype; contradictions between surfaces; placeholder content.
- 1-2: non-functional or incoherent for its claimed scope.

## Creativity & Ambition — novelty of concept and interaction model
Judge ONLY: novelty of the concept, novelty of the interaction model, originality
relative to existing concepts, ambition, memorable/surprising use of the medium, and
the depth with which the concept is pursued. WebMCP indispensability belongs under
WebMCP Leverage, NOT here: a project can be extremely creative even if another agent
API could theoretically have enabled something similar.
- 9-10: genuinely novel concept or interaction model; ambitious scope pursued deeply
  and coherently; memorable.
- 7-8: fresh synthesis or notably original interaction; ambitious for a hackathon.
- 5-6: competent take on a known category with a real new angle.
- 3-4: derivative; novelty mostly surface details.
- 1-2: clone/template with trivial changes.
"That the category exists" is never a penalty; judge the submitted concept.

## Potential Impact — credible value for the intended audience
Judge: is there a real audience; is there a real problem, desire, or use case for that
audience; does the demonstrated solution genuinely address it. NO commercial adoption,
monetization, market pull, or startup viability is required. Entertainment, play, art,
creative expression, learning, niche hobby use, experimentation, and community value
are legitimate forms of impact. A tiny audience fully justifies a top score.
- 9-10: specific real audience with a real problem/desire; solution demonstrably
  addresses it; credible value, appeal, usefulness, or meaning for that audience.
- 7-8: clear audience and problem, good fit, credible value.
- 5-6: identifiable audience, looser fit or partial address.
- 3-4: speculative audience or strained fit; impact mostly aspirational.
- 1-2: no coherent audience/problem pairing.
Audience size is NOT a multiplier.

## Aggregate & ranking
aggregate = leverage + execution + impact + creativity (4-40). Nothing else enters.
Tie-breaks: Leverage, then Execution, then Impact, then Creativity, then higher
combined evidence confidence, then slug alphabetical. See PROTOCOL.md Ranking section.

I wanted a single tab where the code, the live preview, the audit and the AI all look at the same thing. Collaborate and fork — real-time multi-cursor collaboration over Yjs, forks and merge requests, a community showcase. Like Comment Story Updates Tradeoff Cafe Simulator Inspiration Small business owners make difficult decisions all the time, usually without the data, tools or spare time that larger businesses have. A café owner might notice long queues on a Saturday and think about adding another barista. But that simple change affects wages, customer numbers, service speed and weekly profit. These decisions are often made through experience or instinct. Experience is valuable, but it can be difficult to compare several options or understand every trade-off before making a change. I built Tradeoff Café to explore what this could look like with an agent involved. The owner still decides what matters and sets the boundaries. The agent does the repetitive testing, while the simulator handles the actual calculations. What it does Tradeoff Café is a browser-based decision simulator for a fictional café called Wicks Lane Coffee in Marrickville, Sydney. Owners can adjust: Staff numbers across different days and shifts Menu prices Opening hours Food and drink preparation levels The application simulates the café minute by minute and shows how those choices affect profit, revenue, queues, customers served, customers lost and waste. Users can save different scenarios, compare them side by side and inspect the calculations behind each result. The project is not described as AI-powered because the agent does not calculate the answers. The simulation engine owns the numbers. WebMCP simply gives an agent a safe and structured way to operate the same controls available to the user. Human and agent collaboration The most important part of the project is the relationship between the café owner, the agent and the application. The owner provides the real-world judgement. They decide what matters, set non-negotiable limits and lock any settings that should not be changed. For example, the owner might ask to reduce Saturday lunch waits without lowering weekly profit or changing Saturday's opening hours. The agent handles the repetitive work. It can read the current café setup, test staffing and pricing changes, run simulations, compare the results and save the strongest option. The application remains the source of truth. It performs every calculation, checks every constraint and records every action. The agent cannot silently replace the café's baseline plan. It can only suggest the change, and the owner must approve it. This creates a practical form of collaboration: the human provides context and makes the decision, while the agent explores the options and explains the trade-offs. How I built it I built Tradeoff Café with Next.js, React, TypeScript and Zustand. The simulation engine models a representative trading week using seeded, minute-by-minute calculations. It includes customer demand, queue behaviour, staffing capacity, preparation levels, waste, Australian wage assumptions, operating costs and GST. Seeded simulation means the same inputs and seed produce the same results, making comparisons repeatable and easier to trust. The interface includes editable controls, a visual week board, headline results, comparison charts, a scenario library, an activity log and a Show the working ledger. The ledger explains how revenue, labour, cost of sales, overheads and profit were calculated. I implemented WebMCP by exposing six tools directly from the webpage: get_cafe_context set_parameters run_simulation read_metrics save_scenario compare_scenarios These tools use the same store actions as the visible buttons. When an agent changes the roster, the controls, simulation results, charts and activity log all update on the page. There is no separate agent-only calculation or hidden copy of the application state. Challenges One challenge was making the café model realistic enough to create meaningful trade-offs. If staffing had no clear cost, the best answer would always be to add more people. If higher prices did not affect demand, the best answer would always be to charge more. I had to balance these relationships so that each decision had both benefits and consequences. Another challenge was keeping the agent and the user completely synchronized. I solved this by routing both tool calls and interface actions through the same state operations. This means the owner can always see what the agent changed. Safety and control were also important. The agent must respect locked settings and owner-defined limits. Invalid actions return clear explanations, and changing the baseline requires explicit human approval. Finally, the results needed to be understandable. A number alone is not enough for a small business owner. The interface therefore shows comparisons, plain-language summaries, likely ranges and a detailed financial ledger. What I learned I learned that WebMCP is most useful when it supports human judgement rather than trying to replace it. Agents are good at testing many combinations and handling repetitive work, but the owner understands the business, its customers and its real-world constraints. I also learned that trust depends on visibility. Users should be able to see what changed, why it changed and where every result came from. Giving an agent tools is only part of the experience; the actions and outcomes must also remain clear to the person making the final decision. Tradeoff Café shows how a normal interactive website can become a shared workspace for a person and an agent. The owner makes the decisions, the agent does the legwork, and the simulator keeps them both honest. Every mid-market e-commerce and SaaS site is now being visited not just by people, but by AI agents — ChatGPT with browsing, Gemini, Claude, Operator-style computer-use agents, and increasingly, structured tool-callers built on protocols like WebMCP . They search, they compare, they click "Add to Cart," they check out. Sometimes on behalf of a real human sitting one layer removed; sometimes fully autonomously. \n\n Nobody asked these sites whether that was okay, and almost nobody's analytics stack can tell an agent-driven session apart from a human one. Conversion funnels are quietly getting corrupted. Attribution is breaking. And there is no identity layer, no permission model, and no audit trail for any of it — the equivalent of letting an entirely new class of user into your storefront with no ID check and no receipt. \n\n That gap — agents are already transacting on the web, and nobody is governing it — is what started Agentronics. WebMCP's arrival ( navigator.modelContext , a native browser API letting a page register real tools an agent can call instead of scraping the DOM) was the moment the thesis stopped being speculative. It's not "agents might browse your site someday." It's "here's a W3C draft spec letting them do it natively, today, in Chrome." Someone has to sit in the middle and measure, govern, and eventually settle that traffic — the DoubleVerify/IAS role, but for the agentic web instead of the ad-tech web. \n\n What We Built \n\n To make the problem tangible instead of theoretical, we built a small but complete demonstration: a 4-page Amazon-style shopping flow (Home → Search → Cart → Checkout) where every user action is also exposed as a native WebMCP tool . searchProducts , addToCart , updateCartItemQuantity , proceedToCheckout , placeOrder — a dozen tools in total, registered directly against navigator.modelContext.registerTool() , no DOM-scraping polyfills, no screenshot loops. \n\n Point an agent (we tested against Gemini CLI, connected through the Model Context Tool Inspector Chrome extension) at the site and give it a single instruction — "search for headphones, add the wireless noise-cancelling ones with quantity 2, go to cart, checkout, place the order" — and it completes the entire flow through structured tool calls instead of guessing at pixel coordinates. That's the world Agentronics is built to sit inside of: a web where agents transact through declared, inspectable tools, and where every one of those calls is an event someone can measure, authorize, and eventually reconcile. \n\n How We Built It \n\n \n The demo storefront : React 19 + Vite, Tailwind for styling, React Router for the four-page SPA flow, Zustand for cart state shared across pages. No backend — a hardcoded 12-SKU catalog across four categories, so the whole flow runs entirely client-side. \n The WebMCP layer : a small useWebMCPTool hook wraps navigator.modelContext.registerTool() with mandatory feature detection ( "modelContext" in navigator ) and AbortSignal -based cleanup, so tools register on mount and cleanly unregister on unmount as the SPA routes between pages. Read-only tools ( searchProducts , viewCart , getProductDetails ) are marked readOnlyHint: true so an agent can call them freely; mutating tools ( addToCart , placeOrder ) route through client.requestUserInteraction() for confirmation before anything irreversible happens. \n A visible trust surface : a small floating badge shows "WebMCP Active — N tools registered," with every registration and execution logged to console — because a governance thesis is only credible if you can actually see what an agent is doing on your page, not just trust that it's well-behaved. \n The platform underneath : the @agentronics/sdk (published on npm) is what would sit behind a real deployment of this — agent detection across three ingestion paths (WebMCP-native, Web Bot Auth–signed agents like Operator and AWS AgentCore, and stealth Chromium-based agents caught via behavioral fingerprinting), feeding a GCP-hosted intelligence platform (Cloud Run, a GCS Parquet lake, Neon Postgres with pgvector, Vertex AI Gemini for natural-language insight generation). \n Design system : a small, deliberate palette — near-black, periwinkle indigo, warm amber — built as reusable CSS/Tailwind v4 tokens with a proper light/dark flip (not a simple inversion; the accent colors are re-tuned so they glow instead of vibrate on a dark canvas). \n \n\n Challenges We Ran Into \n\n \n WebMCP is brand new and under-documented. It's a February 2026 W3C draft with almost no production examples to learn from beyond the spec text and a handful of Google demo repos. We had to work directly from the IDL rather than from tutorials, which meant a lot of double-checking against the spec to avoid hallucinating APIs that don't exist yet ( ModelContextClient.requestUserInteraction , the exact shape of a tool's content response array, the fact that tools are scoped to page lifetime and vanish on navigation). \n SPA navigation vs. tool lifecycle. Because this is a client-side-routed React app, "navigating" between pages never actually reloads the document — so we had to be deliberate about which tools live at the Layout level (global, persistent: getCartCount , navigateToPage ) versus which are page-scoped and need to cleanly register/unregister via AbortSignal as components mount and unmount. Getting that boundary wrong either leaks stale tools or drops live ones mid-session. \n Confirmation without friction. Mutating actions like addToCart and placeOrder need a human-in-the-loop checkpoint, but over-gating every tool call makes the agent flow no faster than a person clicking through manually. Deciding which tools get readOnlyHint: true and which route through requestUserInteraction() was less a technical problem than a governance-design one — which is, not coincidentally, the exact judgment call Agentronics exists to help other sites make at scale. \n Making an invisible problem visible. The hardest part wasn't the code — it was building something that makes a genuinely abstract thesis ("your analytics are already being corrupted by agent traffic you can't see") into something a judge, or a Head of Growth at a DTC brand, can watch happen in front of them in thirty seconds. \n \n\n What We Learned \n\n Building the demo end-to-end sharpened the thesis rather than just illustrating it. Watching an agent complete a full purchase flow through declared tools — cleanly, quickly, with zero DOM guesswork — made the underlying problem concrete: this is not a hypothetical future traffic class. It is a fast, cheap, and increasingly common way for both real customers and their agents to transact, and right now it looks identical to a human session in every standard analytics tool. The measurement gap isn't coming — it's already open. That's the wedge Agentronics leads with, and building this demo is the clearest proof of it we've been able to put in front of anyone. \n\n \n\n \n Built With \n\n codex gcp npm pnpm python typescript \n \n\n \n Try it out \n\n \n \n \n www.agentronics.dev \n \n \n \n GitHub Repo \n \n \n \n www.npmjs.com \n \n \n \n\n \n\n \n\n \n \n Submitted to\n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n \n The WebMCP Challenge \n \n\n\n \n\n\n \n\n \n \n\n \n \n Created by\n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n Neelakandan NC \n \n \n\n \n\n \n \n \n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n\n \n\n\n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n Like\n \n \n\n\n \n \n \n\n \n \n\n \n\n\n \n \n \n \n Share this project:\n \n\n\n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n\n \n \n \n

A normal chat can suggest an answer, but the reasoning often disappears into prose and the page on screen drifts out of sync. \n\n BuildBrief Studio makes WebMCP the product interface between those two modes. The visual worksheet and six imperative Website Tools share one validated configuration. An agent can discover catalog options, compare two or three candidates, apply a partial recommendation, read the result back, review constraints, and create a preliminary brief. Write tools update the visible UI before returning and leave exact field-level receipts. If the person changes a value afterward, the revision advances and the old brief is visibly marked stale. \n\n The demo is deliberately safe and easy to judge: no login, no personal information, no external API, and no production system. Every dollar value comes from a bundled synthetic catalog and a deterministic formula. Strict schemas and runtime validation reject extra fields and incompatible choices. Read-only annotations match behavior. Permit, access, anchoring, snow-load, and site conditions remain explicit human work rather than being hand-waved by automation. \n\n The result is a small but complete example of what WebMCP enables: not an agent operating beside a website, but a person and agent collaborating through one observable decision surface. \n\n How WebMCP is used \n\n \n search_catalog discovers relevant structures from natural-language needs. \n get_build_state grounds the agent in the person’s latest visible choices. \n compare_builds evaluates two or three validated alternatives in one call. \n apply_build performs a partial visible update and returns exact before/after changes. \n check_constraints separates catalog fit from human verification. \n create_build_brief renders a deterministic handoff artifact on the page. \n \n\n All tools are registered imperatively from the top-level page through document.modelContext.registerTool . They use strict JSON Schema, truthful read/write annotations, concise structured output, and an AbortSignal lifecycle. \n\n Quick judge path \n\n Open the live demo in ChatGPT’s in-app browser, wait for “6 Website Tools ready,” and send the demo prompt shown on the page. Verify that the agent searches, compares against the $15,000 illustrative budget, applies an enclosed 20 × 24 workshop with a roll-up door, surfaces human checks, and creates the visible project brief.
