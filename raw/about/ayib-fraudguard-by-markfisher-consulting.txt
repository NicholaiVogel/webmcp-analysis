But a legitimate-looking transaction can still be fraudulent when a user has been manipulated through social engineering. \n\n A person may receive a message from someone impersonating a bank, business, friend, or payment provider and voluntarily initiate what appears to be a completely normal transaction. \n\n That led us to a simple question: \n\n What if fraud detection could understand the context around a transaction, not just the transaction itself? \n\n That question inspired Ayib FraudGuard by Markfisher Consulting, an AI-powered fraud intelligence and social-engineering protection platform designed to help identify suspicious activity before it becomes a financial loss. \n\n Our vision is simple: Ayib provides the payment infrastructure, while Ayib FraudGuard provides the security intelligence protecting it. \n\n What it does \n\n Ayib FraudGuard analyzes transaction and contextual signals to identify suspicious behavior and generate a risk assessment. \n\n It is designed to help fintechs, banks, payment platforms, marketplaces, and developers detect potential fraud through an API-driven security layer. \n\n Key capabilities include: \n\n AI-assisted anomaly detection\nReal-time transaction risk analysis\nBehavioral pattern analysis\nSocial-engineering risk detection\nRisk scoring\nAPI-based fraud intelligence\nSecurity-focused automation\nDeveloper-friendly integration \n\n Instead of simply asking whether a transaction looks unusual, FraudGuard aims to help answer: \n\n "Does the overall context suggest that this transaction may be fraudulent?" \n\n The resulting intelligence can be incorporated into an application's workflow to support decisions such as allowing, challenging, reviewing, or blocking a transaction. \n\n For the WebMCP Challenge, we are also exploring how fraud intelligence can become more accessible to web applications and AI-powered developer experiences through machine-consumable interfaces and workflows. \n\n How we built it \n\n We built Ayib FraudGuard as a cloud-native, API-first security platform. \n\n The architecture combines a transaction-processing layer with AI-assisted risk analysis and security controls. \n\n At a high level, the system: \n\n Receives transaction and contextual signals.\nProcesses and normalizes the available data.\nEvaluates behavioral and transactional patterns.\nUses AI-assisted analysis to identify anomalies and potential social-engineering indicators.\nProduces a risk assessment.\nMakes that intelligence available to the consuming application through an API. \n\n We designed the platform to be modular so that additional fraud signals, detection models, security services, and developer interfaces can be added without rebuilding the entire system. \n\n Our broader stack and experience includes TypeScript/Node.js, APIs, cloud infrastructure, AI/LLMs, CI/CD, observability, and security engineering. \n\n Challenges we ran into \n\n Ayib FraudGuard was not born from a hypothetical problem. It was born from our experience building and launching Ayib. \n\n When we first launched Ayib, we experienced multiple security breaches and incidents that exposed weaknesses in how we were protecting our payment infrastructure. These experiences made it clear that traditional security controls alone were not enough. We needed a security layer that could continuously analyze activity, identify unusual behavior, and help us detect potential threats before they became financial losses. \n\n That experience became one of the key inspirations behind Ayib FraudGuard. \n\n As we began building FraudGuard, one of our biggest challenges was determining how to combine AI-driven flexibility with the predictability and reliability required by financial systems. \n\n AI can identify patterns that traditional rules may miss, but we could not simply allow an AI model to make financial security decisions without appropriate controls and safeguards. We therefore designed FraudGuard so that AI works as part of a broader security architecture rather than acting as the sole decision-maker. \n\n We also had to solve several practical challenges: \n\n Detecting suspicious activity with low latency.\nBalancing false positives and false negatives.\nMaintaining consistent AI model behavior.\nEnsuring API reliability and availability.\nEstablishing appropriate security boundaries.\nDesigning for future scalability.\nProviding sufficient observability into security decisions.\nMaking risk assessments understandable and actionable.\nIntegrating fraud intelligence into existing payment workflows. \n\n Another major challenge was shifting our thinking from simply responding to security incidents to building infrastructure capable of anticipating and preventing them. \n\n Accomplishments that we're proud of \n\n Our biggest accomplishment is turning lessons from Ayib's early security incidents into a working AI-powered fraud intelligence platform. \n\n Instead of treating our security challenges as isolated incidents, we used them as an opportunity to build a reusable security layer that can potentially protect not only Ayib, but other fintechs, payment platforms, and digital businesses. \n\n We are particularly proud of: \n\n Turning real-world security incidents into a product opportunity.\nBuilding an AI-assisted fraud detection pipeline.\nDesigning FraudGuard as an API-first platform.\nCombining transaction data with behavioral and contextual risk signals.\nBuilding an architecture that can evolve as fraud techniques change.\nExploring how AI and emerging web technologies can make security intelligence more accessible to developers.\nCreating a foundation that can integrate with existing financial infrastructure. \n\n Most importantly, we are proud that FraudGuard represents a shift from reactive security to proactive fraud prevention. \n\n Our experience with Ayib taught us that discovering a security problem after it happens can be extremely costly. FraudGuard is designed around the idea that suspicious activity should be identified as early as possible. \n\n What we learned \n\n Building Ayib and subsequently Ayib FraudGuard taught us that security cannot be an afterthought, especially when building financial infrastructure. \n\n We initially approached security largely from the perspective of protecting the application and infrastructure. Our experiences after launching Ayib showed us that financial security requires a much broader approach that considers transactions, users, behavior, context, infrastructure, and continuously evolving attack patterns. \n\n We learned that fraud detection is not simply a machine-learning problem. \n\n It requires a combination of security engineering, data, infrastructure, AI, user experience, and developer experience. \n\n We also learned that accuracy alone is not enough. A fraud system needs to be fast enough for real-time transactions, reliable enough for production environments, and understandable enough for both humans and applications to act on its recommendations. \n\n Most importantly, we learned that context matters. \n\n A transaction can look completely legitimate when viewed on its own, but become highly suspicious when combined with behavioral, transactional, or contextual signals. \n\n That lesson from building and securing Ayib continues to shape the architecture of FraudGuard today. \n\n What's next for ayib fraudguard by markfisher consulting \n\n Our next goal is to evolve FraudGuard from a fraud detection API into a broader fraud intelligence infrastructure for digital financial services. \n\n We want to move beyond simply identifying suspicious transactions and build a platform capable of understanding relationships between users, transactions, devices, behaviors, and broader fraud patterns. \n\n We plan to expand into: \n\n Advanced behavioral and graph-based fraud detection.\nCross-platform fraud intelligence.\nDevice and identity risk signals.\nSocial-engineering detection.\nExplainable AI risk assessments.\nDeveloper SDKs and CLI tooling.\nReal-time fraud monitoring.\nAutomated security recommendations.\nContinuous model evaluation.\nIntegrations with fintechs, banks, payment platforms, and marketplaces. \n\n We also want to make FraudGuard increasingly accessible through APIs, SDKs, and AI-enabled interfaces so developers can incorporate fraud intelligence directly into their applications and workflows. \n\n Our long-term vision is to build the security intelligence layer for Africa's next generation of financial infrastructure, informed by the real-world security challenges we experienced while building Ayib. \n\n Ayib moves the money. Ayib FraudGuard helps protect it. \n\n \n\n \n Built With \n\n ai azure azuresynapse gpt llm node.js openai psql react typescript \n \n\n \n Try it out \n\n \n \n \n ayibfraudguardses.azurewebsites.net \n \n \n \n GitHub Repo \n \n \n \n\n \n\n \n\n \n \n Submitted to\n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n \n The WebMCP Challenge \n \n\n\n \n\n\n \n\n \n \n\n \n \n Created by\n \n\n \n \n \n \n Technical Founder \n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n Daniel Akinbade \n \n \n\n \n\n \n \n \n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n\n \n\n\n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n Like\n \n \n\n\n \n \n \n\n \n \n\n \n\n\n \n \n \n \n Share this project:\n \n\n\n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n\n \n \n \n \n\n \n \n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n\n\n\n \n \n \n Updates \n\n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n \n Daniel Akinbade \n posted an update\n \n —\n \n 2 days ago \n \n \n\n Great work team \n\n\n \n \n \n\n \n Log in \n or\n sign up for Devpost \n to join the conversation.\n \n\n \n\n \n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n \n Daniel Akinbade \n started this project\n \n —\n \n 2 days ago \n \n \n\n
