One page, two hands. The founder and the agent point at the same row. I run Hyperdrift, a small AI studio in the UK. Every month the government publishes the grant, the consultation or the tender that could change a founder's quarter. Every month I miss it, because it's buried under sixty documents I don't have time to read. So I built a radar. It reads gov.uk, UKRI and Parliament for me. Useful, but not enough: a list of what changed still needs someone who knows my company to say which lines matter. WebMCP was the missing link. Open the radar in a browser that has an agent, and the page hands the agent its controls. It fills my profile from what it already knows about me. It works the list on my screen: filters move, cards light up, every suggestion comes with a reason and a date. Then I tell it something the website will never hear: my NHS partner has gone and I've got six weeks. The list re-ranks in front of me. I drop one, I say why, and it reads my reason before it speaks again. The site keeps the data. My agent keeps my context. I keep the last word. A plug-in on a server can't do that. Try it: radar.hyperdrift.io/explore, in ChatGPT's browser or Chrome with WebMCP on. Three prompts wait at the top of the page. Under the hood: seven tools on document.modelContext, one of them registered only once a shortlist exists; every tool calls the same functions the buttons call.
