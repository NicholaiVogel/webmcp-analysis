What Threadline is Threadline is a provenance-preserving argument debugger for contested online claims. A browser agent reads the same investigation a person is reviewing and uses eight in-page WebMCP tools to stage sources, atomic claims, typed relations, non-destructive groups, and a bounded verdict. Agent-authored proposals are visibly noncanonical until a person accepts them. If a person rejects a proposal with a reason, that rationale returns through get_workspace so a later agent call can revise its approach. The result is not another feed summary. It is an inspectable collaboration in which the agent does the expensive work of researching and structuring an argument, while the person retains authority over what becomes durable truth. The problem Fast-moving technical discussion on X and the wider web flattens primary evidence, repetition, interpretation, prediction, and contradiction into one stream. A claim can look independently corroborated simply because many posts repeat the same underlying source. An autonomous summary hides those dependencies and can quietly become the next person's “fact.” Threadline makes the evidence lineage visible. It separates what a source directly establishes from what people infer, predict, amplify, or dispute. What people and agents do together The agent can: inspect bounded canonical workspace state and deterministic structural analysis; inspect full source evidence inside explicit untrusted-data boundaries; research and stage public sources it actually accessed; propose atomic claims and typed supports , contradicts , qualifies , or amplifies relations; propose non-destructive groups for repeated claims; propose one bounded verdict and its evidence relations as an atomic patch; and focus the visible graph on unresolved or primary-evidence work. The person can: inspect every source trail and staged graph entity; accept a defensible proposal; reject a weak proposal with a recorded rationale; decide which verdict becomes canonical; reopen saved investigations after reload; and export portable JSON or a Markdown evidence brief with the decision receipt. There is no registered accept, reject, reset, import, delete, rename, or case-switch tool. Acceptance is available only through the person-facing UI. This is a capability boundary, not authenticated proof of personhood. The agent and person share one live object. Stable source, claim, relation, group, proposal, and case IDs refer to the exact graph on screen. The agent does not reconstruct graph meaning from pixels. Optimistic concurrency prevents silent cross-talk. Every non-discovery call requires expectedCaseId ; every staging call also requires expectedRevision . A stale staging call or cross-case call returns structured recovery output before any proposal or canonical mutation. Rejection becomes protocol feedback. get_workspace exposes up to eight recent human decisions, including rationale, for later agent calls. Schemas and trust boundaries are explicit. Inputs are closed and bounded. Read tools carry read-only and untrusted-content hints, and full third-party evidence is returned only inside named untrusted-data delimiters. Staging is idempotent. Equivalent proposals reuse a stable ID, and pending entity IDs are reserved so competing proposals cannot present different content under one identity. Because the tools live in the page, Threadline needs no backend, database, account, API key, or bundled AI runtime. A complete product, not a proof of concept The primary interface is a responsive, keyboard-accessible provenance graph and review queue—not a chatbot. Pending graph entities are visibly marked STAGED — NOT CANONICAL and open their owning proposal. Amplification is first-class: repeated claims can be grouped without deleting original wording, relations, or sources. A verdict is an atomic patch. Its conclusion and evidence relations enter canonical state together, while only direct claim-to-verdict edges become its basis receipt. Up to eight investigations can be created, opened, renamed, reset, removed, imported, and exported. When exclusive Web Locks are available, cases labeled Saved survive reload. Otherwise they remain clearly labeled Memory only and exportable before reload. Screenshot transcriptions are explicitly labeled human-supplied evidence; Threadline never fabricates a public URL or treats an attributed statement as proof of its underlying claim. The deterministic preview is visibly labeled as a checked-in fixture replay, never as a live model response. Threadline uses dependency-free browser ES modules, semantic HTML, CSS, functional SVG, and Node's built-in test runner. It is hosted on ChatGPT Sites and released under the MIT License. The bundled challenge case The main investigation asks: Will WebMCP replace browser automation? Nine dated public records include Chrome documentation, the WebMCP explainer, a pinned WindTunnel benchmark result, and WebKit and Mozilla standards-position records. The graph separates a large demonstrated efficiency advantage on instrumented sites from task-level parity, explicit fallback guidance, and unresolved cross-browser implementation. The evidence supports a narrower verdict than the feed does: WebMCP is a semantic fast lane on instrumented sites, not a replacement for browser automation. The walkthrough deliberately stages one defensible group, one weak group, and one atomic verdict. The person accepts the first, rejects “Adoption and fallback” with the rationale “Same topic, different claim,” inspects the verdict evidence, and accepts the verdict. The unresolved standards claim remains visible after resolution.
