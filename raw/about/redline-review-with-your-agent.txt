Redline is shared contract review for you and ChatGPT — WebMCP tools propose edits and flag risks as live hunks on the doc; you accept or reject so propose never silently becomes commit.\n \n\n \n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n \n Like\n \n \n\n \n\n \n \n Comment\n \n\n \n \n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n Story \n \n \n \n \n Updates \n \n \n \n \n \n \n\n\n \n \n\n\n \n \n \n \n\n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n\n\n \n\n\n\n \n Inspiration \n\n Contract review is where AI chat fails hardest: the model pastes a wall of suggested language into the sidebar while you’re staring at a different paragraph. The risk isn’t “bad writing” — it’s silent apply . \n\n WebMCP lets the page expose review actions on the live document. Redline was built so ChatGPT can propose edits and flag risks as hunks you can see, while accept/reject stays a human gate. Propose ≠ commit. \n\n What it does \n\n Redline is agent-native contract review: \n\n \n Open a seeded SaaS MSA with numbered clauses \n Find clauses, propose replace/insert/delete hunks with rationale \n Flag risks and leave comments on the same doc \n Accept or reject hunks in the UI (applies to visible text) \n Summarize risks and export a redline summary \n Full human review UI without WebMCP; 17 site tools when ChatGPT is present \n ?demo=1 happy path for judges and screencasts \n \n\n How we built it \n\n \n Vite + React + TypeScript ( base: '/redline-webmcp/' ) \n Zustand store shared by the document UI and every WebMCP execute \n Feature-detect document.modelContext → navigator.modelContext (top-level) \n readOnly tools for inspection; accept_hunk / reject_hunk / clear_proposals / export_redline as side effects \n MIT, GitHub Pages — Built with Codex \n \n\n Challenges we ran into \n\n Modeling pending hunks vs applied text without corrupting clause anchors; making the safety story obvious in under three minutes; keeping the UI “legal product” rather than another kanban. \n\n Accomplishments that we're proud of \n\n A fourth WebMCP submission that makes the browser’s confirmation model the product: agent proposes on-canvas, human commits. Orthogonal to Nest, Frame, and Brief. \n\n What we learned \n\n The best WebMCP demos aren’t “agent can click.” They’re workflows where shared visual state + gated writes are the point. Redline is that pattern for documents. \n\n What's next for Redline — Review with your agent \n\n Real PDF/DOCX import, multi-party review, clause libraries, and deeper ChatGPT desktop testing with confirmation UX. \n\n \n\n \n Built With \n\n codex \n \n\n \n Try it out \n\n \n \n \n omercelik.github.io \n \n \n \n\n \n\n \n\n \n \n Submitted to\n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n \n The WebMCP Challenge \n \n\n\n \n\n\n \n\n \n \n\n \n \n Created by\n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n Omer Celik \n \n \n\n \n\n \n \n \n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n Serdar Murat ÇELİK \n \n \n\n \n\n \n \n \n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n Abdulhamit Mabocoglu \n \n \n\n \n\n \n \n \n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n\n \n\n\n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n Like\n \n \n\n\n \n \n \n\n \n \n\n \n\n\n \n \n \n \n Share this project:\n \n\n\n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n\n \n \n \n \n\n \n \n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n\n\n\n \n \n \n Updates \n\n \n \n \n \n \n\n \n \n \n \n \n \n Omer Celik \n started this project\n \n —\n \n 3 days ago \n \n \n\n
