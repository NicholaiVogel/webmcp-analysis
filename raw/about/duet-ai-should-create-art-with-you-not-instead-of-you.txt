An editable canvas where artists and AI illustrate and animate together. Through WebMCP, agents transform selected regions into editable layers; or motion ideas into shapes, keyframes, and cels. Like Comment Story Updates Accomplishments that we’re proud of DUET feels genuinely fun to draw with. AI can help artists take their work further while the result remains recognizably theirs -- not a finished image generated on their behalf. Inspiration Imagine trying to create an illustration by describing the final image entirely with words. The AI generates something that is almost right; but not what you pictured. It may also look unmistakably AI-generated. Drawing the idea is better. Yet the usual workflow involves downloading an image from chat, importing it into an editor, making a change, exporting it, uploading it again, switching apps, and repeating. Truly seamless. Zero friction. Definitely not annoying at all. DUET was built to replace that loop with direct collaboration on the canvas. AI should create art with you , not instead of you. Through WebMCP, artists can bring the AI agent they already use into their creative workspace. The agent sees only the parts of the artwork the artist chooses to share, edits only the selected area, and returns results that remain editable inside the project. It is still your drawing. AI simply becomes another creative tool in the workbench. What it does DUET combines two connected creative workspaces: Illustrator: collaborate with an agent using selected regions, contextual references, and editable layers. Animator: turn an agent’s motion ideas into editable shapes, keyframes, cels, and timeline tracks. Illustrator workbench The Illustrator workbench provides the tools expected from a lightweight art application: Brushes and erasing Colour controls Layers Selections and transforms Image import Undo and redo Editable project export Through WebMCP, an artist can select a region of their artwork and ask an agent to edit it. The artist can also share additional regions as visual context, helping the agent understand the intended style, palette, lighting, or composition. The agent’s result is returned as an editable layer clipped to the original selection, not pasted destructively over the artwork. DUET also supports critique and creative direction. Artists can ask questions such as: “What colour would work better here?” “How could I improve this composition?” “Why do the proportions of this apple feel strange?” “What could I draw to make this area feel more magical without changing the character?” Animator workbench The Animator workbench applies the same collaborative philosophy to motion. Instead of generating a finished video and pasting it into the project, an agent can describe an animation using: Coloured vector-like shapes Motion paths Position, scale, rotation, and opacity keyframes Timing and easing DUET converts that structured animation recipe into editable cels on a new timeline track. The artist can then inspect the result, adjust the timing, redraw individual frames, or continue building on top of it. AI contributes motion ideas. The artist keeps control of the animation. How we built it DUET is a browser-based illustration and animation workspace that uses WebMCP as the bridge between the canvas and an AI agent . Illustration workflow The artist selects the region they want the agent to edit. They may select additional regions to provide visual context. Through WebMCP, the agent receives only the content the artist chose to share. The agent returns an edited image. DUET inserts the result as a new editable layer clipped to the original selection. This gives the artist control over what the agent can see, what it can change, and what ultimately remains in the project . Animation workflow For animation, I explored a structured alternative to generated-video paste-ins. Through WebMCP, the agent provides a constrained animation recipe containing simple coloured shapes and keyframes for position, scale, rotation, opacity, and easing. DUET interprets those instructions, renders them into ordinary animation cels, and adds the cels to an editable timeline track. This allows an agent to contribute animation through the application’s existing creative primitives rather than returning a flattened, uneditable result. Challenges we ran into The biggest challenge was deciding what to build within the limited time. DUET could expand in many directions: more drawing tools, richer animation controls, more agent tools for animation. But the central challenge is still that DUET needed to let the artist maintain full and deliberate control over the artistic direction and expression. Without that it's no different than letting the agent do it all for you. That principle shaped both the illustration workflow and the structured animation system. What we learned WebMCP enables a collaborative work flow between your agent and the artist. For DUET, the most useful agent outputs were not necessarily finished images or videos. They were editable intermediate pieces , layers, shapes, keyframes, cels, critiques, and suggestions. Things that the artist could inspect and transform. This makes AI less like a replacement for the creative process and more like a participant within it. What’s next for DUET Expand the illustration toolset toward the controls artists expect from professional creative software. Add more animation tools and reusable motion components. Explore more WebMCP-powered AI-animation applications beyond generating a complete video from a single prompt. Let agents assist with tasks such as in-betweening, timing adjustments, motion studies, composition feedback, and local style-consistent edits. Continue developing ways for AI to contribute meaningfully while keeping every result editable and under the artist’s control.
