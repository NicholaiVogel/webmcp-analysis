Vistect helps blind people understand and create visual documents with images, charts, and diagrams through structured, verifiable collaboration with their AI agent using WebMCP Like Comment Story Updates Vistect Independent Visual Document Understanding and Creation for Blind Professionals Inspiration Vistect began with a seven-hour wait. Arif Hosen is a Project Coordinator at Songjog Foundation, a nonprofit organization, and a member of our team. He is visually impaired and independently handles complex professional responsibilities, including coordinating projects, preparing communications, organizing information, and producing reports. During one project, Arif needed to create a press release and modify a project report card. He could write the content, organize the information, review the wording, and determine what the documents needed to communicate. The barrier appeared when he needed to add and modify visual elements, understand their placement, and verify the final layout. The document tools available to him did not provide sufficient nonvisual access to those visual operations. Arif had to wait for a sighted coworker to become available. He waited seven hours. The task itself did not require seven hours. The delay existed because the final visual layer of the work depended on access to someone else's sight. Arif was fully capable of producing the communication. The limitation was not his knowledge, judgment, creativity, or professional ability. The limitation was an interface that represented images, diagrams, charts, and layouts in a way he could not independently understand or control. That experience led us to ask: Why should a capable professional lose hours of productivity, privacy, and creative control simply because document software makes its visual layer inaccessible? That question became Vistect . What Is Vistect? Vistect is a WebMCP-native visual document workspace where blind and visually impaired people can understand existing visual documents and independently create, modify, inspect, and approve professional multipage documents. It combines two connected capabilities. Understand A user can open a visual document and explore: Pages and sections Headings and reading order Images and their purpose Charts and their underlying values Diagrams, nodes, connections, and paths Tables, icons, captions, and callouts Spatial relationships between visual elements Visual hierarchy and page composition Accessibility and layout problems Uncertain AI interpretations Create A user can create or modify a multipage document containing: Written content Uploaded images Contextual alternative text Icons Data charts AI-assisted diagrams Captions and source notes Structured page layouts Accessible reading order The user can then inspect the result using the same understanding system. This creates a continuous workflow: Understand → Create → Inspect → Validate → Correct → Approve → Export Vistect does not simply generate a document and ask the user to trust it. It gives the user structured access to what was created, how it was arranged, which decisions the agent made, which findings are uncertain, and what remains to be approved. The Independence Vistect Creates Before Vistect Arif could independently: Write a press release Organize its message Edit report content Review the text Determine what the document needed to communicate Make professional and editorial decisions However, he could still require sighted assistance to: Add or replace an image Understand where visual elements were positioned Select an appropriate photograph Modify a diagram Choose and inspect a chart Check spacing and alignment Detect overlapping or truncated content Verify that the exported document looked correct With Vistect A blind or visually impaired professional can: Understand the semantic and spatial structure of a multipage document Navigate by page, heading, image, chart, diagram, table, warning, or decision Inspect every important visual element Upload and compare image candidates Select images based on purpose, composition, tone, and relevance Crop and position images using semantic instructions Create and modify structured diagrams Trace paths and detect missing diagram connections Import data and create accessible charts Verify that a chart accurately represents its source values Detect measurable layout and accessibility problems Review changes made by an agent Approve or reject individual visual decisions Export the exact document version that was reviewed The transformation is not merely that document creation becomes faster. The transformation is that a professional task previously blocked until a sighted person became available can now be completed with much greater independence. Built With Lived Experience Arif is not merely the inspiration for Vistect. He is a member of the team whose workplace experience helped define the product. His experience changed our understanding of the problem. At first, it was easy to assume that the main challenge was generating visual elements. However, generation alone does not create independence. An AI can generate an image, chart, diagram, or document layout. But a blind user must still be able to answer: What exactly did the AI create? Why was this image selected? Does the image support my intended message? What is uncertain about its description? How are the objects arranged? Is every diagram node connected? Does the chart honestly communicate the data? Is any text overlapping or cut off? What did the agent change? Which visual decisions have I not approved? Does the exported version match what I reviewed? Vistect is designed around those questions. The goal is not to let AI make visual decisions for Arif. The goal is to provide enough structured understanding, operational control, validation, and transparency for Arif to make those decisions himself. “I was able to write the press release and work on the report card content myself. But when I needed to add and modify visual elements, I had to wait for a sighted coworker to help me. I do not need someone else to create the document for me. I need a tool that lets me understand and control its visual elements myself.” — Arif Hosen, Project Coordinator at Songjog Foundation and Vistect team member How Vistect Works Vistect represents a document as a structured semantic model rather than treating it as a flat visual canvas. The model contains: Pages Sections Text blocks Images Icons Charts Diagrams Tables Captions Spatial relationships Reading order Sources Accessibility descriptions Validation findings Visual decisions Approval states Document versions This allows Vistect to describe and modify the document through meaningful operations. Instead of requiring the user to manipulate coordinates or inaccessible drag handles, Vistect supports instructions such as: “Place the image below the introduction.” “Make the chart the primary visual on this page.” “Keep the caption with the image.” “Put the diagram on a separate page.” “Align these three icons.” “Connect the unsuccessful outcome back to training.” “Show every visual decision I have not approved.” The application translates these semantic instructions into visual operations. Visual Document Understanding When a user imports a multipage document, Vistect creates both a semantic and spatial representation. A semantic page description might say: Page 4: Employment Outcomes 1. Section heading 2. Introductory paragraph 3. Horizontal bar chart with four categories 4. Chart caption 5. Source note A spatial description of the same page might say: The heading appears at the upper left. The introductory paragraph occupies the upper third of the page. The chart is the dominant visual element and fills most of the center and lower page. The source note appears directly below the chart in smaller text. This helps the user understand both the content and its visual presentation. Vistect also communicates uncertainty rather than presenting every AI-generated interpretation as fact. For example: High-confidence observation: Three adults are seated around a table. Interpretation: The setting appears professional and collaborative. Uncertain observation: One analysis detected a mobility aid near the left edge. Another analysis classified the object as office furniture. Recommendation: Do not include that detail in the final alternative text without additional verification. Image Selection and Placement Vistect helps users upload, inspect, compare, select, crop, and place images. Instead of returning a single image description, Vistect evaluates candidates according to: Relevance to the document's message Composition Emotional tone Professional quality Representation Potential stereotypes Crop flexibility Text-placement compatibility Distracting details Resolution Source and licensing information Confidence and uncertainty For example, if the document is meant to communicate independence, Vistect can explain that one image presents a participant working independently, while another may unintentionally suggest rescue or charity. The user makes the final selection. Image placement also uses semantic instructions. A user can ask Vistect to: Keep the main subject visible Preserve all detected faces Leave space for a title Remove a distracting object near the edge Create a square crop Use the image as a full-width header Place the image after a participant story The proposed crop and placement remain reviewable before approval. AI-Assisted Diagram Creation The user can describe a process in natural language, and their agent can create a structured diagram using Vistect's WebMCP tools. For example: Create a participant journey showing enrollment, assessment, training, internship, employment, and follow-up. If a participant needs additional training after assessment or internship, show a return path. Vistect represents the result as a graph containing nodes, connections, groups, entry points, decision outcomes, and terminal points. The user can inspect: The complete node list Incoming and outgoing connections Primary and alternative paths Decision outcomes Cycles Unreachable nodes Missing connections Visual arrangement Connector crossings Label collisions The graph engine performs deterministic checks rather than relying only on an AI statement that the diagram “looks correct.” The user can then direct corrections and export the diagram as structured SVG, a visual image, and an accessible long description. Accessible Chart Creation Vistect allows users to import a dataset and create a chart without surrendering control over how the data is presented. The system evaluates: The type of data Whether the data represents categories or time The number of series Label lengths The user's intended message Accessibility The possibility of misleading interpretation For example, Vistect can explain that a line chart may incorrectly imply a continuous time trend when the data represents separate outcome categories. Each chart includes: A visual chart An accessible data table A narrative interpretation Source information Chart integrity findings Vistect checks that: Visual values match the source data Labels and units are present Category labels are not truncated Legends match the series Color is not the only method of distinction The chart narrative does not contradict the data Visual Decisions and Final Authority Every important visual choice can be recorded in the Visual Decision Ledger. This includes: Image selection Image crop Image placement Icon choice Chart type Diagram structure Diagram layout Page template Visual priority Reading order Alternative text Long description A decision record can show: What was proposed Which alternatives were considered Why an option was recommended Which tool or agent created it Whether the user approved it Whether later changes made the approval stale Before export, the user can ask: “Show every visual decision I have not approved.” Vistect presents those decisions as a structured review queue. The document can only be finalized after required decisions and blocking validation issues are resolved. Why We Used WebMCP WebMCP is central to Vistect because visual document applications contain complex state that an agent should not have to infer from screenshots or inaccessible canvas controls. Vistect knows: Which pages and visual objects exist How they are positioned Their reading order The underlying chart data The structure of diagrams The source of images Current validation findings Which decisions are approved Which document version is active The user's browser agent understands: The user's current instructions The intended audience The desired tone The conversation context Previously expressed constraints Which unresolved issue the user wants to address WebMCP connects these capabilities through narrowly scoped, structured tools. Examples include: get_document_overview describe_page_layout inspect_image compare_image_candidates propose_image_crop place_image_relative_to create_diagram connect_diagram_nodes trace_diagram_path validate_diagram_structure import_chart_data recommend_chart_types validate_chart_integrity run_layout_checks run_accessibility_checks find_unapproved_visual_decisions preview_export_manifest lock_document_version finalize_locked_export These are not generic “make it look good” commands. Each tool performs a precise operation and returns structured state that the user can inspect. WebMCP allows the agent to collaborate with the user inside the document without guessing how to operate a visual editor. The agent can propose and execute changes, while Vistect enforces validation, versioning, approval, and finalization rules. Human and Agent Collaboration Vistect divides responsibility deliberately. Vistect provides The semantic document model Page and object geometry Visual element metadata Diagram graph structure Chart data Deterministic validation Reading order Version and approval state Export rendering The agent provides Natural-language interaction User-context interpretation Tool selection Alternative generation Explanations and recommendations Coordination across document operations The user provides Purpose Message Professional judgment Representation choices Corrections Approval Final authority This distinction is important. Vistect is not designed to automate the user out of the creative process. It is designed to make the visual process accessible enough that the user can remain in control. Privacy and Security Professional documents may contain confidential project data, personal images, financial information, participant stories, or unpublished organizational material. Vistect follows a local-first approach wherever practical. Operations such as document state management, chart generation, diagram structure, layout validation, reading-order checks, version comparison, and export preparation can be performed locally. If remote AI analysis is required for an image or difficult document region, Vistect is designed to disclose: Which asset will be transmitted Why remote processing is necessary Whether text or people are detected What information will accompany the request Whether the user wants to continue What will be retained Imported documents and images are treated as untrusted content. Their text cannot redefine Vistect's tools or bypass approval requirements. Consequential actions such as locking or exporting a document are tied to an exact document version. If the document changes after approval, the affected approval becomes stale and the user must review the change again. Accessibility Vistect is designed to work with existing assistive technology rather than replace it. The application uses: Semantic HTML Keyboard-operable controls Heading and landmark navigation Predictable focus behavior Screen-reader announcements for agent actions Accessible validation messages Non-color status indicators Structured object navigation No drag-only interactions Scalable text and high-contrast support After an agent action, Vistect can announce: Agent action completed. Image moved below the Executive Summary heading. Page 2 now has one unapproved visual decision. The user can navigate the document by page, heading, image, chart, diagram, table, warning, or approval state. What We Built During the challenge, we built the foundation of a WebMCP-native visual document workspace, including: A semantic multipage document model Visual document understanding Page and object navigation Structured image inspection Visual element placement AI-assisted diagram generation Diagram node and connection management Chart creation from structured data Layout and accessibility validation Visual decision tracking WebMCP tools for document inspection and modification Human approval before final actions Multipage document export Our demonstration focuses on the workflow that inspired the project: creating and modifying a professional project document containing visual elements. Challenges We Faced Visual information is not only object recognition Identifying that an image contains a person, laptop, and desk is not enough. A professional needs to understand why the image is appropriate, how it is composed, what message it communicates, and what remains uncertain. We addressed this by separating: Measurable facts AI interpretations Uncertain observations Human decisions Generation is easier than verification An agent can generate a diagram quickly, but independent authorship requires the user to inspect its structure, find missing routes, and understand its visual arrangement. We represented diagrams as graphs and added structured validation. Visual canvases are coordinate-driven Traditional design applications depend heavily on dragging, resizing, alignment guides, and spatial manipulation. We introduced semantic relationships such as: place after align with group with make primary keep together move to next page Agent actions can reduce user control If the agent makes too many automatic decisions, the blind user becomes a passive recipient. We created approval states, decision records, visible agent actions, and version-bound finalization to preserve user authority. Accessibility must survive asynchronous changes Agent actions can modify the document without direct keyboard interaction. This creates focus-management and announcement challenges. We designed action summaries and review queues so users can understand changes without unexpected focus movement. The product scope is large Visual documents can contain countless formats and object types. We deliberately focused the initial product on structured multipage professional reports rather than attempting to recreate every document or design application. What We Learned The most important lesson was that accessibility is not the same as automation. An AI that completes a visual task for a blind person may save time, but it does not necessarily create independence. Independence requires: Understanding Meaningful alternatives Transparent uncertainty Operational control Error detection Reversibility Approval Final authority We also learned that blind users do not need every visual property translated into coordinates. They need visual relationships translated into useful concepts that support real decisions. Most importantly, we learned that the final accessibility question is not: “Can the AI create the document?” It is: “Can the user understand, correct, approve, and confidently send it without asking a sighted person to check it?” Impact Vistect can support independence in: Employment Education Research Nonprofit work Entrepreneurship Communications Project management Reporting Policy development Client services We plan to measure success through outcomes such as: The percentage of visual document tasks completed without sighted assistance The number of visual decisions personally approved by the user Agent-generated errors independently detected Diagram and chart problems corrected Unapproved changes blocked User confidence in sending the final document Time previously lost waiting for visual assistance For Arif's original task, the baseline was a seven-hour wait. The long-term goal is not only to reduce that wait. It is to remove the dependency that created it. What's Next Our next steps include: Expanding testing with blind and low-vision professionals Improving multipage PDF understanding Strengthening image comparison and uncertainty reporting Supporting additional diagram types Adding more accessible chart formats Improving PDF and accessible HTML export Adding privacy receipts for remote visual analysis Expanding screen-reader compatibility testing Supporting reusable organizational brand systems Exploring DOCX and presentation export Developing tactile-ready diagram and chart outputs Publishing reusable WebMCP accessibility and approval patterns We also want to explore how Vistect can work alongside existing workplace productivity tools without requiring blind users to abandon the systems their organizations already use. Final Vision Vistect started because a capable professional had to wait seven hours for someone else to modify and verify the visual layer of his work. We believe blind and visually impaired people should not be limited to consuming descriptions of visual content or accepting whatever an AI generates. They should be able to direct visual communication, compare alternatives, understand structure, detect errors, make informed decisions, and retain final authority over what they publish. Vistect gives blind professionals independent control over the visual documents they understand, create, modify, and share.
