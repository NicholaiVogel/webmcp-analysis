\n\n When a flight is delayed, cancelled, or overbooked, most passengers don't know what to do. \n\n They may not know: \n\n \n Whether they can get a refund \n Whether they can receive compensation \n Whether the airline should provide meals or accommodation \n How to contact the airline \n How to write a proper complaint or claim \n \n\n Passenger rights are often written in complicated regulations and spread across different websites. \n\n FlightClaims makes this process simpler. \n\n A passenger enters their flight details, tells us what happened, and FlightClaims helps them understand their possible options and prepare a professional request to the airline. \n\n \n\n 🛠️ What It Does \n\n FlightClaims helps passengers in three simple steps : \n\n 1️⃣ Enter Your Flight Details \n\n Enter your: \n\n \n Airline \n Flight number \n Origin and destination \n Flight date \n What happened \n \n\n The user can select: \n\n \n ✈️ Flight delayed \n ❌ Flight cancelled \n 🚫 Denied boarding \n \n\n The app can also support uploading a boarding pass or ticket to extract flight details automatically. \n\n \n\n 2️⃣ Understand Your Possible Rights \n\n FlightClaims: \n\n \n Identifies the flight route \n Calculates the route distance \n Detects the relevant passenger-rights framework \n Analyzes the disruption details \n Shows possible passenger options \n Estimates potential compensation when enough information is available \n \n\n The app supports passenger-rights frameworks such as: \n\n \n 🇮🇳 India — DGCA \n 🇪🇺 Europe — EU261 \n 🇬🇧 United Kingdom — UK261 \n \n\n \n Note: Results are presented as guidance or estimates and are not guaranteed compensation. \n \n\n \n\n 3️⃣ Prepare Your Airline Request \n\n FlightClaims generates a professional request based on the passenger's case. \n\n The passenger can: \n\n \n Review the request \n Edit the request \n Approve the request \n \n\n Then they can copy and send it through the appropriate airline channel. \n\n \n\n 🤖 How WebMCP Is Used \n\n FlightClaims demonstrates how WebMCP allows AI agents to interact with a live web application . \n\n Normally, an AI assistant may need to guess what information is currently available on a website. \n\n With WebMCP, FlightClaims exposes structured tools that allow an AI agent to safely interact with the real application. \n\n Example Tools \n\n query_disruption_rights \n\n Allows an AI agent to analyze the current flight case and its possible passenger options. \n\n prepare_passenger_request \n\n Allows an AI agent to prepare a passenger request using the flight details currently available in the application. \n\n The important part: The AI works with the application's actual live state , not fake or hardcoded data. \n\n \n\n 📐 How It Works \n\n FlightClaims uses airport coordinates to calculate the approximate distance between airports using the Haversine formula . \n\n This helps the application analyze routes where distance is relevant to the passenger-rights framework. \n\n Example \n\n Delhi → Mumbai \n Airport coordinates\n ↓\nDistance calculation\n ↓\nRoute analysis\n ↓\nApplicable passenger-rights framework\n
