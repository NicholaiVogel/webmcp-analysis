Inspiration Service businesses already have websites, but most of those sites expose pages and contact forms rather than the structured truth an AI agent needs to complete a real customer journey. Pricing, availability, exclusions, messages, approvals, and later change orders are scattered across interfaces. An agent can discover a business yet still over-promise, act on stale terms, or mistake a private draft for an accepted agreement. We built Velaire to test a more useful WebMCP pattern: can an ordinary service website become a trustworthy agreement room for a customer's agent, an owner's agent, and the two humans who retain authority? What it does Velaire is a WebMCP-native reference implementation for service businesses, demonstrated through a fictional HVAC company. A customer speaks naturally to ChatGPT; the live website publishes typed capabilities that let the agent check service fit, policies, business evidence, pricing, and safety before opening a structured service case. A separate owner ChatGPT finds that same case through the owner route. The agents are independent and never role-play both parties in one conversation. They communicate through one revisioned ServiceCase that remains authoritative for messages, offers, counters, bookings, receipts, and changed work. The owner agent may prepare an offer, but it stays private until the owner presses Send. The customer agent may compare versions and prepare a booking, but only the customer can confirm it. That confirmation freezes an immutable receipt. If work changes later, Velaire compares the proposed charge against the exact accepted snapshot instead of silently rewriting history. Demo journey The customer asks for same-day help with an AC blowing warm air. ChatGPT checks eligibility, pricing evidence, availability, credentials, and safety. It opens a case with a $180 ceiling and explicit no-surprise-fee constraints. A separate owner agent retrieves the live request and prepares a $195 offer. The owner visibly sends the offer. The customer counters at $175 with no travel or after-hours fee. The owner sends offer version two. The customer agent compares both versions field by field. The agent prepares the booking; the customer confirms it, creating an immutable $175 receipt. The owner proposes a +$145 capacitor change order. Velaire shows the added scope, $320 total, and time impact against the original promise. The customer approves the change, the owner acknowledges it, and both agents independently read revision 9 with no pending decision. No real payment is taken, and every person, address, credential, review, price, and transaction shown is synthetic demonstration data. The WebMCP tool surface Customer discovery and trust velaire_check_service_fit checks need, postcode, urgency, budget, safety, and service eligibility. velaire_get_business_evidence returns source cards for credentials, pricing, availability, cancellation, warranty, and reviews. velaire_get_market_price_context and velaire_compare_quote_context provide transparent pricing context without presenting synthetic estimates as live market truth. Service, policy, site-search, contact, and agent-help tools make the ordinary website understandable without scraping its buttons. Shared customer–owner workflow velaire_open_service_case creates the authoritative record. velaire_get_service_case returns its current revision, messages, offers, decisions, and valid next actions. velaire_submit_case_message adds a bounded question or counteroffer and rejects stale revisions. velaire_wait_for_owner_reply and velaire_wait_for_customer_reply represent waiting honestly, with timeout and cancellation recovery. velaire_set_service_location, velaire_plan_service_route, and velaire_get_case_visuals return map, route, timeline, and visual artifacts. Owner workflow velaire_list_service_cases and velaire_get_owner_case expose the owner queue and exact case state. velaire_stage_owner_reply, velaire_stage_service_offer, and velaire_stage_change_order keep drafts private until a visible human Send action. Agreement protection and audit velaire_compare_offer_versions compares price, timing, deposit, warranty, scope, exclusions, and expiry deterministically. velaire_prepare_booking stages one exact offer version; it cannot confirm it. velaire_get_booking_receipt returns the full accepted snapshot rather than a mutable offer reference. velaire_compare_change_order checks later work against the accepted promise. velaire_audit_invoice_against_receipt detects invoice drift. velaire_get_webmcp_health exposes tool-call success, latency, revisions, read/action distribution, and recovery states. How we built it Velaire uses React, strict TypeScript, and Vite, deployed as a static application on Vercel. It registers native imperative tools through document.modelContext.registerTool(). The human UI and every WebMCP handler dispatch through the same pure TypeScript command gateway and reducer, preventing agent actions from bypassing the rules shown to humans. The demo persists state in localStorage, synchronizes tabs with BroadcastChannel and browser events, uses optimistic revision checks for every write, and records an append-only audit trail. Tool inputs use strict JSON Schema and runtime validation. Route scoping keeps customer and owner capabilities separate. Safety and human authority Emergency phrases such as gas smell, smoke, sparks, fire, or carbon monoxide trigger a safety stop instead of an ordinary quote flow. Agent schemas exclude payment details, direct contact identifiers, and exact home addresses. Agents can inspect, draft, compare, and prepare; humans alone send owner commitments, confirm bookings, approve changed work, and send the final acknowledgment. Challenges The hardest problem was not tool registration. It was preserving meaning across two asynchronous sessions: distinguishing a draft from a sent offer, rejecting stale writes, keeping accepted terms immutable, and treating “awaiting owner” or “awaiting human” as valid workflow states rather than fake success or failure. A second challenge was making the WebMCP layer invisible to ordinary customers. Velaire remains a polished service website; the agent uses the tool surface while the person sees familiar evidence, offer, comparison, booking, map, receipt, and decision interfaces. What we learned WebMCP becomes most valuable when tools expose domain decisions rather than page mechanics. “Click submit” is weaker than “prepare this exact offer version and show the human what will change.” Trust comes from typed outcomes, versioned promises, explicit non-effects, and visible authority boundaries—not simply from adding more actions. What's next The production path is server-side identity, durable cross-device storage, signed owner notifications, real calendar and payment providers behind the same prepare-before-commit gates, and adapters for clinics, agencies, repair companies, and other estimate- or appointment-driven businesses. Velaire shows that a service website can become agent-ready without surrendering control: evidence first, structured negotiation, explicit consent, immutable receipts, and a record both people and agents can trust.
